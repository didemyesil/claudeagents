## Section 1: General Provisions

The TER is established by the Executive Board and implemented by programme management and faculty; the Examination Board safeguards assessment/decision-making quality, fairness and integrity. The TER takes precedence over the Educational Handbook, module descriptions, the Assessment Policy, and Examination Board Rules and Guidelines in case of conflict.

**Article 1.1 Applicability:** applies to teaching, learning and examinations of the BSc Applied Computing; applies to learners enrolled for academic year **2026-2027**; Programme Management ensures implementation and communication to learners.

**Article 1.2 Definitions (selected):** academic year; assessor (faculty appointed by Examination Board); bachelor programme; cohort; competency-based; digital tools (LMS/Applied Computing Platform (ACP), Google Meet, Slack, Google Workspace); diploma; ECTS/EC; Educational Handbook; Examination Board; facilitator; final project; learner; learning lines (Professional Practice Line and Research Practice Line); LMS/ACP; module; module descriptions; programme management/Programme Manager; programme outcomes (Analyse, Advise, Design, Build, Manage and Control, Communicate and Collaborate); Research Practice Line (action inquiry: plan/act/observe/reflect, self-/one-to-one/team-inquiry); route (4-year or 3-year fast-track); supervisor; talent coaching; team coach; track (Applied Software Engineering, Applied Data Science, Applied Machine Learning); workshop.

## Section 2: Programme Structure

**2.1 Academic Year** — total study load 240 EC, ~40 hrs/week; two routes (4-year, 3-year fast-track) leading to the same outcomes/diploma; combines Professional Practice Line and Research Practice Line; final part includes the final project.

**2.2 Organisation** — competency-based bachelor aligned with HBO-i and NLQF level 6; shared six programme outcomes across tracks; delivered via blended learning (workshops, projects, coaching, supervision, digital learning, collaborative practice) using the Applied Computing Platform.

**2.3 Admission and Enrolment** — subject to applicable HBO entry requirements plus programme-specific requirements (Appendix 2); enrolment final only after conditions/registration/financial requirements met; intake/onboarding/orientation may be required; preparatory course offered for candidates lacking background knowledge, waivable via proficiency test + oral assessment.

**2.4 Programme Routes** — 4-year route and 3-year fast-track route, same degree/outcomes; fast-track designed for learners with a VWO diploma justifying acceleration; route switch requires Programme Management (and where relevant Examination Board) approval.

**2.5 Tracks** — Applied Software Engineering, Applied Data Science, Applied Machine Learning; each contributes to shared outcomes with a distinct professional profile; track change requires Examination Board approval via RPL process.

**2.6 Learning as Responsibility and Personal Accountability** — learners expected to participate in all scheduled activities (workshops, standups, coaching, supervision, presentations); must notify Programme Manager of absence in advance (or ASAP if unforeseen); must provide learning evidence for missed activities; time management expected, extension requests per Art. 3.2.1; active participation expected (camera/mic on if online, contributing, written reflection where required); inadequate participation/accountability can lead to exclusion from the programme and/or transfer to a future cohort, with a formal objection route to the Examination Board Chair.

**2.7 Talent Coaching** — supports academic/professional/career development and clarifying professional direction/identity.

**2.8 Certificate and Degree** — certificates may be issued for completed programme phases; bachelor's degree awarded on completion of all requirements, the final project, and demonstration of all learning outcomes; degree/title formally stated per national/institutional regulations.

**2.9 Termination of Studies and Re-registration** — governed by WHW Articles 7.42, 7.32, 7.33 where applicable; can occur at learner request, on completion, or for non-compliance; temporary withdrawal possible for prolonged illness/personal hardship via formal application to the Examination Board through the Programme Manager; returning learners follow the TER in effect at re-registration unless a transitional arrangement is approved.

## Section 3: Assessment of Learning

**3.1 Types of Assessment** — programmatic, competency-based; portfolio of diverse evidence (assignments, projects, reflections, presentations, workshop artefacts, technical deliverables, reports, code repositories, demonstrations, final project documentation); low-/medium-/high-stake assessment moments; full overview in module descriptions/Final Project Guide; deviations require Examination Board approval; standardised rubrics/grading grids made available; peer review and automated feedback (e.g. machine-supported code review) may be used as evidence but never solely determine the final formal result — final judgment remains with appointed assessors.

**3.2 Assessments and Deadlines** — assessors appointed by the Examination Board; deadlines communicated via module descriptions/learning environment; late submissions accepted only with an approved extension (Art. 3.2.1); missed deadlines without extension may be treated as not submitted, affecting ability to demonstrate outcomes; Final Project Guide sets specific final-project submission requirements.

**3.2.1 Extension** — requests for low-/medium-stake assessments go to the Programme Manager (delegated authority, may refer to Examination Board); high-stake/progression-affecting requests decided by the Examination Board; requests normally submitted ≥4 calendar days before deadline unless unforeseen; must state reason + supporting evidence; decision communicated in writing within a reasonable period; final-project-stage requests submitted in writing to the Programme Manager and referred to the Examination Board.

**3.3 Determination and Announcement of Results** — results/feedback generally communicated within 10 working days; exceptions communicated to the learner; medium-/high-stake feedback clarifies standard achieved and next steps; high-stake decisions (incl. final project) made by a qualified panel; final project result communicated after oral presentation and examiner deliberation; peer-review/automated-feedback purpose/criteria/weight communicated in advance; no final result is based solely on peer judgement or machine output without academic review.

**3.4 Progression Principles** — based on the total body of evidence relative to programme/learning outcomes; insufficient evidence may require revision/resubmission/repeat; high-stake decisions holistic, not based on a single assessment; every required programme outcome must be sufficiently demonstrated by programme end for diploma eligibility; final project oral-presentation-stage progression requires a preliminary pass per the Final Project Guide.

**3.5 Improvement, Resubmission, and Reconsideration** — revision/resubmission encouraged where permitted; resubmissions only for originally on-time work (unless extended); final project: formal improvement opportunity after initial internal review, up to 4 weeks for a revised report; internal examiners review the revision for preliminary pass/fail; disagreement between internal examiners triggers appointment of an external examiner as decider; oral presentation only proceeds after reaching a preliminary pass.

**3.6 Validity Period of Results** — results generally valid indefinitely unless the Examination Board determines knowledge/skills are demonstrably outdated (e.g. due to substantially changed professional/curriculum/legal standards), in which case additional requirements may be set.

**3.7 Discussing Feedback and Assessment Results** — learners may request discussion with the assessor to clarify results; discussion does not itself alter the outcome unless a procedural/substantive error is identified and corrected.

**3.8 Right of Appeal** — learners may inspect evaluated work; appeal possible where procedural irregularity, prejudice, bias, or material error is alleged (not solely because the result is lower than expected); appeals in writing to the Examination Board via the Programme Manager; supporting evidence may be requested for personal-circumstance appeals.

**3.9 Inclusive Assessment and Examination Provisions** — reasonable adjustments (extra time, adapted formats, assistive technology, etc.) may be requested for disability/chronic illness/neurodivergence/other relevant circumstances, via the Programme Manager and Examination Board; decisions communicated in writing with appeal route if denied.

**3.10 Certificates and Diploma** — certificates for completed phases where formally provided for; diploma issued on completion of all components, demonstration of all outcomes, successful final project (written/practical/oral), and procedural/financial requirements; diploma/supplement issued per institutional/national regulations; module names on transcripts must match officially approved cohort names (subject to approved transitional arrangements).

**3.11 Academic Integrity** — plagiarism, fraud and other misconduct prohibited, including unauthorised copying, submitting others' work as one's own, improper source use, falsification, and improper undisclosed use of AI/advanced technology tools; suspected misconduct reported to the Programme Manager; learner informed and given the chance to be heard; Examination Board makes the final decision and can invalidate results or impose further disciplinary action; learners remain fully responsible for originality/correctness/explainability/integrity of work even where machine-supported review tools are used.

**3.12 Exemptions through Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL)** — Examination Board may grant exemptions where prior learning sufficiently covers intended learning outcomes/requirements, on a substantiated request with evidence; exemptions alone do not guarantee a diploma — all remaining graduation requirements must still be met.

**3.13 Exemptions through Force Majeure** — illness, physical/psychological circumstances, pregnancy, family circumstances, or other serious situations demonstrably preventing participation may warrant an additional opportunity or other arrangement via the Examination Board through the Programme Manager, with supporting evidence where possible; the Board/Programme Management/Executive Board may postpone, reschedule, or invalidate-and-replace an assessment affected by force majeure.

## Section 4: Learner Support and Guidance

**4.1 Team Coaching, Squad Support, and Supervision** — structured continuous support via team coaching, squad-based learning support, and supervision; squads work together in projects/stand-ups/sprint cycles/retrospectives/peer feedback/team coaching; final-project-phase supervision appropriate to project scope, supporting both process and connecting work to outcomes/professional expectations; further guidelines in the Educational Handbook, module descriptions, and Final Project Guide.

**4.2 Programme Management** — Programme Manager coordinates day-to-day implementation and is the primary learner point of contact: communication/onboarding/engagement; coordinating information/announcements/events; first-line administrative guidance and referrals; communicating institutional academic decisions; ensuring learners know their rights/obligations/support mechanisms; supporting assessment/progression administration (extensions, appeals, special support); works closely with faculty, team coaches, supervisors, and the Examination Board.

## Section 5: Evaluation of the Programme

Systematic evaluation per the institute's QA approach, at activity, module, project/squad, and programme levels; results used to improve design/delivery/guidance/assessment/organisation via a continuous improvement cycle (plan–implement–review–improve); learners, faculty and, where relevant, external stakeholders may be involved.

## Section 6: Tasks and Responsibilities of the Examination Board

Objectively/professionally determines whether learners meet programme requirements per this TER and WHW Article 7.12; safeguards quality/fairness/independence of assessment, examination, progression, and final decisions; appoints assessors; establishes rules/guidelines/instructions; monitors compliance; grants exemptions; decides on individual deviation requests, appeals, fraud/plagiarism/misconduct cases (with appropriate measures), and high-stake decisions including final project/diploma decisions; reports periodically per institutional requirements.

## Section 7: Hardship Clause

Where demonstrable, predominant unreasonableness or unfairness arises in applying the TER, the Executive Board or Examination Board may deviate from one or more provisions, only where justified by the specific circumstances and without undermining programme/assessment/degree integrity; such a decision is reasoned and communicated in writing to the learner.
