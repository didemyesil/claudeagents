# INTRODUCTION

Examination Boards in general perform an important task: they are responsible for assuring the quality of exams and final examinations. Their purpose is to determine, in an independent and expert way, that each student has met the requirements to be awarded a degree set by the programme concerned. This means Examination Boards fulfil a vital role in monitoring the quality of the process leading to the awarding of qualifications. After all, students, stakeholders and supervisory bodies must have faith in the fact that any university awards its qualifications with all due care.

Thanks to the amendment of the Dutch Higher Education and Scientific Research Act (Wet op het hoger onderwijs en wetenschappelijk onderzoek, “WHW” in Dutch) through the Dutch Governance Strengthening Act (Wet versterking besturing, “Wvb” in Dutch), as of 1 September 2010, Examination Boards acquired more substantive tasks and their independent position has been strengthened.

At Team Academy, the Examination Board “is the institution which in an objective and professional manner determines whether a student complies with the conditions posed by the education- and examination regulations with regards to knowledge, insight and skills needed to obtain an (academic) degree.” (direct quote from article 7.12 lid 2 WHW). Its tasks are issuing certificates and diplomas, determining student standards, ensuring test quality, determination of guidelines, granting exemptions, ensuring quality of testing organisation, taking action in case of fraud, attending to requests, reporting and appointing assessors. To this end, the Examination Board meets on a monthly basis. The Examination Board stays in close contact with the executive, programme management and faculty throughout the programme to ensure an effective and efficient execution of its tasks. Formally, the Examination Board reports in writing to the Executive Board on a yearly basis by presenting both an Annual Report for the past and an Annual Plan for the upcoming academic year. In order to function optimally, it is essential that the governing body of the university guarantees that the Examination Board can operate independently and with due expertise, and it is important that (the members) of the Examination Board are well aware of the frameworks within which they must perform their statutory tasks.

The aim of this handbook is to inform Team Academy’s institutional and educational management and the Examination Board about the statutory frameworks within which the Examination Board should operate and the way in which they can properly perform their tasks in this context. These internal Examination Board Rules and Regulations also describe the procedures which are designed to ensure that both the Examination Board and other stakeholders within and outside Team Academy know which procedures are in existence, how they can be applied and which time frame surrounds and protects them.

Part 1 refers to the internal framework of rules and regulations that surrounds the functioning of the Examination Board itself while Section 2 focuses on the alignment of the Examination Board ’s procedures with the most current Team Academy TER (2025).

# Part 1: Internal rules and regulations for the functioning of the Examination Board

## 1A: General Provisions

Where in this document is referred to the Chair of the Examination Board, in his/her absence, s/he will be replaced by the Vice-Chair. In case both the Chair and Vice-Chair are in absentia, the Secretary of the Examination Board will substitute for them and take on their responsibilities and capacities.

## 1B: Installation and Independence of the Examination Board

With the adoption of the Wet Versterking Besturing, “independence and expertise” became the major features that any Exam Committee must demonstrate (WHW, Section 7.12.a. and the Explanatory Memorandum). This independence and expertise apply to the position of Exam Committees within an organization, the composition and appointment of members as well as the tasks and powers of Exam Committees.

The governing body of the university appoints or instals both the Examination Board as a committee and the individual members of the Examination Board , in consultation with the existing members. The Examination Board can be appointed for more than one programme, as long as the activities concern individual programmes. A TER is drawn up that applies to all these programmes, but with separate sections for where different rules apply for the different programmes. Within Team Academy the tasks are divided as follows:

The programme teams (curriculum team and programme management) are responsible for the structure and implementation of the programme as described in the TER, and they ensure that the teaching and the programme meet the quality standards.

The Examination Board advises the Programme Management and the Executive Board on the TER regarding exam regulations and, for each individual student, assesses the results of the education provided within the framework of the programme’s TER.

Due to its supervisory role, it is important that the Examination Board can perform its tasks and exercise its authority independently and with due expertise. The independent functioning of the Examination Board in respect of the Executive Board means that although the Executive Board installs the Examination Board , the university must ensure that the Examination Board can carry out its work independently within the university. This also means, for example, that the Executive Board cannot impose any obligations on the Examination Board regarding the assessment of students. The Executive Board does, however, have final responsibility for the quality of the education provided and the awarding of qualifications (WHW, Section 7.10a, paragraph 1). An Examination Board must act within the boundaries of the TER; this also guarantees that the way in which exams are conducted is in line with the programme framework.

In other words: the Executive Board approves the TER and thus has final responsibility for the quality of the education provided. The Examination Board assesses if students have met the requirements set down in the TER and, after this assessment, the university awards a Master’s Degree. The Examination Board assesses the realised quality against the (quality) requirements and provisions of the TER approved by the Executive Board. If an Examination Board observes that these requirements and provisions are not being met, it has several intervention instruments at its disposal.

In addition to independence in terms of its position in the organisation in respect of those responsible for the quality of the education provided (Executive Board, Programme Management), the Examination Board ’s independence should also be demonstrated by its composition. This means that (1) an Examination Board must always have at least one external member, and that (2) managers with financial responsibility cannot sit on an Examination Board .

With the introduction of the Wet versterking besturing, the emphasis in terms of the responsibility of the Examination Board is shifted to the substantive aspects of examinations. In the WHW, for example, assuring the quality of exams and final examinations is listed explicitly as a task (WHW, Section 7.12b, paragraph 1 under a). To this end, it is necessary that the members of Examination Board s have a high degree of subject-matter expertise, expertise in assessment and knowledge of the statutory frameworks. The Examination Board as a whole must cover these different areas of expertise. When an Examination Board is being compiled, the Executive Board can therefore choose to require each individual member to possess expertise in the different areas, or to have the Examination Board made up of members with different subject-matter expertise and an expert in assessments.

In the appointment process, the Programme Management can nominate new members to the Executive Board. However, such nominations are not binding. Furthermore, at minimum, the Executive Board assures that the Examination Board is listened to regarding the appointment of new members (WHW Section 7.12a, paragraph 3); the Examination Board has an advisory role in this regard.

The Executive Board appoints the members of the Examination Board. The law stipulates no term for such appointments. This means that, in principle, members are appointed for an indefinite period. To guarantee the continuity of knowledge and decision making within the Examination Board , it is desirable members are appointed for a period of four years, with one possible extension of another four years. The same maximum extension applies to the Chair of the Examination Board , but s/he is appointed for a five-year term to avoid the risk of key members and the Chair leaving the Board at the same time.

As a rule, the Examination Board or the Programme Director nominates a candidate member to the Executive Board. The Executive Board then assesses whether the candidate member meets the criteria set for subject-matter and assessment expertise, and consults the members of the Examination Board on the nomination via the Chair of the Examination Board . The Examination Board has an advisory role in this (WHW, Section 7.12a, paragraph 3). An appointment letter is then sent to the candidate member concerned. If the appointment of a Chair is involved, this will be specifically stated in the appointment letter. Should the Executive Board have any reason not to wish to appoint the candidate in question as a member, they will contact the Head of Learning Design and Development Lead for further consultation.

## 1C: Composition of the Examination Board

Team Academy has chosen a setup in which there is a separate Examination Board for each Master’s programme. The central Examination Board is composed of the chair, the vice-chair, an official secretary and one or more members per programme. The permanent examination committee consists of a minimum of 3. Members of the Examination Board preferably are evenly distributed in age, gender and cultural background and able to represent the programme they are active in.

One statutory obligation is that at least one member of the Examination Board is a lecturer within the programme(s) for which the Examination Board is installed. Moreover, one of the members must be “independent” and capable of making well-considered decisions if a conflict of interests arises. Taking the above arguments into account, the profile for internal members is as follows:

An internal member of an Examination Board :

Is a member of the fulltime or parttime faculty of Team Academy and involved in teaching in the programme(s) covered by the Examination Board ;

Follows a training course on his/her area of expertise within the framework of his/her tasks on Examination Board within the first year of membership;

Possesses the ‘soft’ trait of independence and is capable of making a well- considered decision in the event of any conflict of interests. Independence means being independent in behaviour, daring to maintain and defend a personal standpoint that is in opposition to that of others (and to any one-sided interest) in the interest of the quality of the programme, actions in this regard are objective and critical, and recognizing and anticipating situations in which personal and quality interests are (potentially) in conflict.

Has preferably been involved in teaching within the programme(s) or has been involved in developing the new programme for a minimum of three years.

Preferably has a Basic or Senior Qualification Examination (BKE / SKE) or similar. An alternative for the BKE can be the MOOC Erasmus course on Assessing in Higher Education.

Besides the internal member(s), it is mandatory to have an external member in the Examination Board . The goal of including an external member is to increase external legitimacy regarding the assessment and examinations. The external expert thus adds an external perspective to the quality assurance of the assessments and examinations of the programmes that fall under the Examination Board . The external expert can be a fellow subject-matter expert at another university or from a different discipline within the same university. It may be someone who is an expert in the field of assessment, or someone from the professional field (in the Netherlands or abroad).

Due to the ‘weight’ of the Examination Board , at least one of the members (preferably the Chair) should be in possession of a Senior Qualification Examination (SKE). Other members should also strive to obtain this recognition as this adds to the status of the Examination Board .

External members may not be involved as lecturers, or have a role in the management and development of the education provided in the programmes that fall under the Examination Board . Managers with financial responsibility are excluded from being members of Examination Board s. In addition, there are several officials who will not be eligible for membership due to the incompatibility of membership with their other tasks. At Team Academy, the following officials are excluded from sitting on Examination Board s:

Members of the Foundation Board;

Members of the Executive Board;

Directors of Colleges or Graduate Schools;

Programme Directors and Directors of Operations / Operational Managers;

Chairs of the Advisory Boards of the programmes that fall under the Examination Board ;

Student Advisors.

The first six officials are excluded from membership due to their line management or financial management responsibilities regarding the education provided. The Chairs are excluded from membership due to the incompatibility of their tasks (advising the Programme Management) with those of the Examination Boards (monitoring the programmes as a whole). Finally, Student Advisors are excluded from membership due to a possible conflict of interests between the interests of the student on the one hand, and the decisions of the Examination Board on the other. In addition to the officials mentioned above, the Executive Board may exclude other officials from membership if this is desired. The membership of a member of the Examination Board terminates when:

the member takes up a position that is incompatible with membership of the Examination Board ;

the member’s employment is terminated (for internal members);

the member wishes to terminate the membership;

four years after the initial appointment has passed. The Examination Board secretary maintains a retirement schedule and interim vacancies will be filled as soon as possible;

The Examination Board member no longer performs satisfactorily.

In the final instance, the only way to terminate the membership is by a decision of the Executive Board, possibly in combination with immediate suspension. However, this is seen as a far-reaching measure and can only take place if this measure is proportionate[2](#footnote2). As a rule, the Examination Board , its Chair, informs the programme management of any unsatisfactory performance on the part of (a member of) the Examination Board , and the programme management apprises the Executive Board of the situation.

## 1D: Tasks and Profiles of the Examination Board members

The Examination Board operates at three levels:

Strategically it looks at external and internal developments, assesses the impact on current policies and anticipates on it by its composition of the annual plans, guidelines and directions;

Tactically it monitors the quality of assessment (on set criteria defined in the assessment policy), delegated processes;

Operationally it delegates certain tasks in standardized procedures.

The Examination Board meets a minimum of twice a year. Currently, the frequency is set at a monthly internal meeting and a quarterly formal meeting which includes representative(s) from the Management Team. Decisions are made by a majority of the members. The (Vice)Chair and the official secretary will consult more frequently about, for example, the requests of students. The meetings of the Examination Board are, in principle, closed. The Examination Board can invite guests to attend meetings, including a Student Advisor, Educational Quality Advisor or a programme manager The subjects that are dealt with at plenary meetings include advising on the TER and on the assessment policy of a programme, and finalising the assessment forms for the final projects of bachelor’s and master’s programmes. The vice chair takes the meeting minutes, while the official secretary is also responsible for setting down the decisions and advice of the Examination Board and for communicating them to the parties involved.

## 1E: Activities of the Examination Board

The Examination Board must assure the quality of exams and diplomas. To this end, the legislature has given the Examination Board the tasks and powers mentioned below. The tasks of the Examination Board can be mandated to persons and Boards. In this regard, it is important that it is clearly set down to whom the tasks are mandated and what responsibilities are covered by the mandate and under what conditions.

To determine, in an objective and expert manner, if a student meets the requirements set down in the Rules & Regulations (TER) in respect of the knowledge, insight and skills necessary to be awarded a qualification (WHW, Section 7.12, paragraph 2).

To assure the quality of exams and final examinations (WHW, Section 7.12b, paragraph 1 under a).

To set down guidelines and instructions within the framework of the TER in order to assess and approve the results of exams and final examinations (WHW, Section 7.12b, paragraph 1 under b).

To grant exemptions from taking one or more exams (WHW, Section 7.12b, paragraph 1 under d).

To take measures in the event of fraud (WHW, Section 7.12b, paragraph 2).

To assign assessors to conduct exams and determine the results thereof (WHW, Section 7.12c).

To award certificates and related diploma supplements, as proof that a final examination was passed (WHW, Section 7.11, paragraphs 2 and 4).

To grant permission for a student to follow a flexible study programme, for which the final examination leads to the award of a qualification (WHW, Section 7.12b, paragraph 1 under c).

To issue a transcript stating the exams passed to persons who have passed more than one exam but to whom no certificate can be awarded (WHW Section 7.11, paragraph 5).

To draw up an annual report of its activities (WHW, Section 7.12.b, paragraph 5).

Below, these ten tasks / activities are further explained in more detail.

Determining, in an objective and expert manner, if a student meets the requirements set down in the TER in respect of the knowledge, insight and skills necessary to be awarded a qualification

This means that the Examination Board must assess if the student has achieved the attainment level of the programme. If a student has passed all components of the exam in accordance with the provisions of the TER, the qualification can be awarded. The sum of the learning objectives of the individual components should, after all, lead at a minimum to the set attainment levels being achieved. The Examination Board can determine that passing the individual programme components will be closed with a final exam. In such cases, this requirement must be set down in the TER. Within this task, the Examination Board is also the body that is authorised, in special cases, to deviate from the provisions of the TER (i.e. the application of the bachelor-before-master requirement). Several examples of this are: permitting a modified exam or final examination format, extending the period of validity of an exam result, replacing individual units of study with a different unit of study that has the same learning objective and deviating from possibly mandatory participation in a practical exercise. Moreover, the Examination Board can determine, under conditions to be set by the Examination Board itself, that not every exam needs to be passed in order to record a pass for the final examination.[4](#footnote4)

Assuring the quality of exams and final examinations

WHW, Section 7.12b, paragraph 1 under a states that the Examination Board has the task of assuring the quality of exams and final examinations. This provision was included in the Act based on the findings of the Education Inspectorate that Examination Boards spend too little time on “quality assurance and the quality policy involving exams and final examinations”. In practice, this task includes:

Assessing the quality and level of examination papers and related composite examinations.

Determining (possibly) whether the examination assignments and exams meet those final programme and /or test objectives in the presence of final programme and/or test objectives;

The assessment of the results of the grade examinations.

Conducting consultations about determining the result/assessment standard (caesura/cutting scores) of the exams;

The handling of complaints about the made examinations (including the outcome);

Identifying risks that threaten the quality of examinations and making proposals to the university to limit those risks.

Following developments within and outside the organisation that may influence the quality or topicality of the examination.

Drawing up and implementing an assessment policy is an important tool in assuring the quality of exams and final examinations.

Setting down guidelines and instructions within the framework of the TER in order to assess and approve the results of exams and final examinations

The Examination Board should advise the Executive Board annually on the TER. In this way, the Examination Board can add substance to the quality assurance of the programme’s assessment programme.

Granting exemptions from taking one or more exams

The Examination Board has the power to grant a student an exemption for one or more subjects. The way in which this task is carried out is set down in the TER of the programme concerned. Whether or not an exemption can be granted is assessed against the TER. It is therefore necessary that the grounds for granting exemptions are set down in the TER of the relevant programme.[5](#footnote5) Section 7.13, paragraph 2 under r of the WHW clearly states that these grounds could lie in exams or final examinations taken earlier in the higher education system, and in knowledge and skills acquired outside the higher education system.

Broadly speaking, one condition for granting an exemption is that the replacement component has roughly the same learning objective as the component of the curriculum for which an exemption is granted. This task can be mandated to a person or a Board, but the Examination Board remains responsible. The Examination Board should set down who is authorised to sign such transcripts and under what conditions (e.g. the procedure and the delivery of a report to the Examination Board on an annual basis).In addition, it is possible for the Examination Board to have included in the TER components that are frequently used to replace standard components of the curriculum. In such instances, a request for an exemption is ‘automatically’ approved.

Taking measures in the event of fraud

The Examination Board is the body that must take measures in the event of fraud or other forms of academic misconduct..

Assigning assessors to conduct exams and determine the results thereof

An important instrument available to the Exam Committee for its task of assuring the quality of exams and final examinations, is the assigning of assessors. By law, this task lies with the Exam Committee.

Awarding certificates and related diploma supplements, as proof that an exam was passed

The Examination Board is the body that awards the certificate to the student once it has been determined that the student has met the requirements (cf. 1). The certificate and diploma supplement must be signed by the Chair of the Examination Board . In special cases, one of the other members of the Examination Board (with the exception of the external member) may sign the certificate and diploma supplement. The official secretary or members of staff who are not members of an Examination Board are not permitted to sign the certificate and diploma supplement.

Granting permission for a student to follow a flexible study programme, for which the exam leads to the award of a qualification

Team Academy currently has no flexible study programmes. The expectation is that in the future such programmes will be possible (expansion of the modular structure of programmes). The prior approval of the Examination Board will then be a condition for determining if a flexible programme is of the required level and if the study load and attainment levels of the programme are satisfactory.

Issuing a transcript stating the exams passed to persons who have passed more than one exam but to whom no certificate can be awarded

The Examination Board has the power to issue a transcript to students who have passed more than one exam, but to whom no certificate can be awarded. Such a transcript lists the exams that were passed. This can be important for students if, for example, they are transferring from one programme to another and are entitled to an exemption from a component of the new programme based on the results achieved earlier. This task can be mandated to a person or a Board, but the Examination Board remains responsible. The Examination Board should set down who is authorised to sign such transcripts and under what conditions.

Drawing up an annual report of its activities

The Examination Board should submit an annual report on its activities to the Executive Board. The aim of this annual report is:

to ensure the accountability of the Examination Board to the Executive Board;

to deliver input for the Programme Director, Associate Dean of Degree Programmes and the Executive Board on any improvements to the quality of the education;

to provide management information. This information is often requested in the accreditation procedure of a programme.

## 1F: Decisions of the Examination Board

The Examination Board may make decisions on many different matters. How the Examination Board makes its decisions will be set out in the house rules (cf. the Recommendation on p. 17). Examples of decisions set down in the WHW include:

Decisions to grant exemptions.

Approval of the examination schedules of students.

Decisions to award a qualification. In other words, the decision that a student has achieved the attainment levels of the programme.

Decisions to award a distinction, such as Cum Laude.

Decisions to allow a different form of examination for a student with a disability.

Decisions involving the general assessment policy of a programme, for example, approving an assessment form for final projects.

Drawing up guidelines for assessors.

Imposing sanctions for fraud and plagiarism.

Assigning assessors.

Decisions on requests for an extension of the period of validity of an exam.

Decisions on permitting additional opportunities to take an exam.

Determining alternative programme composition (for example, when a curriculum revision is concerned).

Approval of study units successfully completed abroad.

Decisions to approve a flexible study programme.

Not every decision needs to be made by the full Examination Board . The Chair can make several of the decisions referred to above independently. The decision-making process, however, must be transparent. It is therefore up to the Examination Board to set down in the regulations who is authorised to make what type of decision. The guidelines below can be used to this end:

The gravity of the decision plays a role. In the event that an appeal may be lodged against a decision, for example, against the imposition of sanctions for fraud or plagiarism, it is desirable that the decision is made by the full Examination Board .

Decisions involving the assessment or examination policy of a programme (determining alternative final projects, guidelines for assessors), should always be made by the full Examination Board .

A request that has never arisen earlier, will at least be submitted to the Chair.

The official secretary can deal with standard decisions (e.g. replacing a subject that has been passed earlier). Decisions of this type must be well documented, for example in a separate document entitled “Decisions of the Examination Board ”.

In the event that a decision must be made by the full Examination Board , the decision will be made by a majority vote. If the vote is tied, the Chair’s vote will break the tie. If the decision is to be sent to the party or parties involved, the Chair must sign the decision. In the event of so-called ‘standard decision’, the decision can be signed by another person with the designation i.o. (on behalf of) or b.a. (in the absence of).

## 1G: Complaints Handling

Students should, in the first instance, submit any complaints concerning exams or final examinations to the Examination Board of the programme involved, with the exception of objections or appeals. When handling complaints, the Examination Board applies the principle of the right to a hearing and to present counter-arguments: both the student and lecturer will be heard. At least one member of the Examination Board , in addition to the Chair, should be involved in handling complaints. If a student submits a request or complaint to the Examination Board that concerns an assessor who is a member of the Examination Board , the assessor concerned may not take part in handling the request or complaint

A student has, on the basis of the Burgerlijk Wetboek (Civil Code) the right to challenge (“wraking” in Dutch) a member of the Examination Board if s/he has the impression that this member is not impartial to the case. The Central Examination Board decides upon this challenge. Should a member of the Examination Board be challenged then s/he is excluded from taking part in the decision.

Should a student want to appeal a decision taken by the Examination Board, they have the right to appeal decisions of the Examination Board through the independent NRTO disputes and appeals procedure.

# Section 3: Assessment of Learning

## Article 3.1 Types of Assessment

Assessment in the Master’s programme follows a programmatic and competency-based approach. Learners build a portfolio of diverse evidence throughout the programme including, but not limited to, assignments, projects, reflections, presentations, and workshop artefacts (see the Assessment Policy for more information). ### Article 3.3 Extensions

Learners who are unable to meet an assessment deadline may request an extension in advance by submitting the designated extension form to programme management. Extension requests for regular assignments must be submitted at least four days before the deadline indicated in Canvas. Supporting evidence (e.g., medical documentation) may be required depending on the nature of the request. The decision authority for low- and medium-stakes assessments lies with the Programme Manager; for high-stakes assessments, including major integrative assignments, the decision authority lies with the Examination Board. Decisions on extension requests must be communicated to the learner within four days of the request.

### Three-Step Extension Process

The Examination Board endorses the following structured approach for managing extension requests:

First Request: Automatically Granted  
 The first extension request for any assessment is approved automatically, provided it is submitted on time and through the designated process.

Second Request: Requires Reflective Justification  
 A second request requires the learner to provide a written reflection explaining why an additional extension is necessary, acknowledging the responsibilities of combining work, personal life, and part-time Master’s study, and outlining how they will prevent further extension needs.

Third Request: Referral to Internal Confidential Advisor  
 A third request triggers a mandatory referral to an internal confidential advisor, who can support the learner in addressing underlying challenges affecting study progress. The extension may be granted only after this consultation.

Programme management must inform the Examination Board if a learner submits an unusually high number of extension requests. The Examination Board will advise on any additional measures deemed necessary to safeguard academic progress and integrity.

### Final Project Extensions

Learners who cannot meet a Final Project submission deadline may request an extension of up to three months by submitting the extension form at least two weeks before the deadline. Final Project extension decisions fall under the Examination Board, which may apply the same three-step process outlined above where appropriate.

## Article 3.5 Determining and Announcement of Results

This concerns the delegated procedure of the announcement of the assessment results. Officially, it is the Examination Board who determines the results as final. However, in practice it is a procedure which has been delegated to the assessor and programme management.

In case it is considered a valid complaint, the Examination Board will determine whether it concerns an individual incident or not. If not, a guideline or direction might follow.

Only if all procedures have been applied correctly, the results are determined final. The programme manager makes the provisional result final by making it visible to the student in question on the learning management system.

In case the programme manager detects a problem with an assessor or assessor regarding their adherence to assessment deadlines or regarding a tendency to assess consistently extremely low or high, the programme manager is required to share their concern with the Examination Board .

## Article 3.11 Inclusive Assessment and Examination Provisions

Special needs students are learners with permanent or temporary disabilities that affect their education life academically or socially. These disabilities can include, but are not limited to chronic illnesses, physical disabilities, visual or hearing impairments, speech / language disorders, learning difficulties, psychiatric / psychological problems, hyperactivity and attention deficit disorder.

The special needs student should apply with the programme manager if they feel the need for extra support during their studies. They can do this at any time during their studies. If a learner does not apply with the programme manager, they cannot claim any negative effects of their disability on their study progression or study results.

The programme manager fills out an “adjustment request form” in consultation with the learner in question and communicates the student’s requests and disability status to the programme management, instructors and examination board.

If a student is unable to comply with the standard examination or assessment criteria due to a disability, chronic illness, neurodiversity, or other special personal circumstances, the student may submit a request for reasonable adjustments to the Examination Board which will decide on the matter within two weeks of submission. Reasonable adjustments may include, but are not limited to:

Extended examination time;

Alternative formats of examination (e.g. oral examination instead of written, or vice versa);

Adapted physical examination environment;

The use of assistive technology or other supportive tools;

Alternative forms of assessment that align with the intended learning outcomes.

The Examination Board will assess the request on a case-by-case basis and will ensure that the adjustments:

Do not compromise the academic standards or the learning outcomes of the programme;

Enable the student to demonstrate their competences under equitable conditions;

Are aligned with the university’s policy on inclusive education and the Dutch Higher Education and Research Act (WHW).

Students are encouraged to consult the programme manager to discuss their situation and prepare their request for adjustments.

The Examination Board’s decision regarding adjustments will be communicated in writing to the student within 14 days of submission. If the request is denied, the decision will be accompanied by the rationale and information on the appeals procedure.

Confidentiality will be maintained throughout the process, and the privacy of the student will be respected in accordance with applicable privacy regulations.

A learner may ask the Examination Board to delay the issuing of his/her certificate or diploma. The Examination Board will reply to such a request within two weeks of submission.

The names of courses/modules listed on the assessment results and diplomas must correspond to the official course/module names as specified in the Teaching and Examination Regulations (TER) that were active during the academic year in which the course/module was completed.

If a course/module name changes after a student has completed it, the original name under which the course/module was completed will be retained on the assessment results list and diploma.

Exceptions to this rule may only be made in cases where programme management determines that using the original name would cause significant confusion for external stakeholders, such as employers or accrediting bodies. In such cases, a footnote or explanatory remark will be included in the grade list or transcript.

Students have the right to request clarification or documentation regarding the equivalency of module names, if needed for external purposes, such as recognition by other universities or employers.

## Article 3.13 Academic Integrity

Plagiarism and academic misconduct are defined as any act by a student that partially or completely precludes an accurate assessment of their knowledge or skills or those of another student. Plagiarism can include handing in someone else’s work or copying work, long or short, from a source without providing a reference or acknowledgement. This also applies to AI tools that help learners generate responses on any work that is part of an assessment.

If the assessor has a good reason to suppose that plagiarism or academic misconduct has taken place, he/she must report it to the Examination Board and the programme management immediately on the same day that the supposed plagiarism or academic misconduct is detected. This means the case should be reported in writing mentioning the name of the student, the name of the assessment, and a detailed description of the supposed plagiarism or academic misconduct.

The programme manager can ask the assessor for more details if needed before the programme manager informs the learner and invites the learner, at the most one day after receiving the assessor’s report, to a hearing about the claimed plagiarism or the academic misconduct. This meeting should take place within a maximum of three days after receiving the assessor’s report.

The programme manager puts his/her findings from the meeting with the learner on paper and forwards his/her report together with the report from the assessor to the Examination Board together with his/her (non-binding) advice on the matter. This needs to be done within one working day after the meeting with the learner has taken place.

Within a maximum of two weeks upon receiving the reports from the programme manager, the Examination Board makes a final decision based on the evidence. In the case of plagiarism the learner’s work is assigned 0 or no evidence.

This final decision is sent in writing to the programme manager who shares the results immediately with both the learner and assessor in question.

In case of a repeated misconduct or plagiarism, the programme management, after consulting the Examination Board, has the right to terminate the learner’s registration permanently. For this the programme management needs to submit a file containing the name of the student, the name of the assessment(s), and a detailed description of the supposed plagiarism or academic misconduct to the Chair of the Examination Board.

The learner will receive a formal written notice of this decision from the programme management.

The learner can appeal to this decision within six weeks of the announcement of the decision. The written appeal needs to be directed to the Chair of the Examination Board.

The Examination Board will respond to the plaintiff within one week of submission of the complaint or request. This might be an acknowledgment of receipt containing a date and time-limited period of investigation and ruling. The general applicable term for this is a maximum of six weeks. This term can be extended once by a period of four weeks. Furthermore, it should explain the investigation procedure of the case (why it requires further investigation, an explanation whether or not this includes the hearing of both parties, what works will be requested / reviewed). The Chair of the Examination Board will then make the final decision on the result of the appeal.

For an outline of the different kinds of academic misconduct and the different consequences, please refer to appendix 3: Academic Misconduct Consequences Guidelines.

## Article 3.14 Exemptions through Recognition of Prior Learning

Conditions: Credit transfer is when a learner puts credits from another higher education (institute-level) qualification towards a new qualification. Credits from qualifications that have been completed in the last 5 years are acceptable and include Bachelor's degrees, postgraduate diplomas, and Master's degrees. Prior certificated learning is institute-level learning that happened outside of Team Academy. This can include professional development and employment-based awards, or qualifications awarded by another education university. Prior experiential learning relates to knowledge and skills learners have gained through experience. For example, through employment, reading and research, life experience, non-credit courses, voluntary work, etc.

If a learner thinks they have covered the content of a module/ modules via any of the three types of learning above, they can apply for RPL by contacting the programme manager about their application who will invite them for an advisory consultation. In this consultation, the programme manager amongst others explains how to fill out the application form and how to build a portfolio (if needed) and a deadline is set for the application to be submitted. If a learner does not meet this deadline, they automatically forgo on the opportunity to receive RPL credit exemptions.

Once the learner has completed the application form and portfolio, they submit it to the Chair of the Examination Board. S/he will appoint two assessors who are academically connected to the Masters programme to evaluate the application. Within three weeks of submission, the learner will be invited to a conversation with these assessors. The objective of the assessment conversation is to examine some subjects in the portfolio in depth. In the assessment conversation the assessors have the option to ask for additional evidence. And as a result of the conversation, the assessors may require the learner to undertake additional assignments, like an essay or a programme analysis. All this information serves as input for the assessors to define a written advice for the examination board.

The examination board examines the advice and decides whether RPL credit exemptions will be awarded, and if so, for which parts of the programme and for which period of time. The validated result of this decision is shared with the learner and the programme manager within two weeks of the examination board receiving the advice.

Finally, the learner receives a study plan with a remaining study path outlined from the programme manager. Once approved, recognition of prior learning is valid for a limited period (up to five years) which will be stated in the letter.

If a learner gains a credit exemption for a module or modules (or module component where permitted), they may be entitled to a pro-rata reduction in their tuition fees. If this is the case, the assessors will inform the programme manager and s/he will inform the finance department.

Neither degree certificates nor diplomas are awarded on the basis of exemptions alone.

# Academic Appeals Procedure

This guide is intended to help explain the Academic Appeals process. If students are considering making an Academic Appeal, for example because they do not agree with their enrolment status or another decision of the programme management or faculty, they are encouraged to read this guide carefully. This guidance document has been prepared by the university. Every Academic Appeal is considered individually and general statements below may not apply in all circumstances. Academic Appeals may take several weeks to process.

## Academic Appeals

An academic appeal is a procedure that allows students, in certain circumstances, to request a review of a decision relating to their academic progress or award. Circumstances in which students may submit an appeal include the following:  
 • a decision on progress that prevents the student from continuing their studies, usually a decision to terminate registration  
 • a decision requiring the student to interrupt their studies  
 • a decision not to make an award to the student, resulting in their studies being frozen or terminated  
 • a decision to award a lower qualification than the one for which the student is registered, for example when a student registered for a master’s degree is recommended for a certificate  
 In an Academic Appeal, any claims made by students must generally be supported by compelling evidence. Acceptable evidence may include medical certificates, statements from academic staff about particular events, copies of specific regulations, detailed personal statements or confirmation from relevant authorities regarding involvement in a specific incident. Contemporaneous, independent and medical evidence is particularly important and often carries greater weight with an Appeal Panel. Evidence should clearly indicate the significance of the impact on the student and relate to specific time periods. It is the responsibility of students to provide the evidence for the Appeal Panel to consider. The institute will not approach doctors or other individuals on a student's behalf.

## Grounds for Appeal

Students may not appeal simply because their results are not as good as they had hoped or are worse than they believe they deserve. Appeals that challenge the academic judgement of evaluators are not permitted. Acceptable grounds for appeal include the following:  
 • circumstances that materially affected the student's performance and were not known to the Academic Panel or other relevant body at the time of the decision and that were not reasonably practicable to make known beforehand  
 • evidence that a procedural irregularity occurred in the assessment process and that this irregularity may have affected the result  
 A procedural irregularity refers to deviations from the institute’s guidelines or procedures, such as an error in grading, an administrative mistake, incorrect mark calculation or a failure to consider mitigating circumstances submitted on time.  
 • evidence of prejudice or bias on the part of evaluators or within the assessment process  
 The regulations on mitigating circumstances require students to notify the faculty of any relevant matters affecting their academic performance, such as personal or medical circumstances, at the time they occur and to provide supporting documents. Unless there are very good reasons why students were unable to notify the faculty or supply evidence at the time, it is likely that an appeal will be disallowed. The institute may operate systems of double scoring or moderation to ensure accuracy and fairness. Scores are awarded based on academic judgement and agreed assessment criteria. The institute’s competency based model encourages learners to improve their mastery levels through resubmission opportunities according to the Teaching and Examination Regulations.

## How to Submit an Appeal

If students decide that they have eligible grounds for appeal, they should complete and submit an Academic Appeal form with supporting evidence. The official appeal form must be used rather than submitting a statement or letter. The deadline for submitting an appeal is 10 institute working days from the result release date. Appeals submitted after the deadline will normally be disallowed. Students must submit appeals on their own behalf and are responsible for obtaining all necessary evidence. The institute will not contact doctors, employers or other parties on a student’s behalf. Programme managers can support students in understanding the procedure but cannot write the appeal for them. Medical evidence must come from a qualified practitioner. Students residing overseas must submit medical evidence in English or include a certified translation. The institute may conduct checks to ensure the authenticity of submitted evidence. The Examination Board will assess whether the appeal contains eligible grounds. To support its decision, it may request information or a report from faculty members. If the appeal does not demonstrate eligible grounds, the student will be notified in writing and may be asked to provide additional information within a stated timeframe. If students are informed that their appeal does not demonstrate eligible grounds, they may request a review of this decision. To do so, they must show that a procedural irregularity occurred in the handling of the appeal or that evidence was not fully considered. The request will be reviewed by the Appeal Panel.

## Academic Appeal Panel

Academic appeals are initially handled by the Examination Board. Students cannot attend Examination Board meetings. In the case of an appeal, both the student and the involved educator may be invited to a hearing if the Examination Board considers this necessary. Cases are considered based on the submitted documentation.

## Appeal Decision

Students will normally receive formal notification of the outcome within seven working days after the panel meeting. The decision letter will explain the reasons for the outcome and will highlight the possibility to request a review if students believe that a procedural irregularity occurred or that some evidence was not fully considered. If students wish to appeal a decision of the Examination Board, they have the right to do so through the independent NRTO disputes and appeals procedure. Once the institute’s appeal procedures have been completed, students will receive a Completion of Procedures Letter.

## Failure and Second Chance Opportunity Procedure

Within a programmatic assessment model, progression is based on the continuous collection of evidence demonstrating competency development. Instead of defining failure at the level of individual assessments, the programme evaluates whether sufficient evidence has been provided to support a judgement at the certification decision point.

A certification is considered fail when, at the certification decision point:

• the total body of evidence across all learning outcomes and evidence points contributing to that certification does not demonstrate the required level of mastery  
 • no or incomplete evidence has been submitted within the defined timeframe and no allowance has been approved under exceptional circumstances  
 • academic conduct regulations have been breached, resulting in evidence being invalidated for decision making

The panel evaluates evidence holistically. A certification is not achieved when the collected evidence does not sufficiently demonstrate the required competency level.

### Progression to the next certification despite not yet achieving the previous one:

When a student has not yet demonstrated sufficient evidence at the certification decision point, they may still proceed to the next certification, provided that:

• the next certification includes learning outcomes, projects or applied tasks that offer meaningful opportunities to further develop or demonstrate the outcomes that were not yet achieved  
 • continuation is pedagogically appropriate and does not hinder the student’s overall learning trajectory  
 • faculty identify any specific competencies that require additional focus during the following certification

This continuation does not replace the requirement to ultimately achieve the previous certification. Instead, it allows the student to benefit from naturally occurring evidence opportunities in later certification projects and applied activities.

A certification remains not yet achieved until sufficient evidence is demonstrated, and the final certification decision can be made independently of the student’s participation in subsequent certifications.

### Retaking a certification in a subsequent cohort

Students who, despite given opportunities, still have not demonstrated sufficient evidence by the end of the next certification may retake the certification in a subsequent cohort.

Retaking a certification means:  
 • continuing evidence development with the next cohort  
 • following the learning activities, assessment opportunities and competency expectations of that cohort  
 • generating new or updated evidence where requirements have changed or where existing evidence is insufficient

### Reflective requirements and approval process

Students preparing to retake a certification must submit:  
 • a reflective account explaining prior challenges and evidence gaps  
 • a rigorous action plan describing how they will approach evidence development differently

Faculty/panel evaluates the request.

### Evidence validity

Previously collected evidence remains valid unless:

• academic misconduct has taken place  
 • competency standards have changed  
 • the evidence has become outdated or insufficient for decision making

Students may be required to produce additional or updated evidence as needed.

### Finality of certification

Once a certification has been achieved, students are not permitted to retake it or submit further evidence for it.

## Exceptional Circumstances

Exceptional circumstances are personal circumstances that:

• are beyond the student’s control  
 • could not reasonably have been prevented or accommodated  
 • have had a significant and demonstrable negative effect on the student’s ability to generate evidence of learning  
 • occurred during the period relevant to the affected certification or evidence-development period

All elements must be met for an exceptional circumstances application to be considered.

## Temporary Withdrawal

• A student wishing to withdraw temporarily must obtain written approval for a specific period (maximum five years) and may need to satisfy conditions before re-enrolling.  
 • Students who withdraw without permission or who fail to re-enrol by the agreed date may be considered permanently withdrawn.  
 • Students who fail to maintain enrolment and do not respond to communication from their supervisor or the institute for the duration of one certification period will be withdrawn.  
 • Students with approved temporary withdrawal may rejoin a later cohort and continue developing evidence toward certification.

 Students must contact their programme manager within two weeks of being informed that a certification was not yet achieved. The programme manager conducts an advisory consultation to review circumstances, potential evidence, and the application process. A submission deadline is agreed; missing it means forfeiting the opportunity to apply.  

Submission of the application  
 Students complete the exceptional circumstances form by the agreed deadline. The form includes the option to request a certification retake or temporary withdrawal.

 Students submit the application and supporting documentation to the program manager of the Professional Masters in Applied AI programme. Students receive a decision within three weeks. ## Academic Misconduct Consequences Guidelines

Academic integrity is essential to the quality and credibility of the Professional Masters in Applied AI. Within a programmatic assessment model, students continuously collect evidence of their learning and competency development. Academic misconduct undermines the validity and trustworthiness of that evidence and may therefore impact certification-level decisions, as well as the reputation and integrity of the programme and the university.

Types of Academic Misconduct

Academic misconduct refers to any act, intentional or unintentional, that compromises the integrity, authenticity, or fairness of evidence submitted for educational or certification purposes. Misconduct includes, but is not limited to, the categories outlined below.

Academic Misconduct: Violating institutional policies by manipulating learning evidence, altering submitted work, misrepresenting contributions, or otherwise compromising the authenticity of evidence used for competency evaluation.

Bribery: Providing or accepting money, goods, services, or favours in exchange for academic advantage or influence over evidence evaluation or certification decisions.

Cheating: Using unauthorised information or materials to generate evidence, including copying another student’s work, accessing unauthorised sources, or using unapproved aids when generating evidence intended for independent submission.

Collusion: Two or more students working together on a task that is explicitly designated as individual work, resulting in shared or indistinguishable evidence.

Conspiracy / Facilitation: Knowingly assisting or attempting to assist another student in committing academic misconduct, including sharing answers, providing unauthorised support, or enabling plagiarism or cheating.

Duplicate Evidence / Self-Plagiarism: Submitting the same or substantially similar evidence for multiple assessment moments or certifications without explicit approval, thereby misrepresenting the originality of evidence production.

Fabrication: Creating, altering, or falsifying information, data, sources, or evidence, including inventing results, modifying findings, or presenting fabricated material as legitimate evidence of learning.

Improper Use of Digital Tools: Using calculators, software, AI tools, or computing resources in ways that violate instructions or misrepresent authorship and effort. This includes unauthorised sharing of files, prompts, or computational outputs that contribute to evidence intended for independent evaluation.

Improper Online Behaviour: Using online platforms to obtain or disseminate unauthorised support, accessing materials not permitted for the task, or receiving outside assistance for tasks designated as individual evidence points.

Misrepresentation: Any act intended to deceive facilitators, supervisors, the Examination Board, or other academic bodies to obtain advantage. Examples include falsifying reasons for extensions, misrepresenting attendance, or providing misleading information about evidence creation.

Plagiarism: Presenting another person’s ideas, data, methods, expressions, or words as one’s own without proper acknowledgement. Plagiarism includes:

• Poor Disguise: using the structure or essential content of a source with superficial changes

• Misinforming: providing inaccurate or incomplete source information that prevents verification

Plagiarism applies to text, code, images, designs, models, datasets, and all other forms of evidence.

Why Academic Misconduct Is Serious

Academic misconduct undermines:

Integrity of the programme: The credibility of competencies demonstrated, evidence collected, and certifications awarded depends on trust in the authenticity of student work.

Value of degrees and certifications: If misconduct becomes prevalent, it devalues degrees and the reputation of the university, affecting all graduates.

Fairness and equity: Misconduct harms students who meet expectations honestly and follow ethical standards.

Research integrity: Fabricated or falsified data leads to invalid results, wastes resources, and damages trust in applied research practices.

Ethical and professional formation: The programme trains professionals who must act responsibly in real-world, high-impact contexts. Misconduct signals a failure to meet fundamental professional and ethical expectations.

Competency and safety in professional practice: Graduates may enter roles that require high levels of autonomy and decision-making. Misconduct during training could mask insufficient mastery of competencies.

Legal and contractual obligations: The university must uphold academic standards under accreditation and regulatory frameworks. Misconduct may jeopardise these obligations.

Team Academy considers academic integrity essential to preserving the trustworthiness, quality, and value of its education. Academic misconduct compromises the authenticity of evidence used in programmatic assessment and may therefore affect high stake decisions in addition to potential sanctions.

# Sanctions for Academic Misconduct

Sanctions for academic misconduct are applied when a student’s actions undermine the authenticity, integrity or validity of the evidence used in programmatic assessment, or compromise the fairness and credibility of the certification process. Sanctions are determined proportionally, taking into account:

• the seriousness of the misconduct  
 • the intent (deliberate, negligent, accidental)  
 • the impact on evidence validity  
 • the stage of the programme  
 • whether the student has committed previous misconduct

Sanctions are grouped into four levels. The Examination Board decides which sanction applies based on the circumstances of the case.

# Level 1: Formal Warning

A formal written warning recorded in the student’s file.  
 This sanction applies when:

• it is a first occurrence  
 • the misconduct is minor  
 • the misconduct was accidental or due to misunderstanding  
 • the impact on the validity of the evidence is limited and does not materially affect certification decisions

A Level 1 sanction may be accompanied by formative guidance or an integrity coaching session.

# Level 2: Evidence Invalidation and Additional Learning Requirements

Evidence affected by the misconduct is declared invalid and cannot be used in certification decisions.  
 The student must provide new or corrected evidence within a specified period.

This sanction applies when:

• the misconduct affects the validity of one or more evidence points  
 • the misconduct is more than minor, or repeated  
 • the misconduct was deliberate, but limited in scope  
 • the integrity of the competency evaluation for that certification is impacted  
 • the student can reasonably still remediate within the cohort

Consequences may include:

• submitting new or additional evidence  
 • completing an alternative or replacement task  
 • loss of the opportunity to use AI/digital tools for certain tasks  
 • increased supervision or monitoring of academic integrity

# Level 3: Certification Impact

The misconduct materially compromises the student’s ability to demonstrate required competencies within the current certification period.

This sanction applies when:

• the misconduct significantly damages the validity of the evidence portfolio for that certification  
 • the misconduct is major  
 • it is a repeat offence following Level 1 or 2 sanctions  
 • authenticity or authorship of substantial evidence is in question

Consequences include:

• postponement of the certification decision  
 • requirement to continue evidence development in the next certification  
 • requirement to retake the certification with a later cohort  
 • additional conditions applied to the retake (e.g., reflective report, increased supervision)

Previously submitted evidence that is valid may remain usable unless misconduct occurred in that evidence.

# Level 4: Suspension or Exclusion

Applied in cases of very serious misconduct that severely compromises integrity, trust or safety.

This sanction applies when:

• there is substantial deliberate deception, fabrication or falsification  
 • misconduct involves large-scale plagiarism, identity fraud, or purchased work  
 • there is repeated misconduct despite previous sanctions  
 • the misconduct affects the integrity of the entire certification or programme  
 • the student’s behaviour poses a serious risk to the programme, university or external partners

Possible measures include:

• temporary suspension from the programme  
 • removal from learning activities or internships  
 • temporary loss of access to systems or tools  
 • permanent exclusion from the programme (only in the most severe cases)  
 • notation of misconduct in the diploma supplement (only for the gravest cases)  
 • legal action (e.g., document forgery)

# Right to Be Heard and Appeal

Before a sanction is imposed, students have the right to:

• be informed of the allegation  
 • review the evidence  
 • provide a written explanation  
 • be heard by the Examination Board (or a delegated committee)

Students may appeal the Examination Board’s decision according to the programme’s appeals procedure and, where applicable, through the NRTO professional and independent board of appeals.
