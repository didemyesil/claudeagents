## Standards / criteria list

**Framework: Assessment of existing programmes — Extensive framework**
(source §4.3)

### Standard 1 — Intended learning outcomes
"The intended learning outcomes tie in with the level and orientation of the
programme; they are geared to the expectations of the professional field,
the discipline, and international requirements."

The intended learning outcomes demonstrably describe the level of the
programme (Associate Degree, Bachelor's, or Master's) as defined in the
Dutch Qualifications Framework, as well as its orientation (professional or
academic). They tie in with the regional, national or international
perspective of requirements currently set by the professional field and the
discipline. Insofar as applicable, they are in accordance with relevant
legislation and regulations. The points of departure for the set-up of the
programme chime with the educational philosophy and profile of the
institution. The intended learning outcomes are periodically evaluated.

### Standard 2 — Curriculum: orientation
"The curriculum enables the students to master appropriate (professional or
academic) research and professional skills."

The curriculum ties in with current (international) developments,
requirements and expectations in the professional field and the discipline.
Academic skills and/or research skills and/or professional competencies are
substantiated in a manner befitting the orientation and level of the
programme.

### Standard 3 — Curriculum: content
"The contents of the curriculum enable students to achieve the intended
learning outcomes."

The learning outcomes have been adequately translated into educational
objectives of (components of) the curriculum.

### Standard 4 — Curriculum: learning environment
"The structure of the curriculum encourages study and enables students to
achieve the intended learning outcomes."

The curriculum is designed in a manner conducive to achievement of the
intended learning outcomes. The teaching-learning environment encourages
students to play an active role in the design of their own learning process
(student-centred approach). The design of the learning environment chimes
with the institution's educational philosophy. If the programme is taught in
a language other than Dutch (including if it bears a foreign-language name),
the programme must justify its choice.

### Standard 5 — Intake
"The curriculum ties in with the qualifications of the incoming students."

The admission requirements in place are realistic with a view to the
intended learning outcomes.

### Standard 6 — Staff
"The staff team is qualified for the realisation of the curriculum in terms
of content and educational expertise. The team size is sufficient."

Teachers have sufficient expertise in both subject matter and teaching
methods, and a sufficient command of the language of instruction. Staff
policy is conducive to this. Sufficient staff is available to teach the
programme and tutor students.

### Standard 7 — Facilities
"The accommodation and material facilities (infrastructure) are sufficient
for the realisation of the curriculum."

The accommodation of the programme and its facilities are in keeping with
the intended learning outcomes and the teaching-learning environment.

### Standard 8 — Tutoring
"The tutoring of and provision of information to students are conducive to
study progress and tie in with the needs of students."

Students receive appropriate tutoring (including students with a functional
impairment). Information provision by the programme is adequate.

### Standard 9 — Quality assurance
"The programme has an explicit and widely supported quality assurance system
in place. It promotes the quality culture and has a focus on development."

The programme organises effective periodic feedback supporting achievement
of the intended learning outcomes. Existing programmes implement appropriate
improvements based on the results of the previous assessment, via
appropriate evaluation and measurement activities. Evaluation outcomes
demonstrably constitute the basis for development and improvement. Those
responsible are held to account regarding the programme's contribution to
the institution's strategic goals. The programme committee, examination
board, staff, students, alumni, and the relevant professional field are
actively involved in internal quality assurance. Design, recognition and
quality assurance processes are in keeping with the European Standards and
Guidelines (ESG). The programme publishes accurate, reliable, accessible
information regarding its quality.

### Standard 10 — Student assessment
"The programme has an adequate student assessment system in place."

Student assessments are valid, reliable, and sufficiently independent. The
quality of interim and final examinations is sufficiently safeguarded and
meets statutory quality standards. The examining board exerts its legal
authority. Tests support students' own learning processes.

### Standard 11 — Achieved learning outcomes
"The programme demonstrates that the intended learning outcomes are
achieved."

Achievement of the intended learning outcomes is demonstrated by test
results, final projects, and the performance of graduates in actual practice
or in postgraduate programmes. Per the source's note on assessing achieved
learning outcomes: the panel selects and examines final projects from a
minimum of 15 recent graduates (all of them if fewer than 15 graduated in
the assessed period), balanced across satisfactory/good/very good, and
diverse across modes of study, locations, specialisations, graduation
tracks, and curricula.

**Final conclusion**: weighted and substantiated, per the assessment rules
below.

## Required evidence types (per standard)

Derived directly from the source's self-evaluation/appendix requirements
(§4.4.3); not separately itemised per standard in the source, so mapped here
by content:

| Standard no | Required evidence type |
|---|---|
| 1 | Self-evaluation narrative on how intended learning outcomes tie to Dutch Qualifications Framework level/orientation, professional field/discipline requirements, and (where applicable) legislation |
| 2 | Self-evaluation + curriculum appendix showing alignment with current (international) developments in the professional field/discipline |
| 3 | Curriculum appendix mapping intended learning outcomes to educational objectives of curriculum components |
| 4 | Self-evaluation describing curriculum structure and student-centred design; language-of-instruction justification if not Dutch |
| 5 | Self-evaluation describing admission requirements and their fit to intended learning outcomes |
| 6 | Appendix: staff team composition, qualifications, language proficiency, staff policy, team size |
| 7 | Self-evaluation description of accommodation/facilities relative to the learning environment |
| 8 | Self-evaluation description of student tutoring and information provision |
| 9 | Self-evaluation description of the QA system: evaluation/measurement activities, follow-up on the previous assessment, stakeholder involvement, published quality information |
| 10 | Appendix: teaching and examination regulations; self-evaluation on assessment validity/reliability/independence and examining board authority |
| 11 | Selection of ≥15 recent final projects (or all, if fewer graduates) with diversity across modes/locations/specialisations, plus grading data |

## Format / presentation rules

- Self-evaluation report: **maximum 20 pages** (extensive framework),
  excluding appendices. Institution may agree another format/scope with the
  panel.
- Appendices provide insight into curriculum set-up/content, staff team
  composition, and teaching and examination regulations.
- Self-evaluation includes a **student chapter** — a contribution submitted
  by students; the programme is encouraged to establish an independent,
  representative student chapter.
- Panel: minimum 4 members, at least one a higher-education student;
  independence requirements apply (no ties to the institution for 5+ years,
  no consultancy work for the programme for 5+ years).
- Site visit: in principle one day, with separate sessions for students and
  staff, plus a "development dialogue" on potential improvements (reported
  separately, not part of the accreditation application).

## Verdict scale

NVAO's own terminology (source §4.3.1, extensive framework, existing
programmes):

| NVAO term | Maps to system default |
|---|---|
| Meets the standard | Meets |
| Partially meets the standard | At Risk |
| Does not meet the standard | Does Not Meet |

NVAO's own **final-conclusion** combination rule (distinct from this
system's per-standard synthesis in Stage 4 — kept here for reference when
comparing our mock report against NVAO's actual gatekeeping logic):

- **Positive**: programme meets all standards.
- **Conditionally positive**: programme meets Standard 1 and partially meets
  a maximum of five standards, with the panel recommending conditions
  (feasible to remedy within 2 years).
- **Negative**: programme fails one or more standards; OR partially meets
  Standard 1; OR partially meets 1–5 standards without conditions being
  recommended; OR partially meets six or more standards.

## Language / terminology notes

- Use **"programme"** (UK spelling), matching NVAO's own English
  translation — not "program."
- "Generic quality" is a defined term in the source: *"The quality that,
  from an international perspective, may reasonably be expected from a
  higher education Associate Degree, Bachelor's or Master's programme."*
  Use it, don't paraphrase it.
- Say **"intended learning outcomes,"** not "learning objectives" — the
  source is consistent and deliberate about this term throughout.
- Degree levels are always one of: Associate Degree, Bachelor's, Master's —
  per the Dutch Qualifications Framework, with an explicit
  professional/academic orientation split.
- The Dutch text is legally binding; this English config is built from
  NVAO's own translation, which "has no formal status" per NVAO's own
  disclaimer — flag this if a run's outcome is high-stakes enough to warrant
  Didem cross-checking against the Dutch original.
