# Teaching and Examination Regulations (TER) — Tech MBA
## Section 1: General Provisions

### Article 1.1 Applicability of the Regulations

1. These regulations apply to the teaching and examinations for the Tech MBA at Team Academy.
2. These regulations apply to anyone enrolled in the Tech MBA at Team Academy.
3. Programme Management ensures that the guidelines prescribed in this document are followed by the faculty.
4. *[Tech-MBA-specific — validity dates to be confirmed by programme management; the Master's TER's 2025-2026 cohort dates do not automatically apply to the Tech MBA's own cohort cycle, documented separately as starting November 2023/2025 per `Tech MBA Supervision Guide.md`.]*

### Article 1.2 Definitions of Terms Used

Definitions follow the Master's TER (academic year, assessor, admissions consultant, certification, coach, cohort, competency-based, diploma, ECTS, facilitator, learner, LMS, module, programme, supervisor, workshop), with the following Tech-MBA-specific terms added, already in use in the Tech MBA's own documentation: **action learning coach** (facilitates action learning sets), **mentor** (provides group mentoring tailored to career aspirations), **certification** (one of the Tech MBA's four certifications: Transform Your Business; Lead Your Business; Innovate and Grow Your Business; Leadership and Action Research).

## Section 2: Programme Structure

### Article 2.1 Structure of the Academic Year

1. The Tech MBA is offered in consecutive phases made up of four certifications followed by the Final Project, as set out in `Tech MBA Programme Handbook_2024.md`.
2. The Tech MBA is a fully online degree programme.
3. The Tech MBA carries a total study load of 60 EC (1680 hours, at 28 hours per EC), in accordance with the ECTS standard — this matches the Master's TER's ECTS conversion (Article 2.1.3 of the source) and is independently confirmed by the Tech MBA's own Programme Handbook ("60 ECTS credits," "1680 hours").
4. There is one intake per year, in November, consistent with both the Master's TER (Article 2.3.10) and the Tech MBA's own cohort documentation ("November 2025 Cohort").

### Article 2.2 Organisation of the Programme

1. The average weekly workload is approximately 20 hours per week, consistent with the Tech MBA Programme Handbook's own statement ("dedicating approximately 20 hours per week").
2. The institute's learning management system (Canvas) and communication platforms (Slack, AmTech-email) are used for all digital components, per the Tech MBA Supervision Guide.
3. Programme competencies, outcomes and objectives are set out in the Tech MBA Programme Handbook.

### Article 2.3 Application & Selection Process

Follows the Master's TER's application and selection process (online application with diplomas/certifications/CVs; intake interview by the Admissions Consultant assessing motivation and readiness; English-language proficiency requirement; right of appeal to the Examination Board via the Programme Manager). *[Tech-MBA-specific entry requirements and any foundations/proficiency-test equivalent are not yet separately documented for the Tech MBA and should be confirmed with Programme Management — flagged as a residual item, not assumed.]*

### Article 2.4 Learning as a Personal Accountability

Learners are expected to participate in all scheduled learning activities (workshops, action learning sets, mentoring, supervision meetings), notify the Programme Manager of absence in advance where possible, and provide alternative learning evidence for missed activities — as per the Master's TER, Article 2.4.

### Article 2.5 Career Coaching / Mentoring

The Tech MBA's group mentoring component, described in the Tech MBA Programme Handbook, serves the equivalent function to the Master's TER's Career Coaching / Talent Accelerator provision — supporting learners' professional identity and career opportunities, without affecting academic results.

### Article 2.6 Certificate and Degree

1. Learners receive a certificate upon successful completion of each of the Tech MBA's four certifications, based on a positive decision following assessment (module projects, weekly tasks, reflective accounts, knowledge checks, and — where applicable — VIVA).
2. Learners who complete all four certifications and the Final Project, meeting all programme learning outcomes, are awarded the Tech MBA degree.
3. The awarded degree and title are formally stated on the diploma in accordance with national and university regulations.

### Article 2.7 Termination of Studies and Re-registration

Follows the Master's TER, Article 2.7, in full: enrolment may be terminated in accordance with the Dutch Higher Education and Research Act (WHW), Articles 7.42, 7.32 and 7.33; temporary withdrawal, suspension and re-enrolment follow the same written-request procedure.

## Section 3: Assessment of Learning

### Article 3.1 Types of Assessment

Tech MBA assessment is **Tech-MBA-specific** in its weighting, as already documented in the Tech MBA Programme Handbook: Module Projects (50%), Weekly Tasks/Activities (30%), Action Learning Reflective Accounts (10%), and Knowledge Checks (10%) per certification, with a caesura of 60/100 to pass. The Final Project (21.5 ECTS, per the Tech MBA Supervision Guide) is assessed as: Project Plan (15%), Action Research Methodology (30%), Technology Focus (40%), Video Presentation & Q&A/VIVA (15%). This differs in structure from the Master's programmatic low/medium/high-stake portfolio model, and the Tech-MBA-specific figures given here — not the Master's figures — govern.

### Article 3.2 Assessments and Deadlines

Follows the Master's TER's principles (assignments visible on LMS with submission deadlines; late submission accepted only with an approved extension; extension requests follow the standard form/process) — see the Tech MBA Supervision Guide's own extension provisions for the Final Project (up to six months, requested at least two weeks before the deadline, per TER Article 3.5 as cross-referenced there).

### Article 3.3 Determining and Announcement of Results

Assessors record results within a reasonable working-day window after submission (as under the Master's TER); results remain provisional until administrative checks are complete; the Examination Board determines results as final.

### Article 3.4 Progression and Certification Principles

Progression decisions for the Tech MBA are made following the completion of each certification, based on the assessment weighting in Article 3.1 above and a minimum caesura of 60/100.

### Article 3.5 Improvement, Resubmission, and Reconsideration

Follows the Master's TER's principle that learners may resubmit assessed work within a defined window following original feedback, subject to Programme Manager/Examination Board oversight.

### Article 3.6 Validity Period of Results

Assessment results remain valid for an unlimited period unless the knowledge/skills tested become outdated relative to the current course, with a minimum validity period of six years from the granting of the mark, as under the Master's TER, Article 3.6.

### Article 3.7 Discussing the Feedback & Assessment Results

Learners may arrange a call with the assessor/supervisor to discuss results or seek further feedback, as under the Master's TER, Article 3.7.

### Article 3.8 The Right to Appeal Assessment Results and Decisions

Follows the Master's TER, Article 3.8, in full: learners have the right to inspect evaluated work; appeals are permitted only on grounds of mitigating circumstances, procedural irregularity, or evidence of bias — not simple dissatisfaction with a result; the time limit to contest results on such grounds is 10 working days after publication, extendable by application to the Examination Board.

### Article 3.9 Inclusive Assessment and Examination Provisions

*(Carried over in full — this is one of the two academic-integrity/non-discrimination provisions Standard 1 specifically requires; see also the shared Gender Equality and Equal Opportunities Statement.)* The Tech MBA aims to provide an inclusive and accessible learning environment ensuring all learners, regardless of personal circumstances, are given equal opportunities to demonstrate their knowledge, understanding and skills. Learners with permanent or temporary disabilities — including chronic illness, physical disabilities, sensory impairments, learning difficulties, psychological conditions, or neurodivergence — should apply to the Programme Manager for support at any time during their studies. The Programme Manager completes an adjustment-request form in consultation with the learner and communicates the learner's requests and disability status to programme management, instructors and the Examination Board. Where a learner cannot comply with standard assessment criteria due to disability, chronic illness, neurodiversity, or other special personal circumstances, they may request reasonable adjustments (extended time; alternative examination format; adapted physical/online environment; assistive technology; alternative assessment forms aligned with intended learning outcomes) from the Examination Board, which communicates its decision in writing within 14 days, with rationale and appeal information if denied.

### Article 3.10 Certificates and Diploma

The Examination Board issues a certificate for each completed certification and a diploma to learners who have completed all four certifications, met all programme learning outcomes, submitted and passed the Final Project (including the VIVA/Q&A session), and fulfilled all procedural and financial requirements. Certificates and diplomas are issued in English with an accompanying Diploma Supplement.

### Article 3.11 Academic Integrity

*(Carried over in full — the other of the two academic-integrity/non-discrimination provisions Standard 1 specifically requires.)* Plagiarism and academic misconduct are defined as any act by a learner that partially or completely precludes an accurate assessment of their knowledge or skills, or those of another learner, including handing in someone else's work, copying without acknowledgement, and undisclosed or unreflective use of AI tools in assessed work. The Tech MBA's own Final Project Agreement Form already operationalises this specifically for the Final Project, requiring learners to plan, disclose and account for their use of Advanced Technology Tools (AI Usage Planning; ongoing updates; an AI Use Statement and reflective account at submission; retained evidence of use), consistent with this Article and with Team Academy's institution-wide Policy on the Use of Advanced Technology in Teaching and Learning. If an assessor has good reason to suspect plagiarism or misconduct, they report it to the Programme Manager, who informs the learner and convenes a hearing; the Examination Board makes the final decision. In case of plagiarism, the learner's work is assigned a mark of 0. In case of repeated misconduct, programme management, after consulting the Examination Board, may terminate the learner's registration permanently, subject to appeal to the NRTO Board of Appeals within six weeks.

### Article 3.12 Exemptions through Recognition of Prior Learning

Follows the Master's TER, Article 3.12: the Examination Board may grant exemptions through Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL) on the basis of a substantiated learner request and evidence, decided within four weeks; degree certificates and diplomas are not awarded on exemptions alone.

### Article 3.13 Exemptions through Force Majeure

Follows the Master's TER, Article 3.13: individual force majeure (illness, impairment, pregnancy, special family circumstances) and circumstantial force majeure (external emergency) provisions apply identically.

## Section 4: Learner Support and Guidance

### Article 4.1 Coaching and Supervision

Tech MBA learners receive structured support through group mentoring and action learning coaching during the certification phases, and individual supervision (Tech and LAR/Leadership-and-Action-Research supervisors) during the Final Project phase, as detailed in the Tech MBA Supervision Guide.

### Article 4.2 Programme Management

The Programme Manager is responsible for day-to-day coordination and implementation of the Tech MBA, acting as the primary point of contact for learners from enrolment to graduation — overseeing communications and onboarding, coordinating events/announcements, providing first-line guidance, and ensuring learners are informed of their rights, obligations and available guidance mechanisms — in close collaboration with faculty.

## Section 5: Evaluation of the Programme

The Tech MBA's learning experience is evaluated in accordance with the institute's quality assurance framework at activity, module, certificate and programme level, following a "Plan, Implement, Review, Improve" cycle, as detailed in *Institution-wide Quality Assurance Policy*.

## Section 6: Tasks and Responsibilities of the Examination Board

Follows the Master's TER, Section 6, in full — see *Examination Board Handbook* for the detailed handbook.

## Section 7: Hardship Clause

If demonstrable unreasonableness or unfairness of a predominant nature occurs, the Executive Board or the Examination Board may allow deviation from the provisions of this TER.
