# Development of the Programme Competencies in the Professional Master's in Applied AI

To develop the competencies of the applied technical modules of the programme, we followed these steps:

1. Desktop Research
2. Benchmarking Analysis
3. Competency Development Workshops with Faculty
4. External Validation with Industry Representatives

## Stage 1: Desktop Research

### 1.1 Purpose of Desktop Research

- Establish an evidence-based understanding of current and emerging industry needs in Applied AI and Data Science.
- Identify the optimal balance between AI-specific skills and data science foundations for a professional master's programme.

### 1.2 Key Industry Findings

a) AI in Job Titles
- AI Engineer is the #1 fastest-growing job right now, according to LinkedIn's 2025 Jobs on the Rise.
- The prevalence of AI titles in online job postings has grown to 65% in 2024.

b) AI in Global Reports
- According to the Future of Jobs by WEF 2025, AI and ML specialists are the third fastest-growing jobs.

c) AI in the Investments Landscape
- EU's InvestAI initiative will mobilise €200 billion in AI investment.

d) Data Science Roles Still Dominant
- Data scientists' roles are still in demand. AI is transforming the labour market, with Data Scientists leading demand at 67.99% of AI-related job postings.
- Many organisations expect data scientists to be proficient in machine learning and even AI deployment, while conversely, AI engineers need a solid grounding in statistics and data handling.
- The growing market demand for data science and analytics is driving strategic investments in advanced data tools and platforms.

### 1.3 The Convergence of AI and Data Science

- AI demand is rising rapidly, but data science remains foundational for most AI roles.
- Industry expects cross-functional skillsets: AI professionals with data handling/statistical expertise; data scientists with AI deployment skills.
- The market trend is towards integration rather than separation of AI and data science capabilities.

### 1.4 Insights for Curriculum/Competency Development

- A balanced programme is essential: maintain strong data science fundamentals (e.g. data exploration, statistical analysis, visualisation); embed AI-focused competencies (e.g. ML, DL, generative AI, MLOps and AI ethics).
- Position AI as an extension of data science, not a replacement.
- Prepare graduates for dual-role capabilities, able to design AI models and interpret/analyse data for strategic decision-making.
- Align competencies with both technical mastery and human skills (ethical application, domain-specific problem solving, cross-disciplinary communication), supported by leadership modules and project-based learning.

## Stage 2: Benchmarking with Other Programmes

See the full "Benchmarking the Future of Data Science and AI Education" report (saved separately in this appendices folder).

### 2.1 Purpose of Benchmarking

- Identify strengths, gaps, and innovative practices in AI and Data Science education relevant to the Applied AI Professional Master's.
- Ensure the programme is competitive, aligned with market demands, and differentiated in its value proposition.
- Focus comparison on: HBO professional master's programmes in the Netherlands, online platforms (MOOCs), and boot camps.

### 2.2 Key Findings from Benchmarking

a) Common Strengths Across Programmes
- Strong technical foundations: programming (Python, R), machine learning, statistical modelling, and data visualisation.
- Project-based learning and capstones with industry relevance (especially HBO and bootcamps).
- Ethical and responsible AI is increasingly embedded in advanced applied programmes.

b) Gaps Identified
- Limited social learning and personalised mentorship in MOOC-based models.
- Underemphasis on leadership, interdisciplinary application, and strategic management skills in many formats.
- Uneven industry collaboration — bootcamps and HBO excel, MOOCs lag.

c) Differentiating Practices
- HBO: strong applied learning through industry projects and internships.
- Bootcamps: immersive, cohort-driven experiences with strong career support.
- MOOCs: flexible, modular learning with globally recognised certifications.

### 2.3 Comparative Dimensions

| Dimension | HBO | MOOC | Bootcamp |
|---|---|---|---|
| Technical Skills | High | High | Medium-High |
| Industry Collaboration | Strong | Low | Strong |
| Social Learning & Mentorship | Strong | Low | Strong |
| Flexibility | Medium | High | Low-Medium |
| Leadership/Soft Skills | Moderate | Low | Moderate |

### 2.4 Insights for Curriculum/Competency Development

- Combine HBO's applied industry focus with bootcamps' immersive, project-driven approach.
- Integrate mentorship and community-building to fill the social learning gap seen in MOOCs.
- Balance technical mastery with leadership, ethical AI, and interdisciplinary problem-solving to differentiate from purely technical bootcamps.
- Maintain continuous industry engagement (e.g. connecting with field representatives, work-based final project) to ensure practical relevance.
- Adopt flexible delivery elements from MOOCs (e.g. a blend of online and face-to-face learning) without sacrificing personalised support.

## Stage 3: Competency Development Workshops with Faculty

### 3.1 Purpose of the Workshops

- Translate market insights (Stage 1) and benchmarking results (Stage 2) into a competency-driven curriculum for the programme.
- Leverage the expertise of faculty members with both industry experience and doctoral-level academic qualifications to ensure the competencies are academically rigorous and professionally relevant.

### 3.2 Process Overview

**a) Starting Point: Exit Profile and Programme Orientation**
The process began with defining the exit profile: what a graduate should look like upon completion of the programme. The orientation of the programme ensured all subsequent curriculum design decisions were outcome-driven and aligned with the programme's vision to produce AI professionals who are technically strong, ethically aware, and industry-ready.

**b) Workshops with Faculty**
Conducted as a series of collaborative workshops with the faculty team. Participants brought dual perspectives: industry practice (e.g. real-world problem solving, deployment contexts) and academic expertise (e.g. research-led insight, advanced methodologies). Activities included mapping competencies, stress-testing them against industry scenarios, and checking their alignment with accreditation and industry frameworks such as EDISON.

**c) Using Stage 1 and Stage 2 Insights**
From Stage 1 (Desktop Research): confirmed the need to balance AI-specific expertise with data science foundations. From Stage 2 (Benchmarking): saw the competitive advantage of combining HBO's applied learning with bootcamp-level immersion and learning flexibility from MOOCs.

**d) Competency Mapping with the EDISON Framework**
The programme made use of the EDISON Data Science Framework (EDSF), specifically its Data Science Life Cycle, to structure the competency set, covering Business Understanding, Data Analytics, Modelling, Deployment, and Maintenance. Data Engineering (also part of EDISON) was not included as a focus competency because the programme is designed as an Applied AI master's emphasising AI application, model deployment, and optimisation rather than specialised infrastructure/pipeline development; industry feedback indicated graduates would more often collaborate with dedicated data engineering teams rather than perform these functions themselves.

**e) EDISON's Influence**
Reinforced the need to integrate competencies beyond core modelling, such as business problem framing, deployment, and optimisation, to ensure graduates can deliver end-to-end AI solutions in real-world contexts.

### 3.3 Final Competency Areas (For Technical Modules)

Based on industry needs, benchmarking results, and EDISON alignment, the following competency areas were prioritised for the technical part of the programme:

1. Business Problem Framing
2. Exploratory Analytics
3. Machine Learning Modelling
4. Deep Learning
5. Natural Language Processing (NLP)
6. Computer Vision
7. Large Language Model Development
8. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) Implementation
9. Machine Learning Operations (MLOps)
10. Responsible AI

## Stage 4: External Validation with Industry Representatives (Applied AI Line)

### 4.1 Purpose of External Validation

The final stage of competency development involved validating the programme's technical competencies and curriculum structure with field experts working in AI and data science. The purpose was to ensure that the competency framework accurately reflects current professional practice and meets the expectations placed on graduates of an applied professional master's programme.

### 4.2 Validation Process

Industry representatives reviewed the competencies, curriculum outline and module sequence in line with the target competencies. Their feedback addressed the relevance of the competency areas, practical applicability of module content, and alignment with professional expectations. Input was collected through a structured review and written commentary. The curriculum team analysed this feedback and incorporated it where it aligned with the programme's applied orientation and design principles. External consultation continues as part of the programme's improvement cycle.

### 4.3 Verified Feedback Themes and Programme Response

a) **Confirmation of the importance of an end-to-end applied AI lifecycle.** Industry stressed the importance of preparing students to understand and work across the full lifecycle of applied AI projects, including business understanding, modelling, deployment, and monitoring. This feedback confirmed the programme's existing structure, which already embeds an end-to-end applied AI process.

b) **Importance of communication, contextualisation and data storytelling.** Reviewers highlighted that communication and contextualisation of insights are essential capabilities for applied AI professionals and should remain part of the learning experience. The programme will continue to emphasise these competencies through project-based learning tasks where students present findings and justify decisions. No new content was required, as this feedback aligns with practices (e.g. projects) already present in the programme.

c) **Cloud deployment expectations within MLOps.** Cloud deployment was identified as a key professional skill for AI practitioners. The MLOps content has been made sure to exist in the curriculum to ensure alignment with typical organisational practices.

### 4.5 Ongoing Validation

External validation is an iterative process. The curriculum team continues to collect feedback from industry representatives and integrate relevant insights into module updates, project briefs, and competency refinement to ensure that the programme remains aligned with evolving practice in Applied AI.
