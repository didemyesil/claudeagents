## Standard 1 — Policy for Quality Assurance
- Source status: verified
- Standard statement: Institutions should have a policy for quality assurance
  that is made public and forms part of their strategic management. Internal
  stakeholders should develop and implement this policy through appropriate
  structures and processes, while involving external stakeholders.
- Guidelines requirements:
  - Forms a coherent institutional QA system that operates as a cycle for
    continuous improvement and contributes to the institution's accountability
  - Supports the development of a quality culture in which all internal
    stakeholders assume responsibility for quality and engage in QA at all
    levels of the institution
  - Has a formal status and is publicly available
  - Is most effective when it reflects the relationship between research and
    learning & teaching
  - Takes account of both the national context and the institutional context
  - Supports the organisation of the QA system
  - Supports departments/schools/faculties/institutional leadership/staff/
    students taking on their QA responsibilities
  - Supports academic integrity and freedom, and vigilance against academic
    fraud
  - Guards against intolerance or discrimination
  - Supports involvement of external stakeholders in QA
  - How the policy is implemented, monitored and revised is the institution's
    own decision
  - Covers any elements of the institution's activities that are subcontracted
    to or carried out by other parties
- Sample questions:
  1. What documents reflect the QA policy?
  2. Where published — open or internal only?
  3. Available to teaching staff, employees, students, employers?
  4. Do other institutions/stakeholders participate in designing it?
  5. How did the QA policy change over time?
  6. Results of assessing stakeholder satisfaction with the QA policy?
  7. Does the EP match the stated mission/strategy of the EO?
  8. How is research–teaching–learning linkage reflected?
  9. Does the QA policy include business/scientific community/staff/student
     interaction — mechanisms, examples?
  10. Are competences and decision-making processes for EP development
      defined?
  11. How transparent is educational process information to students?
  12. Are there EP objective/concept revision procedures?
  13. Do developed EPs comply with the EO's regulatory documents?
  14. Does the EO have a gender equality/equal opportunities concept?
  15. Describe the university's QA system and its application/continuous
      improvement within the EP.
  16. What activities are outsourced and how is adherence monitored?
- Required evidence type (summary): Published QA policy document; stakeholder
  involvement records; evidence of QA policy revision history and satisfaction
  assessment
- Panel lens: Education & Assessment Expert; Industry Representative; Student
- Note: Config's Guidelines text explicitly calls for involvement of external
  stakeholders and students in policy development — hence multi-lens tag. For
  ex-ante purposes, "revision history and satisfaction assessment" (Guidelines
  requirement above, and Sample Questions 5–6) should be presented as a
  planned review mechanism, not historical data — no prior cycle exists yet
  for this new programme.

## Standard 2 — Design and Approval of Programmes
- Source status: verified
- Standard statement: Institutions should have processes for the design and
  approval of their programmes. The programmes should be designed so that
  they meet the objectives set for them, including the intended learning
  outcomes. The qualification resulting from a programme should be clearly
  specified and communicated, and refer to the correct level of the national
  qualifications framework for higher education and, consequently, to the
  Framework for Qualifications of the European Higher Education Area.
- Guidelines requirements:
  - Study programmes are at the core of the HEI's teaching mission — academic
    knowledge and transferable skills
  - Programmes are designed with overall objectives in line with
    institutional strategy and explicit intended learning outcomes
  - Programmes are designed involving students and other stakeholders
  - Programmes benefit from external expertise and reference points
  - Programmes reflect the four purposes of higher education of the Council
    of Europe
  - Programmes enable smooth student progression
  - Programmes define expected student workload (e.g. ECTS)
  - Programmes include well-structured placement opportunities where
    appropriate
  - Programmes are subject to a formal institutional approval process
- Sample questions:
  1. Goal of the EP / general qualification / target group?
  2. Are qualification objectives adequately set out in governing documents
     and the Diploma Supplement?
  3. What skills/methodological competencies are taught?
  4. What skills (incl. foreign languages) are developed?
  5. Opportunities for successful learning/participation in society?
  6. How does the university monitor changes since the last QA procedure —
     recommendations, decisions, implementation?
  7. How are changes accounted for ahead of the next external assessment?
  8. Are professional-environment requirements reflected?
  9. Does the programme have a special profile (double degree, dual
     education, distance, etc.) properly described/justified?
  10. How is content–learning-outcomes correspondence to level (bachelor/
      master/doctoral) ensured?
  11. Reasonable compulsory/elective/optional module volumes?
  12. Mobility window (e.g. a semester abroad)?
  13. How does student-assessment correlate with ECTS?
  14. Is the EP harmonious re: expected outcomes — do discipline contents
      ensure achievement?
  15. Course names match content?
  16. Modern science reflected?
  17. Classroom-vs-self-study ratio appropriate?
  18. Module descriptions complete and informative?
  19. Is the EP technically feasible re: student workload?
  20. Are EP development/approval procedures defined and documented?
  21. Is there external examination of the EP — by whom, what requirements?
- Required evidence type (summary): Documented EP design/approval procedure;
  intended learning outcomes mapped to NQF/QF-EHEA level; curriculum
  structure, ECTS workload, module descriptions; external EP examination
  records
- Panel lens: Education & Assessment Expert; Subject Expert; Industry
  Representative
- Note: Sample questions reference "professional-environment requirements"
  and external examination of the EP — justifies Industry Representative and
  Subject Expert tags alongside Education & Assessment Expert. "External EP
  examination records" (evidence type) and Sample Question 21 are
  ex-ante-sensitive: for a new programme this is likely a planned/committed
  external review step rather than a completed one — must not be presented as
  already executed unless it genuinely has been.

## Standard 3 — Student-Centred Learning, Teaching and Assessment
- Source status: verified
- Standard statement: Institutions should ensure that the programmes are
  delivered in a way that encourages students to take an active role in
  creating the learning process, and that the assessment of students
  reflects this approach.
- Guidelines requirements:
  - Student-centred learning and teaching plays an important role in
    stimulating students' motivation, self-reflection and engagement
  - Implementation respects and attends to student diversity, enabling
    flexible learning paths
  - Considers/uses different delivery modes where appropriate
  - Flexibly uses varied pedagogical methods
  - Regularly evaluates and adjusts delivery/methods
  - Encourages learner autonomy with adequate guidance/support
  - Promotes mutual respect in the learner-teacher relationship
  - Has appropriate procedures for student complaints
  - Assessors are familiar with testing/examination methods and receive
    support developing their skills
  - Assessment criteria and marking criteria are published in advance
  - Assessment lets students demonstrate the extent intended outcomes are
    achieved, with feedback linked to learning advice
  - Where possible, more than one examiner is used
  - Regulations account for mitigating circumstances
  - Assessment is consistent, fairly applied, per stated procedures
  - A formal student-appeals procedure is in place
- Sample questions:
  1. Respect/attention to different student groups, flexible trajectory?
  2. How are student needs factored into EP formation?
  3. What trajectory-forming opportunities do students have?
  4. Equal opportunities across student groups for achieving outcomes?
  5. Does EP delivery account for individual student peculiarities?
  6. Own research in teaching of EP subjects (examples)?
  7. Feedback system on teaching methods/outcome assessment?
  8. How is student academic freedom ensured?
  9. Consistency/transparency/objectivity of the outcome-assessment mechanism
     per EP?
  10. How is the knowledge/skills/competency assessment mechanism
      implemented?
  11. What teaching/learning forms used, including innovative (e.g. online)?
  12. Sufficient variability?
  13. Do didactic concepts ensure career-oriented competency formation?
  14. Are assessment tools competency-oriented, sufficiently variable across
      qualification criteria?
  15. Modular-oriented, combined assessment tools?
  16. Do normative/training documents reflect examination procedures and
      types?
  17. Do they account for students with disabilities?
  18. Complaint/appeal procedures in the EP?
  19. What learning-outcome assessment methods are used?
  20. How are evaluators trained/professionally developed?
- Required evidence type (summary): Description of pedagogical methods and
  delivery modes used; feedback system records; published assessment
  criteria/marking criteria; student complaints/appeal procedure
- Panel lens: Education & Assessment Expert; Student
- Note: None.

## Standard 4 — Student Admission, Progression, Recognition and Certification
- Source status: verified
- Standard statement: Institutions should consistently apply pre-defined and
  published regulations covering all phases of the student "life cycle",
  e.g. student admission, progression, recognition and certification.
- Guidelines requirements:
  - Providing necessary conditions/support for student academic progress is
    in everyone's interest
  - Fit-for-purpose admission, recognition and completion procedures are
    vital, particularly for mobile students
  - Access policies, admission processes and criteria must be implemented
    consistently and transparently
  - Induction to the institution and programme is provided
  - Institutions need processes and tools to collect, monitor and act on
    student-progression information
  - Fair recognition of qualifications, study periods and prior learning
    (incl. non-formal/informal) relies on institutional recognition practice
    in line with the Lisbon Recognition Convention
  - Fair recognition also relies on cooperation with other institutions, QA
    agencies, and the national ENIC/NARIC centre
  - Graduation documentation must explain the qualification gained,
    including achieved learning outcomes and the context/level/content/
    status of studies completed
- Sample questions:
  1. How is the student contingent formed, what additional applicant
     requirements exist?
  2. How do students/applicants learn about contingent-formation procedures
     (admission, transfer, re-crediting, expulsion, etc.)?
  3. How does the university evaluate admission-process vs. subsequent
     progress correspondence?
  4. Is there Lisbon-Convention-compliant recognition of prior learning
     outcomes/qualifications?
  5. Are training requirements transparent to all target groups — what
     information channels exist?
  6. Is there a mechanism for recognising academic-mobility results and
     additional/formal/non-formal education results?
  7. Requirements for prior learning recognition — examples?
  8. What normative document regulates academic-mobility-result recognition?
  9. Is there an opportunity to prepare students for professional
     certification — what types are possible?
- Required evidence type (summary): Published admission/progression/
  recognition/certification regulations; Lisbon Convention-aligned
  recognition mechanism; ENIC/NARIC cooperation evidence; graduation
  documentation templates
- Panel lens: Education & Assessment Expert; Student
- Note: "Progression" evidence (Guidelines requirement and Sample Question 3)
  should be framed as the designed monitoring/regulatory mechanism for this
  ex-ante application, not demonstrated student outcomes — no cohort yet
  exists.

## Standard 5 — Teaching Staff
- Source status: verified
- Standard statement: Institutions should assure themselves of the
  competence of their teachers. They should apply fair and transparent
  processes for the recruitment and development of the staff.
- Guidelines requirements:
  - The teacher's role is essential to a high-quality student experience and
    knowledge/competence/skill acquisition
  - HEIs have primary responsibility for staff quality and a supportive
    working environment
  - Sets up clear, transparent, fair recruitment and employment-condition
    processes recognising teaching's importance
  - Offers and promotes professional development opportunities
  - Encourages scholarly activity linking education and research
  - Encourages teaching-method innovation and new-technology use
- Sample questions:
  1. Are human resources sufficient to implement the EP and guarantee its
     profile?
  2. What documents reflect personnel policy — management transparency?
  3. How are promotions made?
  4. Does teacher personnel potential correspond to university strategy and
     EP specifics?
  5. How is teacher competence level defined relative to professional
     standard, industry framework, EQF?
  6. Requirement differences by position skill level?
  7. How do teacher qualification requirements differ by EP level (BA/MA/
     PhD)?
  8. Requirements for experience, field, teaching quality?
  9. Is teaching/examination load balanced?
  10. Is an interdisciplinary approach implemented?
  11. Measures for professional development/staff qualification?
  12. How is teachers' professional/personal development stimulated —
      self-development, innovative methods, integration of scientific
      activity and education?
  13. Do teachers use ICT (online education, e-portfolio, MOOC)?
  14. How has teaching quality/graduate competency changed with new
      technologies?
  15. Are industry practitioners involved in teaching, how are they
      selected?
  16. Academic mobility dynamics/results of EP teachers over the past 5
      years and contribution to quality/EP development?
  17. How is teacher involvement in research work conducted?
- Required evidence type (summary): Recruitment/employment policy documents;
  staff qualifications vs. EQF/industry framework; professional development
  records; practitioner-involvement records; academic mobility records
  (5-year)
- Panel lens: Education & Assessment Expert; Subject Expert; Industry
  Representative
- Note: Config's Sample Question 15 explicitly asks whether "industry
  practitioners [are] involved in teaching" — Industry Representative lens
  justified. **"Academic mobility records (5-year)" (evidence type) and
  Sample Question 16 are 3–5 year track-record items per the config's own
  table; per the config's ex-ante note this requirement is relaxed for this
  application** — Stage 2 should present staff qualifications/experience to
  date rather than force a 5-year mobility history for a not-yet-running
  programme.

## Standard 6 — Learning Resources and Student Support
- Source status: verified
- Standard statement: Institutions should have appropriate funding for
  learning and teaching activities and ensure that adequate and readily
  accessible learning resources and student support are provided.
- Guidelines requirements:
  - Institutions provide a range of resources to assist student learning —
    physical (libraries, study facilities, IT) and human (tutors,
    counsellors, advisers)
  - Support services matter particularly for student mobility
  - The needs of a diverse student population (mature, part-time, employed,
    international, disabled) are accounted for when allocating/planning/
    providing resources and support
  - The shift toward student-centred, flexible learning is accounted for
    when allocating/planning/providing resources and support
  - Internal QA ensures resources are fit for purpose, accessible, and
    students are informed of available services
  - Support and administrative staff need to be qualified with development
    opportunities
- Sample questions:
  1. Are financial resources/educational infrastructure sufficient for EP
     goals?
  2. Do material-technical/information resources ensure planned EP results —
     how is material-resource development planned?
  3. What information systems are used, what do they solve/serve?
  4. How do students access subject information?
  5. How is the website used to inform students/employees/stakeholders —
     what's published, how often updated, is current information
     accessible?
  6. Do information resources match EP specifics — plagiarism checks on
     research/graduation works/theses?
  7. Access to educational internet resources, functioning Wi-Fi?
  8. Are library resources sufficient, long opening hours?
  9. Are online technologies used — their expediency?
  10. Are individual support/consulting appropriately regulated/planned (EP
      manual, consulting hours, teaching-aid support)?
  11. Are students assisted with accommodation, internships, semesters
      abroad?
  12. What are procedures for supporting various student groups, including
      information/consulting?
  13. Do support programmes exist for disabled students and special life
      situations, do they meet requirements?
  14. Are cooperative relations established with other organisations for
      professional practice?
  15. Is educational equipment/software similar to industry equivalents?
  16. How does the EO ensure safety-requirement compliance in learning?
  17. How are diverse student-group needs accounted for in the EP context?
- Required evidence type (summary): Funding/infrastructure inventory vs. EP
  goals; library/IT/plagiarism-check/Wi-Fi resource description; student
  support procedure documentation; safety compliance evidence
- Panel lens: Education & Assessment Expert; Student
- Note: None.

## Standard 7 — Information Management
- Source status: verified
- Standard statement: Institutions should ensure that they collect, analyse
  and use relevant information for the effective management of their
  programmes and other activities.
- Guidelines requirements:
  - Reliable data is crucial for informed decision-making
  - Effective information collection/analysis about programmes and other
    activities feeds the internal QA system
  - Information gathered depends on institution type/mission
  - Information of interest includes key performance indicators
  - Information of interest includes student population profile
  - Information of interest includes progression/success/drop-out rates
  - Information of interest includes student satisfaction with programmes
  - Information of interest includes learning resources/support available
  - Information of interest includes graduate career paths
  - Various collection methods may be used
  - Students and staff should be involved in providing/analysing information
    and planning follow-up
- Sample questions:
  1. What information systems improve the internal QA system?
  2. How is EO/EP activity effectiveness and efficiency assessed?
  3. What quality-system assessment processes exist (teaching assessment,
     satisfaction monitoring, graduate employment/career analysis,
     information collection/processing)?
  4. What information-management processes exist, how are stakeholders
     involved in collecting/analysing information and decision-making?
  5. How constantly is processed information used to improve internal QA?
  6. How are risks identified/projected from information analysis?
  7. How is internal reporting on information management conducted?
  8. How do tools change when information requirements change?
  9. What communicative mechanisms implement the EP plan and changes?
  10. How does the EO protect information?
- Required evidence type (summary): Description of information collection/
  analysis system; KPI and student-progression data; stakeholder involvement
  in information use; risk identification records
- Panel lens: Education & Assessment Expert
- Note: Config's Guidelines/sample questions for this standard are framed
  institutionally (information systems, internal QA feedback loops) without
  a clear subject-matter, industry, or student-specific angle — single-lens
  tag reflects that, not an omission. "KPI and student-progression data"
  (evidence type) is ex-ante-sensitive: for a new programme this must be the
  designed data-collection system, not actual collected data (none exists
  pre-launch).

## Standard 8 — Public Information
- Source status: verified
- Standard statement: Institutions should publish information about their
  activities, including programmes, which is clear, accurate, objective,
  up-to-date and readily accessible.
- Guidelines requirements:
  - Information on institutions' activities is useful for prospective/
    current students, graduates, other stakeholders, and the public
  - Institutions provide information about programmes offered and selection
    criteria
  - Institutions provide information about intended learning outcomes
  - Institutions provide information about qualifications awarded
  - Institutions provide information about teaching/learning/assessment
    procedures used
  - Institutions provide information about pass rates
  - Institutions provide information about learning opportunities
  - Institutions provide information about graduate employment information
- Sample questions:
  1. What stakeholder information processes are defined in the EO?
  2. Does the EO have a dedicated information resource (website, portal)
     distributing EP-development-plan information?
  3. What distribution channels (media, information networks) inform the
     public/stakeholders?
  4. Does the EO publish information meeting stakeholder needs (programmes,
     expected outcomes, qualifications, teaching/learning/assessment
     procedures, passing points, tuition fees, study opportunities, teacher
     information, employment opportunities, partner cooperation, financial
     statements)?
  5. How is satisfaction with received-information quality/completeness
     investigated?
  6. Is there a graduate community/association — how does it participate in
     EO activities?
- Required evidence type (summary): Published information across EO web
  resources covering programmes, outcomes, qualifications, procedures, fees,
  employment; satisfaction-with-information records
- Panel lens: Education & Assessment Expert; Student; Industry Representative
- Note: Config's Sample Question 4 lists "employment opportunities" and
  "partner cooperation" among published-information items — justifies
  Industry Representative alongside Student (prospective-student-facing
  information) and Education & Assessment Expert.

## Standard 9 — On-going Monitoring and Periodic Review of Programmes
- Source status: verified
- Standard statement: Institutions should monitor and periodically review
  their programmes to ensure that they achieve the objectives set for them
  and respond to the needs of students and society. These reviews should
  lead to continuous improvement of the programme. Any action planned or
  taken as a result should be communicated to all those concerned.
- Guidelines requirements:
  - Regular monitoring/review/revision aims to keep provision appropriate
    and maintain a supportive, effective learning environment
  - Includes evaluation of programme content vs. latest discipline research
    (currency)
  - Includes evaluation of changing societal needs
  - Includes evaluation of student workload/progression/completion
  - Includes evaluation of effectiveness of student-assessment procedures
  - Includes evaluation of student expectations/needs/satisfaction
  - Includes evaluation of learning environment and support-service fitness
    for purpose
  - Programmes are reviewed/revised regularly involving students and other
    stakeholders
  - Information collected is analysed and the programme adapted
  - Revised programme specifications are published
- Sample questions:
  1. How are programmes monitored/evaluated at the university?
  2. How is EP objective achievement tracked?
  3. How is the need for curriculum/EP content change determined (labour
     market, employer requirements, societal demand)?
  4. Are students represented in collegiate bodies, involved in further
     learning development?
  5. How are students/employers/other stakeholders involved in EP review?
  6. Are survey results factored into evaluating/revising the EP?
  7. Does programme content reflect the latest science in the discipline?
  8. How is professional-practice satisfaction/quality monitored and its
     results used?
  9. How is achievement of professional-practice goals and compliance with
     upcoming professional activity guaranteed?
  10. How is student/community-needs monitoring conducted — demonstrate
      results.
  11. How is the learner's personal development monitored during the
      programme, what methods, where recorded?
  12. Are students and graduates surveyed?
  13. Do surveys account for student-load checks?
- Required evidence type (summary): Documented EP review procedure and
  records; stakeholder (student/employer) involvement in review; published
  revised programme specifications
- Panel lens: Education & Assessment Expert; Subject Expert; Industry
  Representative; Student
- Note: Config's Guidelines explicitly name "changing societal needs,"
  "latest discipline research," and involvement of "students and other
  stakeholders" — all four personas justified. **This standard is inherently
  track-record-oriented (monitoring/review cycles); per the config's
  ex-ante note, for this application it must be presented as the designed
  review mechanism/plan, not as evidence of reviews already conducted** — no
  prior cycle can exist for a programme not yet delivered.

## Standard 10 — Cyclical External Quality Assurance
- Source status: verified
- Standard statement: Institutions should undergo external quality
  assurance in line with the ESG on a cyclical basis.
- Guidelines requirements:
  - External QA verifies internal QA effectiveness
  - External QA acts as a catalyst for improvement
  - External QA offers new perspectives, assuring the institution and the
    public of quality
  - Institutions participate in cyclical external QA taking account, where
    relevant, of their legislative framework
  - External QA may take different forms and focus at different
    organisational levels (programme, faculty, institution)
  - QA is continuous — institutions ensure progress made since the last
    external QA activity is taken into account when preparing for the next
- Sample questions:
  1. Do the EO and EP participate in external evaluation procedures?
  2. How regularly do they undergo external assessment — what were the
     results of national/international evaluations the EO took part in
     (links if published)?
  3. What decisions followed the last external QA procedure, how/to what
     extent are recommendations implemented, how are changes accounted for
     ahead of the upcoming procedure?
  4. What role does external evaluation effectiveness play in developing the
     EO's internal QA system?
- Required evidence type (summary): Record of prior external QA
  participation and results (if any — first-time/initial accreditation may
  have none); evidence of how prior QA findings were acted upon
- Panel lens: Education & Assessment Expert
- Note: Config's Guidelines/sample questions frame this standard
  institutionally (prior external QA cycles, national/international
  evaluations) with no clear subject-matter, industry, or student-specific
  angle — single-lens tag reflects that. Config itself flags this evidence
  type as possibly absent for initial accreditation ("if any —
  first-time/initial accreditation may have none") — this is carried forward
  verbatim, not resolved by me. Stage 2 must not invent a prior-QA record
  where none exists; state absence plainly if that is the case for this
  applicant.
