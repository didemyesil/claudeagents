# Programme Outcomes of the Bachelor's in Applied Computing and the Alignment with Qualification Frameworks

The programme outcomes of the Bachelor's in Applied Computing at Schoolvision are based on the professional workflow of the digital domain and on the needs of the labour market for graduates who combine up-to-date technical capability with adaptable human skills. They are shaped by desktop research on industry needs, a benchmark of comparable programmes in the Netherlands, and the HBO-i framework. In our view, a bachelor's graduate in Applied Computing must be able to analyse a professional challenge, advise stakeholders, design and build a digital solution, manage the quality and progress of their own work, and communicate and collaborate responsibly with others in a changing professional context.

The curriculum therefore integrates technical development in one of three tracks (Applied Software Engineering, Applied Data Science and Applied Machine Learning) with an action inquiry line in which students learn to plan, act, observe and reflect on their own practice (self-inquiry), in dialogue with others (one-to-one inquiry) and within teams (team inquiry). Learning takes place in project-based, agile working structures such as squads, stand-ups and team coaching, and culminates in an individual graduation project in a professional context. All three tracks share the same six programme outcomes and are distinguished by their track competencies. The programme outcomes align with level 6 of the Dutch Qualifications Framework (NLQF), the first cycle of the Framework for Qualifications of the European Higher Education Area (QF-EHEA), the Dublin Descriptors and the HBO-i framework. Thus, the programme outcomes are identified as follows:

**1. Analyse**
Graduates analyse complex professional challenges and define requirements, constraints and directions for solution development.

**2. Advise**
Graduates provide substantiated advice to stakeholders, based on analysis and considering professional, organisational, social and ethical aspects.

**3. Design**
Graduates design fit-for-purpose digital solutions using appropriate architectures, methods and models.

**4. Build**
Graduates build and validate digital products through implementation, testing and improvement.

**5. Manage & Control**
Graduates manage their professional work, monitor quality and progress, and make responsible decisions in changing contexts.

**6. Communicate & Collaborate**
Graduates communicate effectively with stakeholders and collaborate responsibly towards shared goals.

## The four purposes of higher education

The Council of Europe describes four purposes of higher education. The programme outcomes serve them as follows.

| Purpose of higher education | Programme outcomes that serve it |
|---|---|
| Preparation for sustainable employment | Design: graduates design fit-for-purpose digital solutions using appropriate architectures, methods and models. Build: graduates build and validate digital products through implementation, testing and improvement. Advise: graduates provide substantiated advice to stakeholders. |
| Preparation for life as active citizens in democratic societies | Advise: graduates consider professional, organisational, social and ethical aspects in their advice. Manage & Control: graduates make responsible decisions in changing contexts. Communicate & Collaborate: graduates collaborate responsibly towards shared goals. |
| Personal development | Manage & Control: graduates manage their professional work and monitor quality and progress. Communicate & Collaborate: graduates communicate effectively with stakeholders. |
| Development and maintenance of a broad, advanced knowledge base | Analyse: graduates analyse complex professional challenges and define requirements, constraints and directions for solution development. Design: graduates use appropriate architectures, methods and models. Build: graduates implement, test and improve digital products. |
