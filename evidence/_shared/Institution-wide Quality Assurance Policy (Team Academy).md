**Policy for Quality Assurance at Team Academy**

**ESG 1.1. Policy for Quality Assurance**

*"Institutes should have a policy for quality assurance that is made public and forms part of their strategic management. Internal stakeholders should develop and implement this policy through appropriate structures and processes, while involving external stakeholders."*

This section aims to set the context for Quality Assurance at Team Academy and provide information on the institute's guiding principles for quality assurance, its organisational structure for quality assurance, the evaluation system of the quality assurance system and the multi-layered approach to quality assurance for its programmes. The institute's quality assurance policy complies with the requirements of the "European Standards and Guidelines for Quality Assurance in the European Higher Education Area" (ESG).

**1.1.1. Context and Objectives**

The vision of Team Academy is to bridge the skills gap that the world is currently facing. To address this challenge, Team Academy has the ambition to develop and offer master's degree programmes in the technology field. The programmes will aim to develop talent for the labour market. All staff and management teams are responsible for ensuring that the programmes meet the needs of the 21st century and the requirements of the job market that is constantly evolving. This is aimed to be achieved through an effective internal quality assurance system for the programmes. The quality assurance system ensures that the institute effectively and collaboratively develops and offers high-quality programmes, helping individuals develop a set of knowledge, skills and tools needed in the tech fields, contributing to their professional mastery. More specifically, through a quality assurance system, the institute ensures that:

- Quality culture is maintained and developed by all community members, including learners, staff and management;
- Relevant content, competencies and appropriate planning of the programmes facilitate each learner's learning experience and employability;
- The programmes are developed in connection with the needs of the global job market.
- The ongoing improvement and development in the programmes comply with international accreditation requirements.

Working on improving the quality of education is an ongoing focus in the programmes at Team Academy in line with the standards and guidelines for quality assurance in the European Higher Education Area (ESG). To ensure that improvement occurs in all aspects of the programmes, a set of principles and guidelines is outlined in this document.

**1.1.2. Concept of Quality**

Quality in education is often based on the extent to which processes and procedures are in place to establish an educational standard and constantly improve it. Quality also refers to a result of the interaction and relationship between all parties and all educational activities involving staff, external partners, learners, learning resources, learning environment and the support mechanisms.

**1.1.3. Guiding principles for quality assurance**

Quality assurance at Team Academy is supported by the following principles:

- **Adherence to common standards:** quality assurance is inspired and informed by the Standards and Guidelines for Quality Assurance in the European Higher Education Area (ESG).
- **Collaboration:** quality assurance is a collaborative effort between all community members at Team Academy and involves internal and external bodies.
- **Transparency:** policies, principles and guidelines are transparent to all community members.
- **Innovation:** Team Academy constantly explores new processes and tools to improve its educational standards, which encourages innovative approaches in its practices and quality assurance system.
- **Agility:** Teams at Team Academy are guided by an agile mindset that pushes them to respond to matters quickly. Constant iteration and improvement in educational practices is a core value.
- **Reflection**: plays a critical role in all the educational practices of the institute. This helps the institute maintain constant improvement in its policies and processes.

The quality assurance system set out for the professional master's programmes at Team Academy in this document is a collaborative effort, transparent to all community members and constantly improved by an innovative and agile mindset.

**1.1.4 Organisation of the Quality Assurance System**

Quality assurance at Team Academy aims to provide a clear organisational structure for all teams, faculty and learners. Figure 1 below provides the overall organisation consisting of the **institute's quality assurance** and the **programme quality assurance** systems.

**Figure 1. Organisation of Quality Assurance**

External players (Ministry of Education, Culture and Science; NVAO; Field Representatives; Knowledge Partners) → Internal players (Executive Board, Management Teams, Institute Quality Assurance, Exam Board, Managing Director, QA Team, Programme Teams (programme management, curriculum design & development), Educational Council, Admissions Teams, Faculty, Learners, Programme Quality Assurance).

**The Institute's quality assurance**

Team Academy is subject to external oversight by national and international quality assurance bodies. In the Dutch context, the Ministry of Education, Culture and Science (OCW) provides the regulatory framework for higher education institutions, while the Accreditation Organisation of the Netherlands and Flanders (NVAO) is the key body responsible for programme accreditation and institutional quality assurance. The institute's accreditation efforts are closely aligned with the expectations of these organisations, as well as international benchmarks. Team Academy is also actively engaged with global accreditation bodies to ensure that its programmes remain aligned with international standards and entrepreneurial education practices.

The Institute Quality Assurance function is embedded within the governance structure of Team Academy. At the institutional level, the Institute Board defines the strategic vision and educational direction of the organisation. The board works closely with management teams who are responsible for the implementation of that vision and for maintaining academic excellence across all programmes. The governance model adopted by Team Academy is collaborative and iterative. It encourages active input from faculty, staff, and learners.

To ensure transparency and alignment across organisational levels, Team Academy applies periodical performance reviews. This allows the institute to clearly define its institutional goals, track progress, and review outcomes periodically. Monthly check-in meetings between the Executive Board and programme teams create space for reflection, strategic adjustment, and progress tracking. This process ensures that the institute remains agile and responsive to developments in both the professional field and higher education.

**The programme quality assurance system**

The quality assurance at the programme level is informed by the institute's quality assurance policies and overall strategies. External players have a crucial role in maintaining the quality assurance for the institute's programmes. The relationships with the external players help the institute improve the connection between the academic profile of the programmes and the requirements of the sectors. The representatives from the field offer insights into the curriculum development. Global faculty help the institute develop fresh thinking at the intersection of leadership and emerging technologies. The content partners of the institute help the programmes' curriculum stay updated.

The internal organisation of the programmes' quality assurance involves the curriculum teams, management teams, admissions teams and faculty staff, as well as the learners. These players constantly interact with each other and inform each other. The main responsibility of the quality assurance is with the QA Team, composed of programme managers and led by a QA Lead. The QA team reports to the Executive Board.

**1.1.5 Evaluation of the Quality Assurance System**

The evaluation of the QA assurance system is designed to ensure that the quality assurance system complies with the institute's core principles, as well as the external stakeholders, constantly and iteratively improving itself and developing best practices and innovation in all aspects of the institute's academic endeavours.

The evaluation of the QA assurance system is an ongoing process at the institute. The institute's policies, procedures and guidelines are evaluated in collaboration with the executive board and other teams. The main responsibility for the evaluation of the QA assurance belongs to the QA Team. The executive board is responsible for the approval of the iterative improvements and changes regarding the QA system coordinated by the QA team.

All feedback, insights and observations from all external and internal players, including the learners, are incorporated into the reviews for the evaluation of the QA system.

**Review of this Policy document and stakeholder satisfaction with it (planned mechanism)**

In addition to the ongoing evaluation of the quality assurance *system* described above, this Policy for Quality Assurance document is itself subject to a planned review cycle, distinct from the programme/module/activity-level review cycles described in section 1.1.6. The QA Team is responsible for reviewing this document on an annual basis, ahead of the start of each academic year, and for proposing any revisions arising from regulatory changes (e.g. ESG, NVAO, WHW), from Field Representative or Educational Council input, or from experience applying the policy in practice. Any revision is subject to formal approval by the Executive Board, consistent with the approval authority already described above for changes to the QA system.

As this policy has not yet been applied to a running cohort, no stakeholder-satisfaction results specific to the policy document exist at this stage; this is stated plainly rather than implied otherwise. Once the cluster's programmes are operational, satisfaction with this policy document specifically (as distinct from programme-level learner satisfaction, which is already covered under ESG 1.7 Information management) will be assessed as part of the QA Team's annual review, drawing on feedback gathered from faculty, programme management, the Educational Council (student representatives) and Field Representatives during that review — the same stakeholder groups already named in sections 1.1.4 and 1.1.6 as participants in the institute's quality assurance system. This is presented here as a planned, ex-ante mechanism for an initial accreditation application, not as a historical record of reviews already conducted.

**Adherence to the Plan–Do–Check–Act (PDCA) Cycle**

Team Academy's quality assurance system is built upon the continuous improvement philosophy, which is operationalised through the **Plan–Do–Check–Act (PDCA) cycle.** This cycle provides a structured approach for maintaining and improving the quality of all educational activities, from programme design to learner assessment, and from institutional strategy to faculty development.

At the **Plan** stage, the curriculum team, with faculty in collaboration with field representatives, designs and develops the curriculum, defines strategic objectives, and aligns educational activities with learner needs and labour market demands. The **Do** phase focuses on the implementation of these plans through the delivery of learning activities, assessments and coaching. In the **Check** phase, internal and external feedback is gathered systematically through reflections, learner surveys, exam board reviews, and performance reviews. This phase ensures that progress is evaluated against key performance indicators and programme outcomes. Lastly, the **Act** phase uses these insights to inform targeted improvements. Adjustments are made at various levels, from module design to faculty practice, and formally approved through ongoing review processes by the QA team and executive board.

The PDCA cycle is embedded in both institutional and programme-level quality assurance and is applied across all layers of educational design and delivery — activity, module, certificate, and programme levels — to ensure responsiveness, agility, and excellence in line with the ESG framework. This cyclical approach allows Team Academy to remain dynamic, learner-centred, and closely aligned with the evolving expectations of the professional field.

**1.1.6. Multi-layered approach to quality assurance at the programme level**

In accordance with the PDCA cycle, the quality procedures seek the views and feedback of all community members who take part in and benefit from the academic offerings of Team Academy. We follow a certain set of steps and processes to be able to ensure an increased academic quality of the programmes. These take place at different levels internally:

**Activity level**

Activities refer to the smallest learning activities that the learners are engaged with during the programme at Team Academy. This level involves maintaining and improving the quality standards of the weekly workshops, learning resources, assignments, projects, team coaching experience or supervision services. The quality of the learning activities is maintained through:

- Designing and planning of the learning activities by the curriculum and programme management teams, as well as field experts, based on a set of design principles;
- Gathering continuous feedback from learners through reflections or surveys;
- Acting on the feedback immediately to improve the learning experience.

**Module level**

Modules refer to the four or five weeks of learning episodes in the programmes that focus on a set of competencies and learning outcomes, and that revolve around a common theme or topic.

- Designing and planning of the modules in line with the programme's objectives and the competencies;
- Gathering learner feedback at the end of each module.
- Acting on the learner feedback to improve the module experience.

**Certificate level**

Certificates refer to the larger components in the programme consisting of several modules. The quality of the certificates is typically maintained through:

- Designing and planning of the certificates by the curriculum and programme management teams, as well as the field experts and external advisors, based on the market needs;
- Evaluating each learner's learning mastery levels in each target competency at the Certificate's VIVA and receiving feedback from the learners;
- Improving the alignment between the programme's academic profile and the market needs based on the learners' competency development and their feedback.

**Programme level**

At the programme level, the institute's main objective is to ensure the intended objectives of the programme are fulfilled. To measure this and maintain the desired academic profile of the programme, three main success metrics are taken into account:

- The extent to which the programme helps learners achieve the desired competency development.
- The quality of the final project is the most important output of a graduate.
- The employability status of the learners upon completion of the programme.

A typical cycle of improvement applies to the programme as well:

- Designing and planning of the programmes in collaboration with field experts based on the market needs
- Evaluating each learner's learning mastery levels in each target competency through their final project, and their employability status at the end of the programme
- Improving the alignment between the programme's academic profile and the market needs based on the success metrics

**ESG 1.2. Design and approval of programmes**

*"Institutes should have formal mechanisms for the approval, periodic review and monitoring of their programmes and awards."*

Design and approval policies prescribe how programmes are designed, structured and approved, as well as how certain improvements are made at the programme level. At Team Academy, the **Curriculum Team** is responsible for designing and developing programmes in collaboration with field representatives. The **Executive Board** is responsible for the formal approval of programmes and major programme changes. For the design and approval of the programmes, the Curriculum Team and faculty follow internal procedures established for programme design and programme approval.

The programmes at Team Academy go through constant improvements. The curriculum team is responsible for reviewing and improving the curriculum in collaboration with faculty members, students and field representatives. For conducting the improvements, the curriculum team will follow specific guidelines for monitoring, reviewing and improving the programmes.

**ESG 1.3. Student-centred learning, teaching and assessment**

*"Institutes should ensure that the programmes are delivered in a way that encourages students to take an active role in creating the learning process, and that the assessment of students reflects this approach."*

Team Academy ensures that the learning experience in all programmes encourages learners to take an active role and take responsibility for their own learning as well as for the learning of other community members. To this end, the curriculum team ensures that the faculty members will follow the guidelines for creating a learner-centred programme experience. Teaching and examination rules and procedures are aligned with the competency-based education standards. All faculty must follow the rules and procedures prescribed in the *Teaching and Examination Regulations* document.

The curriculum team and the programme management team must ensure that:

- All programmes' curriculum structure and assessment strategies are outlined in the educational handbooks and made available to all learners.
- All learners are aware of their learner responsibilities to themselves and the community in the educational handbooks.
- Procedures for extensions for assignments and appeals to academic decisions are defined and made available to all learners in the educational handbooks.

**ESG 1.4. Student admission, progression, recognition and certification**

*"Institutes should consistently apply pre-defined and published regulations covering all phases of the student's life cycle", e.g. student admission, progression, recognition and certification."*

This section aims to provide clear admission, progression and recognition policy and guidelines which are applied consistently and fairly and transparently.

**Student admission**

All applicants expressing an interest in a programme are dealt with professionally and given information and guidance on what will suit them personally and professionally. All applicants from diverse backgrounds, cultures, genders and nationalities, regardless of their physical disability, are encouraged to apply to Team Academy programmes. The admissions consultants team is responsible for assessing an applicant's suitability for the programmes and for informing the programme management of onboarding. The admissions team will follow the procedures and guidelines for the admissions process.

**Learner progression**

Programmes at Team Academy place a considerable emphasis on helping each learner's academic achievement and raising educational standards. The learner progression policy is based on an understanding that each learner should reach and even surpass the intended mastery levels. The institute believes that support and intervention may be required at different stages in a learner's academic journey, and the institute is willing to provide it through certain mechanisms. Programme management is responsible for making all the necessary programme information available to all community members, including learners. The professional master's programme will follow the guidelines to facilitate and support learners' academic journey.

**Recognition and certification**

Team Academy holds the status of a *Recognised Legal Entity for Higher Education* (RPHO) in the Netherlands, which authorises it to offer accredited higher education programmes under the Dutch Higher Education and Research Act (WHW). All programmes must ensure consistency in the standards applying to the certificate and diploma that the institute issues. Specific guidelines for recognition and certification must be followed by the programme management teams.

**ESG 1.5. Teaching staff**

*"Institutes should assure themselves of the competence of their teachers. They should apply fair and transparent processes for the recruitment and development of the staff."*

The institute ensures that fair and transparent processes for the recruitment, onboarding and development of the staff are in place. The programme management teams are responsible for the selection and development of the teaching staff, and must follow the guidelines for recruitment and professional development of the faculty staff.

**ESG 1.6. Learning resources and learner support**

*"Institutes should have appropriate funding for learning and teaching activities and ensure that adequate and readily accessible learning resources and student support are provided."*

The institute is responsible for providing appropriate learning resources, a learning environment and learner support in all programmes. The programme management teams must ensure that available resources and support generate high-quality teaching and learning results in order to achieve the programme's intended graduate profile.

**Learning Resources and Communication**

The programmes at Team Academy respect the needs of a diverse learner population (such as part-time or full-time employed students). Therefore, the learning experience allows for sufficient flexibility for learners to adjust their studies based on their existing schedules. The programme management teams and faculty must ensure that learning resources and communication tools comply with the guidelines for learning resources and communication.

**Learner Support**

Each programme is assigned a programme manager to coordinate and facilitate interaction between faculty and learners to address issues. Programme managers regularly hold Town Halls to meet with learners and faculty for general announcements or general inquiries with the community. Each learner must have access to a learner support mechanism via team coaching and supervision. They must also engage with peer-learning and peer-feedback in the learning and assessment process.

The programme managers are responsible for monitoring and checking the progress of the learners and ensuring they are provided with further guidance and support where necessary. Team coaches and supervisors must follow the guidelines provided by the programme management as well.

**ESG 1.7 Information management**

*"Institutes should ensure that they collect, analyse and use relevant information for the effective management of their programmes and other activities."*

For effective management of all programmes and other activities, the institute management uses the performance review system to define and track its organisational goals across different teams. Information and data regarding the institute's business objectives are kept and monitored digitally regularly.

Programme management teams are responsible for keeping and providing the data, such as learner progress, learner mastery, assessment results or drop-out rates. All learner data will be kept on the institute's learning management system and exported by the programme managers where necessary. All survey results and learner reflections will be securely stored on the institute's learning management system. All other data regarding the programme and all activities will be kept in the institute's internal documents. The data includes:

- Student population/profiles
- Completion/drop-out rates
- Number of degrees
- Number of students placed in jobs
- Number of learner workload
- Learning mastery scores
- Overall certificate scores
- Qualitative or quantitative data on learner satisfaction
- Ratio between learners and academic staff

**ESG 1.8. Public information**

*"Institutes should publish information about their activities, including programmes, which is clear, accurate, objective, up-to-date and readily accessible."*

The publicly published information is helpful for the prospective and current Team Academy students, graduates, stakeholders and the public. While publication of its information is an ongoing work for the institute, the marketing team must ensure that the institute publishes the information on its website regarding the following:

- The programme details, including the objectives, the curriculum, target competencies, the credits and the faculty;
- The programme's admission criteria and the admission steps;
- The learning approach in the programmes.
- The programme brochures upon request by the applicants.
- The online application forms;

**Publication status of this Policy document.** The items listed immediately above concern published *programme*-level information. This Policy for Quality Assurance document itself is, at the current stage, an internal governance document — shared with staff, faculty, the Educational Council (student representatives) and Field Representatives, but not yet published on the institute's public website. This reflects the fact that the three programmes covered by this application have not yet launched. Team Academy's plan is to publish this policy document publicly once the cluster is operational, alongside the programme-level information already listed above. This is stated here plainly, as the honest, ex-ante answer, rather than as a claim of an existing public presence.

**ESG 1.9 On-going monitoring and periodic review of programmes**

*"Institutes should monitor and periodically review their programmes to ensure that they achieve the objectives set for them and respond to the needs of students and society. These reviews should lead to continuous improvement of the programme. Any action planned or taken as a result should be communicated to all those concerned."*

The institute constantly monitors and reviews its programmes for an increased academic quality. This is mainly to ensure that the programmes achieve their intended learning outcomes and graduate profile, as well as to improve the connection between the programme and the job market as well as society. The curriculum team, the programme management team and faculty must ensure that ongoing monitoring and review of the programmes follow the guidelines set out for the process.

**ESG 1.10 Cyclical external quality assurance**

*"Institutes should undergo external quality assurance in line with the ESG on a cyclical basis."*

Team Academy puts a deliberate effort into raising its academic standards through programme accreditations such as NVAO. As part of the external quality assurance, the institute is formally and informally in close contact with field experts and employers whose market insights help improve the quality of the programmes.
