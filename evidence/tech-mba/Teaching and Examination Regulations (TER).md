# Teaching and Examination Regulations

## Tech MBA

*(Adapted from Team Academy's Master's-level Teaching and Examination Regulations — `/evidence/professional-masters-applied-ai/appendices/Appendix 19 - Teaching and Examinations Regulations.md` — per Didem's confirmation that Tech MBA shares the Master's regulatory framework rather than operating under a materially different assessment/examination model. This document reproduces the shared institutional rules and adapts the programme-structure articles to Tech MBA's actual, on-file programme facts (source: `Tech MBA Programme Handbook_2024.md`, `Tech MBA Supervision Guide.md`). Sections describing Applied-AI-specific curriculum content (module list, proficiency-test subject matter) are not carried over; Tech MBA's own curriculum documents already on file — `Tech MBA Curriculum v10 (CURRENT).md` and the Programme Handbook — remain the authoritative source for module-level content and are cross-referenced rather than duplicated here.)*

## Section 1: General Provisions

### Article 1.1 Applicability of the Regulations

1. These regulations apply to the teaching and examinations for the Tech MBA at Team Academy / Amsterdam Tech.
2. The Programme Management ensures that the guidelines prescribed in this document are followed by the faculty.
3. As with the Professional Master's in Applied AI, these regulations are defined by the Executive Board and implemented by the programme management and faculty; the Examination Board safeguards the quality of the examinations and advises on the teaching and examination regulations where necessary.
4. The terms defined in this TER also apply to the Tech MBA's Assessment Policy and to the Rules and Guidelines of the Examination Board. If a provision in this TER conflicts with those documents, this TER prevails.

### Article 1.2 Definitions of Terms Used

Definitions follow the institution-wide terms already defined for Team Academy's Master's-level programmes (academic year, assessor, admissions consultant, certification, cohort, competency-based, diploma, ECTS, facilitator, learner, LMS, module, programme, supervisor, workshop — see Appendix 19/Appendix 12 for full definitions), with the following Tech MBA-specific terms:

- **Programme Manager**: the Tech MBA's programme manager, responsible for day-to-day coordination and the primary point of contact for learners from enrolment to graduation.
- **Internal Examiner / External Examiner**: the assessors appointed to evaluate the Final Project, per the Tech MBA Supervision Guide — two Internal Examiners review the submission; where they do not agree on Pass/Fail, an External Examiner is appointed to make the deciding assessment.

## Section 2: Programme Structure

### Article 2.1 Structure of the Academic Year and Programme

1. The Tech MBA is a fully online degree programme, designed to be completed in approximately 20 months, leading to a professional master's degree.
2. The programme carries a total study load of 1680 hours, equivalent to 60 ECTS, in line with the ECTS standard applied across Team Academy programmes.
3. The programme consists of four certifications plus a Final Project, each with its own workload and credit allocation, taken consecutively:

| Certification | Total workload | Credit points |
|---|---|---|
| Transform your Business | 392 hours | 14 ECTS |
| Lead Your Business | 280 hours | 10 ECTS |
| Innovate and Grow Your Business | 224 hours | 8 ECTS |
| Leadership and Action Research | 224 hours | 8 ECTS |
| Final Project | 560 hours | 20 ECTS |
| **Total** | **1680 hours** | **60 ECTS** |

4. On average, learners are advised to dedicate approximately 20 hours per week to their studies, with additional time for module projects.
5. The university's learning management system (LMS) and communication platforms are used for all digital components, consistent with the online delivery mode.
6. For intended programme competencies, outcomes and module-level content, refer to the Tech MBA Curriculum and Programme Handbook.

### Article 2.2 Organisation of the Programme

1. The Tech MBA is coordinated by a Programme Manager, who is the primary point of contact for learners from enrolment to graduation, in the same operational role the Programme Manager fulfils for Team Academy's other programmes.
2. Assessment throughout the programme is weighted between module projects and the Final Project, as detailed in the Assessment Policy (Tech MBA).

### Article 2.3 Application and Selection Process

The Tech MBA follows Team Academy's institution-wide admissions approach (online application requesting diplomas/certifications/CVs, an intake interview assessing motivation and readiness, one intake period per academic year). Programme-specific entry requirements and the detailed step-by-step process are maintained in the Tech MBA's own admissions materials; this TER defers to those documents for procedural detail not yet consolidated here, and to the Examination Board for any appeal regarding the admissions decision, consistent with Article 2.3 of the Master's TER.

### Article 2.4 Learning as a Personal Accountability

Learners are expected to actively participate in all required learning activities (workshops, coaching, module projects). Where a learner cannot meet these expectations, the same institution-wide accountability and escalation procedure described in the Master's TER (Article 2.4) applies: the Programme Manager addresses concerns with the learner in the first instance, and any decision to exclude or transfer a learner can be objected to via written objection to the Chair of the Examination Board.

### Article 2.5 Certificate and Degree

1. Learners receive a certificate upon successful completion of each of the four certifications.
2. Learners who successfully complete all four certifications and the Final Project, including all required assessment components, are awarded the Tech MBA degree/diploma.
3. Before the Examination Board approves a certificate or diploma, the Programme Manager verifies that the learner has fulfilled all academic and administrative requirements (grades, participation, tuition fee payment), consistent with the institution-wide procedure.

### Article 2.6 Termination of Studies and Re-registration

A learner's enrolment may be terminated in accordance with the Dutch Higher Education and Research Act (WHW), including Articles 7.42, 7.32 and 7.33, following the same institution-wide provisions as the Master's programme (Article 2.7 of the Master's TER).

## Section 3: Assessment of Learning

### Article 3.1 Types of Assessment

1. The Tech MBA's summative assessment system is set out in four parts (the four certifications), each consisting of interim assessments that are weighted and ultimately result in a final qualification, based on a numeric evaluation score (out of 100) and a caesura of 60 to pass each certification.
2. Module projects form a substantial part of assessment and are evaluated by faculty members, who provide expert feedback.
3. The Final Project comprises a work-based project plan, a written report, a video presentation and a live Q&A session, assessed by a panel of Internal Examiners (with an External Examiner where the Internal Examiners do not agree on Pass/Fail) — consistent with the institution-wide principle of a multi-assessor high-stake decision applied in the Master's programme.
4. Learners must achieve at least an average of 60 across the preceding certifications to be permitted to proceed to the Final Project module.

### Article 3.2 Assessments and Deadlines, Extensions

The Tech MBA follows the same institution-wide extension framework as the Master's programme (Article 3.2/3.2.1 of the Master's TER): learners may request an extension using the standard extension form before the relevant deadline; decision authority for lower/medium-stake requests rests with the Programme Manager, and high-stake/Final Project extension requests are decided by the Examination Board.

### Article 3.3 Determining and Announcement of Results

Results are determined following the institution-wide procedure: the assessor/examiner determines the result, the Programme Manager performs a quality check, and results are communicated to the learner via the LMS or the applicable formal channel. For the Final Project specifically, the first formal grading follows submission review by two Internal Examiners; where they disagree, an External Examiner is appointed within two weeks to make the deciding review.

### Article 3.4 The Right to Appeal Assessment Results and Decisions

Learners have the right to request an inspection/review of their assessed work and, on the institution-wide grounds described in the Master's TER (Article 3.8) — mitigating circumstances, procedural irregularity, or evidence of bias — may appeal to the Examination Board. Appeals that simply contest academic judgement are not permitted. Final recourse is available through the independent NRTO disputes and appeals procedure, consistent with every other Team Academy programme.

### Article 3.5 Inclusive Assessment and Examination Provisions

The Tech MBA applies the same institution-wide inclusive-assessment provisions as the Master's programme (Article 3.9 of the Master's TER): learners with disabilities, chronic illness, neurodiversity or other special personal circumstances may apply to the Programme Manager for reasonable adjustments (extended time, alternative examination formats, assistive technology, adapted assessment forms), decided by the Examination Board and communicated in writing within the institution-wide timeframe.

### Article 3.6 Academic Integrity

Academic integrity for the Tech MBA follows the same institution-wide definition and procedure as the Master's programme (Article 3.11 of the Master's TER): plagiarism and academic misconduct — including undisclosed or unreferenced use of AI tools — are reported by the assessor/supervisor to the Programme Manager, who informs the learner and refers the matter to the Examination Board for a final decision, with the right of appeal to the NRTO's Board of Appeals. This closes the gap the evidence map identified: prior to this adaptation, the Tech MBA's only reference to academic integrity was a single passing mention in the Supervision Guide regarding project ownership, with no formal misconduct procedure behind it — this TER now supplies that procedure by adopting the institution-wide one.

### Article 3.7 Exemptions through Recognition of Prior Learning and Force Majeure

The Tech MBA follows the same institution-wide RPL and force majeure provisions described in the Master's TER (Articles 3.12–3.13), applied by the Examination Board on request.

## Section 4: Learner Support and Guidance

Learners receive structured support consistent with the Tech MBA Supervision Guide — including a Programme Manager as primary contact and supervisors for the Final Project phase — following the same institution-wide model of learner support described for Team Academy's other programmes (team coaching / supervision, with the Examination Board remaining independent of learner-support delivery).

## Section 5: Evaluation of the Programme

The Tech MBA's learning experience is evaluated in accordance with Team Academy's institution-wide quality assurance framework (activity, module, certificate/certification and programme levels) and its Plan–Do–Check–Act cycle. Further details are in the Policy for Quality Assurance (shared, `/evidence/_shared/Institution-wide Quality Assurance Policy (Team Academy).md`).

## Section 6: Tasks and Responsibilities of the Examination Board

The Examination Board for the Tech MBA carries out the same institution-wide statutory tasks (WHW Article 7.12) as for Team Academy's other programmes: determining whether a learner meets the requirements set out in this TER; assuring the quality of exams and final assessments; granting exemptions; deciding on deviation requests; handling fraud, plagiarism and other academic misconduct; and reporting annually on its activities. For the Tech MBA's Final Project specifically, the Examination Board's quality-assurance role is exercised through the Internal/External Examiner review process described in Article 3.3.

## Section 7: Hardship Clause

If demonstrable unreasonableness or unfairness of a predominant nature occurs, the Executive Board or the Examination Board may allow deviation from the provisions of this TER, consistent with the institution-wide hardship clause.
