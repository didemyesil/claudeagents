# **Section 1: General Provisions** 

  

Teaching and Examination Regulations (TER) are defined by the Executive Board and implemented by the programme management and the programme faculty. The Examination Board safeguards the quality of the examinations and advises on the teaching and examination regulations where necessary. This TER contains information about your Master’s education and assessment here at Team Academy. It describes the rights and obligations that apply to you. Therefore, besides the learner and programme handbook, always read the TER if you want information on your rights and obligations concerning education and assessment. If the handbooks conflict with the TER on any point, the TER takes precedence\!

  

The terms defined in the Teaching and Examinations Regulations (TER) for the relevant programme shall also apply to the Assessment Policy and the Rules and Guidelines of the Examination Board. If a provision in the TER for the relevant programme conflicts with the above documents, the first shall prevail.

  
  

## **Article 1.1 Applicability of the Regulations** 

1\. These regulations apply to the teaching and examinations for the professional Master's at Team Academy. 

2\.  These regulations apply to anyone who first enrolled in a Master’s degree at Team Academy for the academic year 2025-2026.

3\. The Programme Management ensures that the guidelines prescribed in this document are followed by the faculty.

4\. The 2025-2026 TER takes effect on 1 November 2025 and remains valid until 31 August 2027.

  
  

## **Article 1.2 Definitions of Terms Used**

1.  academic year: the period between the start date (November) and the end date of the programme year;
2.  assessor: a faculty member, appointed by the exam board, with demonstrable expertise in the subject area or professional field, who, either appointed as assessor or under the responsibility of an appointed assessor, is involved in the assessment of examinations.
3.  admissions consultant: an  admission professional who assesses, on behalf of the faculty, whether a candidate meets the requirements for admission to the Master's degree programme of his/her choice;
4.  certification: the formal recognition that a learner has completed a series of modules meeting the required learning outcomes and assessment criteria;
5.  coach: the faculty staff member who provides individual or team coaching to the learner;
6.  cohort: the group of learners who have registered for a degree programme for the first time in a given academic year. There is one intake per year.
7.  competency-based: referring to the type of learning, assessment and academic reporting that are based on learners demonstrating that they have learned the knowledge and skills they are expected to learn as they progress through the programme;
8.  diploma: the official document awarded upon the successful completion of a degree programme, which signifies that the learner has met all academic requirements.
9.  ECTS (European Credit Transfer and Accumulation System): a credit with a workload of 28 hours of study;
10. facilitator: the faculty staff member who facilitates learning in workshops every week;
11. learner: the student who has been admitted to the programme;
12. LMS:  learning management system
13. module: a self-contained unit of study within a programme that covers specific learning objectives, competencies, and assessments, contributing to the overall qualification;
14. programme: the totality and cohesion of the course components, learning activities and methods, contact hours (e.g workshops), assessment and recommended learning resources;
15. supervisor: the faculty member who provides supervision during the final project phase in the programme;
16. workshop: an interactive and hands-on learning activity facilitated by a faculty staff member.

  
  

# **Section 2: Programme Structure**

  

## **Article 2.1  Structure of the Academic Year** 

1\.  The programmes are offered in consecutive phases, which are made up of modules and certifications.

2\. The professional master's in Applied AI programme is available in two modes of study: full-time (on-campus) and part-time (online). Both modes follow the same curriculum structure, intended learning outcomes, assessments, and quality standards. The sequencing of modules is the same, but the workload distribution differs proportionally according to the mode of study.

3\. The full-time programme carries a study load of 60 EC per academic year, equivalent to 1680 hours (60 × 28 hours), in accordance with the ECTS standard and the requirements of the IND for students holding a residence permit for study.

4\. The part-time programme distributes the same total number of ECs over a longer period and is not eligible for IND study-permit requirements.  
  

  

## **Article 2.2  Organisation of the programme**

1.  The weekly workload for the full-time master’s programme (including study and contact hours) is approximately 40 hours/week in the full-time, 20 hours/ week in the part-time, on average, depending on the module and the pace of the learner. This corresponds to a total study load of 1680 hours (60 EC) per academic year.
2.  The weekly workload for the part-time programme is approximately 20 hours per week, with the same total EC obtained over an extended study duration.
3.  The university’s learning management system (LMS) and communication platforms are used for all digital components.
4.  For intended programme competencies, outcomes and objectives, please refer to Appendix 1.

  

## **Article 2.3  Application & Selection Process (see Appendix 2 for more detailed information)**

1.  The programme requires an online application, which asks for diplomas (s), certification(s), and CVs.
2.  An intake interview conducted by the admissions officer is part of the application process. This interview serves to assess the candidate's general motivation as well as their technical skills. 
3.  The formal language of the programme is English. English proficiency at the C1 level is required to be able to complete the programme.
4.  Candidates enrol in a structured foundations module (the preparatory module “0”) tailored to ensure readiness for the professional master's degree. After successful completion, candidates transition into the main programme. 
5.  If the candidate wishes to waive this foundations module, they can take a proficiency test before their enrollment, which has two components: (1) an online test that covers questions on basic Python programming, basic mathematics/statistics and leadership awareness. (2) a follow-up call by key faculty to review and validate the assessment results. 
6.  For those who take the test, key faculty make a final decision based on the candidate’s cumulative performance and approve either direct admission to the main programme or conditional admission (which means the candidate still needs to enrol in the preparatory module “0”). 
7.  As with any decision that is related to a test, assignment or assessment, learners can appeal any decision regarding the test, assignment or assessment to the exam board. For this, they need to address a request stating their concern/complaint to the exam board and submit it to the programme manager, who will take note of the request and immediately forward it to the chair of the exam board. A decision will normally follow within four weeks upon submission of the request.
8.  Students can join the cohort until the end of week 1 of the first module at the latest. Later applicants can apply to the exam board for an exception through the programme manager. They still need to first complete the online application, which asks for diploma(s), certification(s) and CVs, and conduct an intake interview with the admissions officer. The results of these will be taken into consideration by the exam board. The content of a module “0” (consisting of online required study/reading material) can be individually assigned to late starters. 
9.  Specific entry requirements for the programme are in the appendix.
10. During the academic year, there is one intake period for the programmes, which all start in November.

  

## **Article 2.4  Learning as a Personal Accountability**

1.  The programmes at Team Academy thrive on learner interactions and a community of people supporting each other and contributing to each other’s progress. Therefore, accountability for their own and others’ learning is key.
2.  Learners are expected to participate in all scheduled learning activities, including workshops, coaching meetings, and VIVA sessions.
3.  If a learner cannot attend a scheduled activity, they must notify the programme manager in advance whenever reasonably possible. If advance notice is not feasible due to unforeseen circumstances such as sudden illness, personal emergencies, or unexpected delays (e.g. traffic issues) the learner must notify the programme manager as soon as possible.
4.  When a learner misses a session, they must provide learning evidence that demonstrates achievement of the learning outcome(s) related to the missed activity.
5.  Time management is considered an important part of a professional Master's learner's competencies. Learners are expected to hand in their assignments before a clearly set time window. If they need more time, they can apply to the exam board for an extension (for more information, see article 3.2.1), in which they need to explain the reasons for the delay. The exam board has partly delegated the decision-making power on extension requests to the programme manager. If you wish for an extension request, please follow this [link.](https://elu2.typeform.com/to/qW3tjtes) 
6.  A professional Master's learner is expected to recognise the importance of active participation (personally attending workshops, coaching or VIVA sessions, if online; having a camera and microphone turned on throughout the session, actively contributing to the quality of the discussion in the sessions and in retrospect reflecting on the content of the session in writing when required).
7.  In the event of inadequate participation or lack of accountability observed by faculty or programme management, the university may decide to exclude the learner from further participation in the degree programme and/or transfer his/her registration to a future cohort. 
8.  If the learner in question decides to object to this decision, the programme manager needs to send a copy of the formal (written) objection to the Chair of the Examination Board, together with an overview of the facts that led to the decision to exclude the learner from the degree programme in the first place.

#   **Section 3: Assessment of Learning** 

  

## **Article 3.1  Types of Assessment**

1.  Assessment in the Master’s programme follows a programmatic and competency-based approach. Learners build a portfolio of diverse evidence throughout the programme. Evidence includes, but is not limited to, assignments, projects, reflections, presentations, and workshop artefacts (see the Assessment Policy for more information). Every certificate phase is concluded with an assessment that is categorised as low (which does not count toward the mastery scores), medium, or high stake, and distributed across the learning journey to support continuous development. 
2.  A full overview of all assessments can be found in Appendix 3, the programme of assessments. Any deviations from this programme of assessments during the academic year need to be approved by the exam board. 
3.  The Examination Board safeguards the quality of assessments, ensuring that the final judgment regarding learners is made with due care and fairness.
4.  Each assessment item is evaluated using standardised rubrics, available to learners in the module descriptions and the Learning Management System (LMS). Medium-stake submissions are assessed by an exam board-qualified assessor. High-stake assessments, such as portfolio evaluations at the end of a certification and VIVA sessions, are assessed by (at least) three qualified assessors. Students are awarded the ECs for the certificate only after the Examination Board confirms the final result based on the VIVA evaluation.
5.  Assessment results and feedback are communicated via the LMS.
6.  Feedback is an essential part of the assessment system and is structured to support learning through **feed-up** - clarifying the standards learners are expected to meet; **feedback** - identifying current performance and strengths; and **feedforward** - outlining concrete next steps for further development.
7.  Learners are supported by a team coach during the modules and a supervisor during the final project phase to guide their academic and professional growth.

  

## **Article 3.2  Assessments and Deadlines**

1.  Assessors, appointed by the Examination Board, are responsible for assessing the learner’s work throughout the programme, in alignment with the programme’s competency framework and rubric-based criteria.
2.  High-stake progression decisions, such as portfolio reviews at the end of a certification and VIVA sessions, are made by a panel of qualified assessors, based on the review of curated learning evidence and supported by rubrics.
3.  Assignments are visible on the LMS for that specific module and always include a submission deadline.
4.  Assignments submitted after the original deadline are accepted only if a formal extension request has been submitted and approved in advance. In such cases, the assessor will assess the work within 10 calendar days of submission. The procedure for an extension request is described in Article 3.2.1.
5.  Extension requests of low and medium level stake assessments should be submitted to the programme manager, who will seek approval at the exam board, while those of the high stake assessment should be requested at and approved by the exam board.
6.  If a learner misses a deadline without an approved extension, the corresponding learning activity may not be counted as valid evidence in the portfolio. If this leads to insufficient data to demonstrate the core competencies at a decision point, the learner may not progress until the gap is resolved in accordance with the feedback and improvement process.

  

### **Article 3.2.1  Extension**

1.  A learner may address a request for a deadline extension to the examination board (who have delegated the primary decision authority for lower and medium stake assessments in this matter to the programme manager).

<!-- end list -->

1.  A learner may request a deadline extension for assignments or final project-related submissions. At least four days before the deadline of an assignment expires, the learner fills out a standard extension form stating a valid reason. Either the programme manager or the exam board will communicate their decision within four days. Requests for extension that are sent in too late are not accepted.
2.  In case the programme manager finds that a learner submits an unusually high number of extension requests, the programme manager informs the exam board, who will advise on any action that may need to be taken. 
3.  For Final Project report submissions, if learners cannot meet the given deadline (the minimum amount of study), the learner should request an extension for a maximum of six months by filling out a standard extension form and submitting this to the programme manager. Final project extension requests should be submitted at least 2 weeks before the deadline expires. Failure to submit the Final Project Report or any required deliverables by the deadline without an approved extension will result in an automatic "Fail" for that stage of the project.

  

## **Article 3.3  Determining and Announcements of Results**

1.  Assessors are expected to review and record assessment evidence (e.g. formative feedback or summative decision for medium-stake assessment) within **10 working days** after submission. If this deadline cannot be met due to exceptional circumstances, the assessor must inform programme management so that an alternate assessor can be appointed to maintain continuity.
2.  For medium and high-stake assessments, learners receive feedback structured as feed-up, feedback, and feedforward, addressing the intended standard, current performance, and recommendations for further development. High stake assessments result in a panel decision, as further explained in article 3.4 (conditional progression, progression or no progression).
3.  Final project assessments are conducted by a panel of at least three assessors (two faculty and one independent/external assessor) with distinct but complementary perspectives: assessor 1 focuses on action research methodology and process, while assessor 2 evaluates the domain-specific dimensions (e.g. artificial intelligence).
4.  If consensus between the internal assessors is not reached, the external assessor provides a deciding evaluation based on established assessment criteria.
5.  Upon sufficient demonstration of competence in the project portfolio, the learner proceeds to a final presentation and Q\&A session (VIVA). The panel’s decision on progression or graduation is based on a criterion-based, holistic review of the portfolio and the learner’s performance during the VIVA.

## **Article 3.4  Progression and Certification Principles**

1.  Decision Panel Composition & Voting: Progression and graduation decisions are made by a panel of at least three assessors. 
2.  Portfolio Coverage: Each certification designates certain learning outcomes (LOs) as data points. These must be demonstrated within the respective certification for a learner to pass directly. 
3.  Conditional Progression:  
    \- If one LO shows insufficient data to support the required level of competency, the learner is granted conditional progression. The learner is then given a defined period by the panel to revise and/or resubmit alternative learning evidence for that LO.  
    \- If two or more LOs show insufficient data, the learner may still progress conditionally, provided they commit to improving the relevant LOs in the next certification cycle. 

The learner can only be awarded a conditional progression upon discussing their ideas for gaining and showcasing learning evidence with the panel, who then decide on a conditional progression. The programme manager then sends a formal conditional approval for progression to the learner.    

1.  Progression: Learners progress when all the certification’s LOs are substantiated with data points that demonstrate the required level of competency as determined by the panel using standardised rubrics.
2.  No Progression: If, according to the panel, a learner displays insufficient development across multiple learning outcomes and realistic progression cannot be justified by the learner, they will not be permitted to continue to the next certification. The learner will be informed in writing of this negative progress decision and the possibility of rejoining the next cohort at the point where they leave off. They can appeal this decision to the exam board within a period of four weeks upon formally receiving the no-progress decision from the programme manager.   
3.  Program-Wide Rule: By the end of the programme, every learning outcome must be passed. Learners who do not meet this requirement will not be eligible to receive the diploma.

## **Article 3.5  Improvement, Resubmission, and Reconsideration**

1.  Learners are encouraged to improve and revise their submitted work throughout the programme. This includes updating artefacts in their portfolio as they receive feedback or develop stronger competencies.
2.  If a learner does not progress at a certification decision point, there is either the possibility of a conditional progress or a no-progress decision as mentioned in article 3.4.
3.  In case of conditional progression, the panel will define a clear revision window during which the learner can submit additional or improved evidence for one or more core learning outcomes.
4.  There is a standard resubmission option for every assessment so that learners can show an improvement in their Professional Mastery level based on the original feedback. This option is mentioned in the LMS assignment instructions, and when the learner receives their original feedback in the LMS.
5.  The standard resubmission is bound to a deadline of 14 working days upon receiving the original feedback on LMS. As with the original grade/feedback, the assessor will publish feedback/grades on LMS within 10 working days upon assignment resubmission.
6.  Resubmissions are only accepted for previously submitted work. If no submission was made before the original deadline as mentioned in the assignment description on LMS, the original opportunity is considered forfeited (unless an extension was officially granted).
7.  In this system of resubmission (points 4, 5 and 6), learners can revise each data point once per certification cycle. They can choose to do this by resubmission of the original assignment, but they are also allowed to propose an alternative way to achieve the data point in question with the assessor in question. If the assessor agrees, they need to submit their learning evidence within the time frame agreed with the assessor. 
8.  Time management and professional responsibility are assessed through competency-based indicators. Evidence of timeliness may influence progression discussions and should be documented by faculty.

  

## **Article 3.6  Validity Period of Results**

1\. Assessment results that count towards a master’s degree remain valid for an unlimited amount of time, except in cases of temporary withdrawal exceeding five years, as per the Failure and Second Chance Policy.

2\. A mark or exemption remains valid indefinitely, unless the knowledge, insight or skills tested are out of date compared to the current course. However, the minimum validity period of a completed course is always six years from the granting of the exemption or mark. A mark cannot expire earlier, not even if the knowledge, insight or skills have become outdated.

  

## **Article 3.7  Discussing the Feedback & Assessment Results**

1.  After the feedback/result of any assignment has been shared, the learner can arrange a call with the assessor to discuss the results or to receive more feedback on his/her work if the written feedback has been found insufficient. 

## **Article 3.8  The Right to Appeal Assessment Results and Decisions**  

1.  For a period of 14 working days after notification of the results of any assessment, the learner has the right to inspect his/her evaluated work on request. Information on academic appeals is provided in the Educational Handbook.
2.  The programme management officially (in writing) gives permission for inspection upon receiving the learner's written request.
3.  A learner cannot appeal an assessment result with the exam board if their results are not as good as they had hoped for or worse than they believe they deserve. However, appeals are possible if, for example, there were mitigating circumstances materially affecting the learner’s performance, there appears to be evidence that there has been a procedural irregularity in the conduct of assessment (procedures), or there appears to be evidence of prejudice or bias on the part of the evaluators or in the assessment process.
4.  The time to contest results based on other grounds as mentioned above is limited to a period of 10 working days after the publication of the results. Should the learner wish to contest the score after this initial period has passed, they can then appeal to the exam board to request acceptance of their late contest. 
5.  It is important to note that the regulations on mitigating circumstances require learners to notify their faculty and/or programme manager of any matters which may be relevant to their academic performance (e.g. personal or medical circumstances) at the time they occur and to supply supporting documentary evidence, such as medical certificates. Unless there were very good reasons why the learner was unable to notify faculty members or supply evidence at the time, an appeal will likely be disallowed.

## **Article 3.9  Inclusive Assessment and Examination Provisions**

1.  The programme aims to provide an inclusive and accessible learning environment that ensures all learners, regardless of personal circumstances, are given equal opportunities to demonstrate their knowledge, understanding, and skills. 
2.  *Special needs learners* are learners with permanent or temporary disabilities that affect their education life academically or socially. These disabilities can include, but are not limited to, chronic illnesses, physical disabilities, visual or hearing impairments, speech/language disorders, learning difficulties, psychological problems, neural divergent conditions such as autism or hyperactivity and attention deficiency.
3.  The special needs learner should apply to the programme manager if they feel the need for extra support during their studies. They can do this at any time during their studies. If a learner does not apply to the programme manager, they cannot claim any negative effects of their disability on their study progression or study results.
4.  The programme manager fills out an “adjustment request form” in consultation with the learner in question and communicates the learner’s requests and disability status to the programme management, instructors and examination board.
5.  If a learner is specifically unable to comply with the standard examination or assessment criteria due to a disability, chronic illness, neurodiversity, or other special personal circumstances, the learner may submit a request for reasonable adjustments to the Examination Board. The Examination Board’s decision regarding adjustments will be communicated in writing to the learner within 14 days of submission. If the request is denied, the decision will be accompanied by the rationale and information on the appeals procedure. 

## **Article 3.10  Certificates and Diplomas**

1\.  The Examination Board issues a certificate to a learner who has completed a certification phase based on a positive decision by the assessment panel, in accordance with the progression principles defined in Article 3.4. The Examination Board issues a diploma to a learner who has:

a. completed all certification phases;

b. met all programme learning outcomes;

c. submitted and passed the final project and final VIVA;

d. fulfilled all procedural requirements, including full payment of tuition fees.

2\.  Certificates and diplomas are issued in English. A diploma supplement is provided alongside the diploma, following the applicable principles and guidelines.

3\.  A learner may request the Examination Board to postpone the issuing of a certificate or diploma.

4\.  The names of modules listed on the diploma and accompanying grade list must correspond to the official module names defined in the Teaching and Examination Regulations (TER) valid during the academic year in which the module was completed.

5\.  If a module name changes after completion of the module, the original module name will be retained on the transcript. Exceptions may be made where Programme Management determines that using the original name would cause significant confusion to external stakeholders. In such cases, a clarifying footnote or explanatory remark will be added to the transcript or supplement.

6\.  Learners have the right to request documentation explaining the equivalency of module names for external recognition purposes.

  

## **Article 3.11  Academic Integrity** 

1.  Plagiarism and academic misconduct are defined as any act by a learner that partially or completely precludes an accurate assessment of their knowledge or skills or those of another learner. Plagiarism can include handing in someone else’s work or copying work, long or short, from a source without providing a reference or acknowledgement. This also applies to AI tools that help learners generate responses on any work that is part of an assessment, where learners fail to disclose the use of such tools, provide proper referencing and citation for content or insights generated by these tools and demonstrate reflective and critical thinking regarding the use and results of these tools. Faculty is also required to integrate such technology into their teaching with disclosure, accountability and reflection. Refer to the [Policy on the Use of Advanced Technology in Teaching and Learning](https://docs.google.com/document/d/16D0mqK9nxBB61BaMOgsQRTlzT3YMLUfOLJhGoE-Yf8Y/edit?usp=sharing) for further details.
2.  If the assessor has a good reason to suppose that plagiarism or academic misconduct has taken place, s/he must report it to the Programme Manager.
3.  The programme manager informs the learner and invites the learner to a hearing about the claimed plagiarism or academic misconduct. The Examination Board makes a final decision based on the evidence. In the case of plagiarism, the learner’s work is assigned 0.
4.  In case of repeated misconduct or plagiarism, the programme management, after consulting the Examination Board, has the right to terminate the learner’s registration permanently. 
5.  The learner can appeal to this decision within six weeks of the announcement of the decision to the NRTO’s Board of Appeals, which has the final decision on the result of the appeal.

  

## **Article 3.12  Exemptions through Recognition of Prior Learning** 

1.  At Team Academy, a formal RPL policy has been adopted, which is complemented with a tailormade procedure for learners in the Professional Master's. 
2.  The Examination Board is authorised to grant exemptions to a learner through Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL) if a learner has demonstrated the achievement of prior learning that corresponds in sufficient measure to the module(s) which the learner wishes to be recognised. Learners must submit a substantiated request to the Examination Board, indicating and documenting the evidence that the learner has achieved the learning outcomes in question. Based on the evidence submitted, the Examination Board will determine if the learner has met the requirements for the relevant module and inform the learner within four weeks from the date on which the request was received.
3.  Neither degree certificates nor diplomas are awarded based on exemptions alone.

  

## **Article 3.13  Exemptions through Force Majeure**

1.  If individual force majeure applies, due to which a learner has not been able to do an assessment, they can submit an application to the examination board for an extra test attempt. Individual force majeure applies if one or more of the following circumstances occur: illness, physical, sensory or other impairment, pregnancy or special family circumstances because of which the learner was unable to do the assessment.
2.  Circumstantial force majeure occurs if there is an external emergency before a test due to which a learner may not be able to do the assessment. In this case, the university director or the executive board may decide that the test concerned will not be conducted and will be rescheduled. If this decision is made, that decision will be communicated to the learner as soon as possible. 

In the event of an emergency during or after an assessment, the examination board will decide whether or not the assessment in question can take place or whether the assessment results can be upheld. If the assessment results cannot be upheld, the test is declared invalid, and an additional test opportunity is offered.

# **Appendix 1: Program Competencies, Outcomes and Objectives**

  

**Programme Outcomes**

The programme outcomes of the Professional Master’s in Applied Artificial Intelligence at Team Academy are based on the current developments in AI, leadership, and practitioner research. They are shaped in close dialogue with the professional field to ensure that the curriculum remains responsive to the rapidly evolving AI landscape and the needs of society. In our view, a Professional Master’s in Applied AI must enable graduates to work autonomously and responsibly on complex, real-world challenges, using advanced AI methods alongside reflective leadership and cycles of inquiry to create meaningful solutions. 

The curriculum, therefore, integrates technical expertise such as machine learning, deep learning, natural language processing, large language models, retrieval-augmented generation, computer vision, responsible AI, and MLOps, with action research methodology and its inquiry-based tools at the self, group and organisational levels. Through this integration, students not only gain mastery of applied AI techniques but also learn to apply them responsibly, collaboratively, and with impact. This holistic approach aligns with the **Dutch Qualifications Framework (NLQF) at level 7**, which emphasises mastery, research competence, interprofessional conduct, and effect in professional practice. 

**Mastery**  

Graduates demonstrate mastery of Applied AI by acting autonomously and ethically in complex and uncertain contexts, reflecting critically on their personal leadership practice, and integrating responsible AI principles into professional decision-making.

**Practitioner Research** 

Graduates are able to design and conduct practitioner research that frames business problems into AI solutions, analyse and model data with advanced techniques, and generate evidence-based insights through cycles of inquiry and reflection, which contributes to fresh thinking and applications in their professional practice.

**Interprofessional Conduct**

Graduates collaborate effectively across disciplines and organisational levels, facilitating dialogue and interventions in diverse professional and cultural contexts, and communicating complex AI concepts and research outcomes clearly to both specialist and non-specialist audiences.

Effect 

Graduates deliver measurable impact in their professional practice by deploying AI systems in organisational settings with a sense of responsibility, transferring knowledge across domains, and helping to shape future-oriented strategies that have an impact on their work and communities.

  

*Further information on the programme’s competencies and specific learning outcomes can be found in the Educational Handbook as well as in the module descriptions.*

# **Appendix 2: Professional Master's in Applied AI, Application & Selection Process**

The Professional Master's in Applied AI admission process consists of the following structured steps.

**1. Basic Entry Requirements:** all candidates must meet these:

  - **Bachelor's degree** (from an accredited institution)
  - **English Proficiency:** C1 (proof required, IELTS, TOEFL, or equivalent)
  - **Motivation and Commitment:** Demonstrable interest in artificial intelligence and research; commitment to invest the required study hours.

**2. Application Submission:** Candidates submit an **online application form**, including the following documentation:

  - Academic diplomas
  - Relevant certifications (if available)
  - Updated CV highlighting education and relevant professional experience

**3. Initial Intake Interview:** during which the **Admissions Consultant** assesses:

  - Candidate’s overall motivation and fit for the programme
  - General communication skills and readiness for graduate-level online education
  - Verification of previous education, specifically identifying evidence of:
      
      - intermediate-level programming skills (preferably in Python)
      - fundamental knowledge of mathematics and statistics

**4**. **Enrolment in Preparatory programme:** Candidates will enrol in a structured foundations module tailored to ensure readiness for the professional master's degree. After successful completion, candidates transition into the main programme. If the candidate wishes to waive this foundations module, they can always take a proficiency assessment before their enrollment. The proficiency assessment has two components:

1.  *\*An online test that covers questions on the following topics:\**

<!-- end list -->

  - Basic Python programming
  - Basic mathematics and statistics fundamentals
  - Leadership awareness

<!-- end list -->

1.  A follow-up call by the key faculty after the results of the online test to review and validate the assessment results.

**5. Decision & Recommendations (for those who have taken the test):**  The key faculty members make a final decision based on the candidate’s cumulative performance:

  - **Admission to the Main programme:** the candidate demonstrated adequate competencies and readiness for direct enrolment.
  - **Conditional Admission:** faculty recommend enrolment in a **Preparatory programme** designed to bridge gaps in skills (programming, mathematics/statistics and/or leadership).

If you are hindered by a functional disability or chronic illness, you can request the examination board for extra facilities. For this, you need the recommendation of your programme manager. S/he can also inform you about the possible facilities. If the exam board grants the facility, this facility is valid for the duration as described in the decision of the examination board. # Programme-specific provisions — Tech MBA

### Article 1.1 Applicability of the Regulations

1. These regulations apply to the teaching and examinations for the Tech MBA at Team Academy.
2. These regulations apply to anyone enrolled in the Tech MBA at Team Academy.
3. Programme Management ensures that the guidelines prescribed in this document are followed by the faculty.

### Article 1.2 Definitions of Terms Used

Definitions follow the Master's TER (academic year, assessor, admissions consultant, certification, coach, cohort, competency-based, diploma, ECTS, facilitator, learner, LMS, module, programme, supervisor, workshop), with the following Tech-MBA-specific terms added, already in use in the Tech MBA's own documentation: **action learning coach** (facilitates action learning sets), **mentor** (provides group mentoring tailored to career aspirations), **certification** (one of the Tech MBA's four certifications: Transform Your Business; Lead Your Business; Innovate and Grow Your Business; Leadership and Action Research).

## Section 2: Programme Structure

### Article 2.1 Structure of the Academic Year

1. The Tech MBA is offered in consecutive phases made up of four certifications followed by the Final Project, as set out in `Tech MBA Programme Handbook_2024.md`.
2. The Tech MBA is a fully online degree programme.
3. 4. There is one intake per year, in November, consistent with both the Master's TER (Article 2.3.10) and the Tech MBA's own cohort documentation ("November 2025 Cohort").

### Article 2.2 Organisation of the Programme

1. The average weekly workload is approximately 20 hours per week, consistent with the Tech MBA Programme Handbook's own statement ("dedicating approximately 20 hours per week").
2. The institute's learning management system (Canvas) and communication platforms (Slack, AmTech-email) are used for all digital components, per the Tech MBA Supervision Guide.
3. Programme competencies, outcomes and objectives are set out in the Tech MBA Programme Handbook.

### Article 2.3 Application & Selection Process

Follows the Master's TER's application and selection process (online application with diplomas/certifications/CVs; intake interview by the Admissions Consultant assessing motivation and readiness; English-language proficiency requirement; right of appeal to the Examination Board via the Programme Manager). *[Tech-MBA-specific entry requirements and any foundations/proficiency-test equivalent are not yet separately documented for the Tech MBA and should be confirmed with Programme Management — flagged as a residual item, not assumed.]*

### Article 2.4 Learning as a Personal Accountability

Learners are expected to participate in all scheduled learning activities (workshops, action learning sets, mentoring, supervision meetings), notify the Programme Manager of absence in advance where possible, and provide alternative learning evidence for missed activities — as per the Master's TER, Article 2.4.

### Article 2.5 Career Coaching / Mentoring

The Tech MBA's group mentoring component, described in the Tech MBA Programme Handbook, serves the equivalent function to the Master's TER's Career Coaching / Talent Accelerator provision — supporting learners' professional identity and career opportunities, without affecting academic results.

### Article 2.6 Certificate and Degree

1. Learners receive a certificate upon successful completion of each of the Tech MBA's four certifications, based on a positive decision following assessment (module projects, weekly tasks, reflective accounts, knowledge checks, and — where applicable — VIVA).
2. Learners who complete all four certifications and the Final Project, meeting all programme learning outcomes, are awarded the Tech MBA degree.
3. The awarded degree and title are formally stated on the diploma in accordance with national and university regulations.

### Article 2.7 Termination of Studies and Re-registration

Follows the Master's TER, Article 2.7, in full: enrolment may be terminated in accordance with the Dutch Higher Education and Research Act (WHW), Articles 7.42, 7.32 and 7.33; temporary withdrawal, suspension and re-enrolment follow the same written-request procedure.

## Section 3: Assessment of Learning

### Article 3.1 Types of Assessment

Tech MBA assessment is **Tech-MBA-specific** in its weighting, as already documented in the Tech MBA Programme Handbook: Module Projects (50%), Weekly Tasks/Activities (30%), Action Learning Reflective Accounts (10%), and Knowledge Checks (10%) per certification, with a caesura of 60/100 to pass. The Final Project (21.5 ECTS, per the Tech MBA Supervision Guide) is assessed as: Project Plan (15%), Action Research Methodology (30%), Technology Focus (40%), Video Presentation & Q&A/VIVA (15%). This differs in structure from the Master's programmatic low/medium/high-stake portfolio model, and the Tech-MBA-specific figures given here — not the Master's figures — govern.

### Article 3.2 Assessments and Deadlines

Follows the Master's TER's principles (assignments visible on LMS with submission deadlines; late submission accepted only with an approved extension; extension requests follow the standard form/process) — see the Tech MBA Supervision Guide's own extension provisions for the Final Project (up to six months, requested at least two weeks before the deadline, per TER Article 3.5 as cross-referenced there).

### Article 3.3 Determining and Announcement of Results

Assessors record results within a reasonable working-day window after submission (as under the Master's TER); results remain provisional until administrative checks are complete; the Examination Board determines results as final.

### Article 3.4 Progression and Certification Principles

Progression decisions for the Tech MBA are made following the completion of each certification, based on the assessment weighting in Article 3.1 above and a minimum caesura of 60/100.

### Article 3.5 Improvement, Resubmission, and Reconsideration

Follows the Master's TER's principle that learners may resubmit assessed work within a defined window following original feedback, subject to Programme Manager/Examination Board oversight.

### Article 3.6 Validity Period of Results

Assessment results remain valid for an unlimited period unless the knowledge/skills tested become outdated relative to the current course, with a minimum validity period of six years from the granting of the mark, as under the Master's TER, Article 3.6.

### Article 3.7 Discussing the Feedback & Assessment Results

Learners may arrange a call with the assessor/supervisor to discuss results or seek further feedback, as under the Master's TER, Article 3.7.

### Article 3.8 The Right to Appeal Assessment Results and Decisions

Follows the Master's TER, Article 3.8, in full: learners have the right to inspect evaluated work; appeals are permitted only on grounds of mitigating circumstances, procedural irregularity, or evidence of bias — not simple dissatisfaction with a result; the time limit to contest results on such grounds is 10 working days after publication, extendable by application to the Examination Board.

### Article 3.9 Inclusive Assessment and Examination Provisions

*(Carried over in full — this is one of the two academic-integrity/non-discrimination provisions Standard 1 specifically requires; see also the shared Gender Equality and Equal Opportunities Statement.)* The Tech MBA aims to provide an inclusive and accessible learning environment ensuring all learners, regardless of personal circumstances, are given equal opportunities to demonstrate their knowledge, understanding and skills. Learners with permanent or temporary disabilities — including chronic illness, physical disabilities, sensory impairments, learning difficulties, psychological conditions, or neurodivergence — should apply to the Programme Manager for support at any time during their studies. The Programme Manager completes an adjustment-request form in consultation with the learner and communicates the learner's requests and disability status to programme management, instructors and the Examination Board. Where a learner cannot comply with standard assessment criteria due to disability, chronic illness, neurodiversity, or other special personal circumstances, they may request reasonable adjustments (extended time; alternative examination format; adapted physical/online environment; assistive technology; alternative assessment forms aligned with intended learning outcomes) from the Examination Board, which communicates its decision in writing within 14 days, with rationale and appeal information if denied.

### Article 3.10 Certificates and Diploma

The Examination Board issues a certificate for each completed certification and a diploma to learners who have completed all four certifications, met all programme learning outcomes, submitted and passed the Final Project (including the VIVA/Q&A session), and fulfilled all procedural and financial requirements. Certificates and diplomas are issued in English with an accompanying Diploma Supplement.

### Article 3.11 Academic Integrity

*(Carried over in full — the other of the two academic-integrity/non-discrimination provisions Standard 1 specifically requires.)* Plagiarism and academic misconduct are defined as any act by a learner that partially or completely precludes an accurate assessment of their knowledge or skills, or those of another learner, including handing in someone else's work, copying without acknowledgement, and undisclosed or unreflective use of AI tools in assessed work. The Tech MBA's own Final Project Agreement Form already operationalises this specifically for the Final Project, requiring learners to plan, disclose and account for their use of Advanced Technology Tools (AI Usage Planning; ongoing updates; an AI Use Statement and reflective account at submission; retained evidence of use), consistent with this Article and with Team Academy's institution-wide Policy on the Use of Advanced Technology in Teaching and Learning. If an assessor has good reason to suspect plagiarism or misconduct, they report it to the Programme Manager, who informs the learner and convenes a hearing; the Examination Board makes the final decision. In case of plagiarism, the learner's work is assigned a mark of 0. In case of repeated misconduct, programme management, after consulting the Examination Board, may terminate the learner's registration permanently, subject to appeal to the NRTO Board of Appeals within six weeks.

### Article 3.12 Exemptions through Recognition of Prior Learning

Follows the Master's TER, Article 3.12: the Examination Board may grant exemptions through Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL) on the basis of a substantiated learner request and evidence, decided within four weeks; degree certificates and diplomas are not awarded on exemptions alone.

### Article 3.13 Exemptions through Force Majeure

Follows the Master's TER, Article 3.13: individual force majeure (illness, impairment, pregnancy, special family circumstances) and circumstantial force majeure (external emergency) provisions apply identically.
