## INTRODUCTION

Examination Boards in general perform an important task: they are responsible for assuring the quality of exams and final examinations. Their purpose is to determine, in an independent and expert way, that each student has met the requirements to be awarded a degree set by the programme concerned. This means Examination Boards fulfil a vital role in monitoring the quality of the process leading to the awarding of qualifications. After all, students, stakeholders and supervisory bodies must have faith in the fact that any institution awards its qualifications with all due care.

Thanks to the amendment of the Dutch Higher Education and Scientific Research Act (Wet op het hoger onderwijs en wetenschappelijk onderzoek, “WHW” in Dutch) through the Dutch Governance Strengthening Act (Wet versterking besturing, “Wvb” in Dutch), as of 1 September 2010, Examination Boards acquired more substantive tasks and their independent position has been strengthened.

At Team Academy, the Examination Board “is the institution which in an objective and professional manner determines whether a student complies with the conditions posed by the education- and examination regulations with regards to knowledge, insight and skills needed to obtain a degree.” (direct quote from article 7.12 lid 2 WHW). Its tasks are issuing certificates and diplomas, determining student standards, ensuring test quality, determination of guidelines, granting exemptions, ensuring quality of testing organisation, taking action in case of fraud, attending to requests, reporting and appointing assessors. To this end, the Examination Board meets on a monthly basis. The Examination Board stays in close contact with the executive, programme management and faculty throughout the programme to ensure an effective and efficient execution of its tasks. Formally, the Examination Board reports in writing to the Executive Board on a yearly basis by presenting both an Annual Report for the past and an Annual Plan for the upcoming academic year. In order to function optimally, it is essential that the governing body of the institution guarantees that the Examination Board can operate independently and with due expertise, and it is important that (the members) of the Examination Board are well aware of the frameworks within which they must perform their statutory tasks.

The aim of this handbook is to inform Team Academy’s institutional and educational management and the Examination Board about the statutory frameworks within which the Examination Board should operate and the way in which they can properly perform their tasks in this context. These internal Examination Board Rules and Regulations also describe the procedures which are designed to ensure that both the Examination Board and other stakeholders within and outside Team Academy know which procedures are in existence, how they can be applied and which time frame surrounds and protects them.  
 Part 1 refers to the internal framework of rules and regulations that surrounds the functioning of the Examination Board itself while Section 2 focuses on the alignment of the Examination Board’s procedures with the most current Team Academy TER (2025).

Part 1: Internal rules and regulations for the functioning of the Examination Board

1A: General Provisions  
 Where in this document is referred to the Chair of the Examination Board, in his/her absence, s/he will be replaced by the Vice-Chair. In case both the Chair and Vice-Chair are in absentia, the Secretary of the Examination Board will substitute for them and take on their responsibilities and capacities.

1B: Installation and Independence of the Examination Board  
 With the adoption of the Wet Versterking Besturing, “independence and expertise” became the major features that any Exam Committee must demonstrate (WHW, Section 7.12.a. and the Explanatory Memorandum). This independence and expertise apply to the position of Exam Committees within an organization, the composition and appointment of members as well as the tasks and powers of Exam Committees.

The governing body of the institution appoints or instals both the Examination Board as a committee and the individual members of the Examination Board, in consultation with the existing members. The Examination Board can be appointed for more than one programme, as long as the activities concern individual programmes. A TER is drawn up that applies to all these programmes, but with separate sections for where different rules apply for the different programmes. Within Team Academy the tasks are divided as follows:  
 The programme teams (curriculum team and programme management) are responsible for the structure and implementation of the programme as described in the TER, and they ensure that the teaching and the programme meet the quality standards.  
 The Examination Board advises the Programme Management and the Executive Board on the TER regarding exam regulations and, for each individual student, assesses the results of the education provided within the framework of the programme’s TER.

Due to its supervisory role, it is important that the Examination Board can perform its tasks and exercise its authority independently and with due expertise. The independent functioning of the Examination Board in respect of the Executive Board means that although the Executive Board installs the Examination Board, the institution must ensure that the Examination Board can carry out its work independently within the institution. This also means, for example, that the Executive Board cannot impose any obligations on the Examination Board regarding the assessment of students. The Executive Board does, however, have final responsibility for the quality of the education provided and the awarding of qualifications (WHW, Section 7.10a, paragraph 1). An Examination Board must act within the boundaries of the TER; this also guarantees that the way in which exams are conducted is in line with the programme framework.

In other words: the Executive Board approves the TER and thus has final responsibility for the quality of the education provided. The Examination Board assesses if students have met the requirements set down in the TER and, after this assessment, the institution awards a Bachelor’s Degree. The Examination Board assesses the realised quality against the (quality) requirements and provisions of the TER approved by the Executive Board. If an Examination Board observes that these requirements and provisions are not being met, it has several intervention instruments at its disposal.

In addition to independence in terms of its position in the organisation in respect of those responsible for the quality of the education provided (Executive Board, Programme Management), the Examination Board’s independence should also be demonstrated by its composition. This means that (1) an Examination Board must always have at least one external member, and that (2) managers with financial responsibility cannot sit on an Examination Board.  
 With the introduction of the Wet versterking besturing, the emphasis in terms of the responsibility of the Examination Board is shifted to the substantive aspects of examinations. In the WHW, for example, assuring the quality of exams and final examinations is listed explicitly as a task (WHW, Section 7.12b, paragraph 1 under a). To this end, it is necessary that the members of Examination Boards have a high degree of subject-matter expertise, expertise in assessment and knowledge of the statutory frameworks. The Examination Board as a whole must cover these different areas of expertise. When an Examination Board is being compiled, the Executive Board can therefore choose to require each individual member to possess expertise in the different areas, or to have the Examination Board made up of members with different subject-matter expertise and an expert in assessments.

In the appointment process, the Programme Management can nominate new members to the Executive Board. However, such nominations are not binding. Furthermore, at minimum, the Executive Board assures that the Examination Board is listened to regarding the appointment of new members (WHW Section 7.12a, paragraph 3); the Examination Board has an advisory role in this regard.  
 The Executive Board appoints the members of the Examination Board. The law stipulates no term for such appointments. This means that, in principle, members are appointed for an indefinite period. To guarantee the continuity of knowledge and decision making within the Examination Board, it is desirable members are appointed for a period of four years, with one possible extension of another four years. The same maximum extension applies to the Chair of the Examination Board, but s/he is appointed for a five-year term to avoid the risk of key members and the Chair leaving the Board at the same time.

As a rule, the Examination Board or the Programme Director nominates a candidate member to the Executive Board. The Executive Board then assesses whether the candidate member meets the criteria set for subject-matter and assessment expertise, and consults the members of the Examination Board on the nomination via the Chair of the Examination Board. The Examination Board has an advisory role in this (WHW, Section 7.12a, paragraph 3). An appointment letter is then sent to the candidate member concerned. If the appointment of a Chair is involved, this will be specifically stated in the appointment letter. Should the Executive Board have any reason not to wish to appoint the candidate in question as a member, they will contact the Head of Learning Design and Development Lead for further consultation.

1C: Composition of the Examination Board  
 Team Academy has chosen a setup in which there is a separate Examination Board for each Bachelor’s programme. The central Examination Board is composed of the chair, the vice-chair, an official secretary and one or more members per programme. The permanent examination committee consists of a minimum of 3. Members of the Examination Board preferably are evenly distributed in age, gender and cultural background and able to represent the programme they are active in.

One statutory obligation is that at least one member of the Examination Board is a lecturer within the programme(s) for which the Examination Board is installed. Moreover, one of the members must be “independent” and capable of making well-considered decisions if a conflict of interests arises. Taking the above arguments into account, the profile for internal members is as follows:

An internal member of an Examination Board:  
 Is a member of the fulltime or parttime faculty of Team Academy and involved in teaching in the programme(s) covered by the Examination Board;  
 Follows a training course on his/her area of expertise within the framework of his/her tasks on Examination Board within the first year of membership;  
 Possesses the ‘soft’ trait of independence and is capable of making a well-considered decision in the event of any conflict of interests. Independence means being independent in behaviour, daring to maintain and defend a personal standpoint that is in opposition to that of others (and to any one-sided interest) in the interest of the quality of the programme, actions in this regard are objective and critical, and recognizing and anticipating situations in which personal and quality interests are (potentially) in conflict.  
 Has preferably been involved in teaching within the programme(s) or has been involved in developing the new programme for a minimum of three years.  
 Preferably has a Basic or Senior Qualification Examination (BKE / SKE) or similar. An alternative for the BKE can be the MOOC Erasmus course on Assessing in Higher Education.

Besides the internal member(s), it is mandatory to have an external member in the Examination Board. The goal of including an external member is to increase external legitimacy regarding the assessment and examinations. The external expert thus adds an external perspective to the quality assurance of the assessments and examinations of the programmes that fall under the Examination Board. The external expert can be a fellow subject-matter expert at another institution or from a different discipline within the same institution. It may be someone who is an expert in the field of assessment, or someone from the professional field (in the Netherlands or abroad).

Profile of an assessment expert:  
 has knowledge and insight into the (faculty) assessment policy framework.  
 has knowledge and insight into the standard quality criteria for assessments (validity, reliability, transparency and workability).  
 is able to apply the quality criteria to the usual higher professional education (in Dutch: HBO) assessment formats (open questions, multiple-choice questions, essays, papers, theses, etc.);  
 is able to assess the quality of formative assessment formats;  
 has insight into the quality and shortcomings of the usual assessment formats;  
 has insight into the standard methods for determining the caesura;  
 is able to perform an elementary item analysis (reliability, p-value, Point-Biserial correlation, etc.);  
 is able to perform an elementary analysis of assessment results (e.g. linking it to earlier results and course evaluations);  
 is able to provide adequate feedback;  
 has teaching experience at higher professional education level (HBO);  
 is familiar with digital assessment.

The institution is obliged to offer the members of the Examination Board sufficient opportunity for the development of expertise. At Team Academy, this fulfilled as follows:  
 On appointment, each new Examination Board member receives a copy of the Examination Board Handbook and the TER of the programme(s);  
 Every year, at least one training course or participation at a congress is organised for the Examination Board members to develop their expertise.

Due to the ‘weight’ of the Examination Board, at least one of the members (preferably the Chair) should be in possession of a Senior Qualification Examination (SKE). Other members should also strive to obtain this recognition as this adds to the status of the Examination Board.

External members may not be involved as lecturers, or have a role in the management and development of the education provided in the programmes that fall under the Examination Board. Managers with financial responsibility are excluded from being members of Examination Boards. In addition, there are several officials who will not be eligible for membership due to the incompatibility of membership with their other tasks. At Team Academy, the following officials are excluded from sitting on Examination Boards:

Members of the Foundation Board;

Members of the Executive Board;

Directors of Colleges or Graduate Schools;

Programme Directors and Directors of Operations / Operational Managers;

Chairs of the Advisory Boards of the programmes that fall under the Examination Board;

Student Advisors.

The first six officials are excluded from membership due to their line management or financial management responsibilities regarding the education provided. The Chairs are excluded from membership due to the incompatibility of their tasks (advising the Programme Management) with those of the Examination Boards (monitoring the programmes as a whole). Finally, Student Advisors are excluded from membership due to a possible conflict of interests between the interests of the student on the one hand, and the decisions of the Examination Board on the other. In addition to the officials mentioned above, the Executive Board may exclude other officials from membership if this is desired. The membership of a member of the Examination Board terminates when:

the member takes up a position that is incompatible with membership of the Examination Board;

the member’s employment is terminated (for internal members);

the member wishes to terminate the membership;

four years after the initial appointment has passed. The Examination Board secretary maintains a retirement schedule and interim vacancies will be filled as soon as possible;

the Examination Board member no longer performs satisfactorily.

In the final instance, the only way to terminate the membership is by a decision of the Executive Board, possibly in combination with immediate suspension. However, this is seen as a far-reaching measure and can only take place if this measure is proportionate. As a rule, the Examination Board, its Chair, informs the programme management of any unsatisfactory performance on the part of (a member of) the Examination Board, and the programme management apprises the Executive Board of the situation.

1D: Tasks and Profiles of the Examination Board members  
 The Examination Board operates at three levels:

Strategically it looks at external and internal developments, assesses the impact on current policies and anticipates on it by its composition of the annual plans, guidelines and directions;

Tactically it monitors the quality of assessment (on set criteria defined in the assessment policy), delegated processes;

Operationally it delegates certain tasks in standardized procedures.

The Examination Board meets a minimum of twice a year. Currently, the frequency is set at a monthly internal meeting and a quarterly formal meeting which includes representative(s) from the Management Team. Decisions are made by a majority of the members. The (Vice)Chair and the official secretary will consult more frequently about, for example, the requests of students. The meetings of the Examination Board are, in principle, closed. The Examination Board can invite guests to attend meetings, including a Student Advisor, Educational Quality Advisor or a programme manager. The subjects that are dealt with at plenary meetings include:

advising on the TER;

advising on the assessment policy of a programme;

finalising the assessment forms for the final projects of bachelor’s programmes.

The vice chair takes the meeting minutes, while the official secretary is also responsible for setting down the decisions and advice of the Examination Board and for communicating them to the parties involved.

Chair  
 The Executive Board appoints a Chair-person in consultation with the Examination Board (WHW Article 7.12a, sub 4). The Chair is responsible for the independence and expertise of the Examination Board in its functioning, and is accountable for this. Moreover, the Chair should be a competent process monitor; s/he should allow the experts to be heard. The Chair of the Examination Board:

is responsible for the independence and expertise of the Examination Board in its functioning, and is accountable for this;

is accountable for and defends the policy and decisions made in respect of internal and external parties such as the Appeal Board;

signs certificates and diploma supplements;

advises the Executive Board, on behalf of the Examination Board, regarding the appointment of members of the Examination Board;

prepares meetings together with the official secretary;

chairs the meetings of the Examination Board;

contributes to the drawing up of the annual report and approves the annual report;

is responsible for the mandates approved of by the Examination Board;

(if applicable) is a member of the central Examination Board.

1E: Activities of the Examination Board  
 The Examination Board must assure the quality of exams and diplomas. To this end, the legislature has given the Examination Board the tasks and powers mentioned below. The tasks of the Examination Board can be mandated to persons and Boards. In this regard, it is important that it is clearly set down to whom the tasks are mandated and what responsibilities are covered by the mandate and under what conditions.

The statutory tasks and powers of the Examination Board are:

To determine, in an objective and expert manner, if a student meets the requirements set down in the Rules & Regulations (TER) in respect of the knowledge, insight and skills necessary to be awarded a qualification (WHW, Section 7.12, paragraph 2).

To assure the quality of exams and final examinations (WHW, Section 7.12b, paragraph 1 under a).

To set down guidelines and instructions within the framework of the TER in order to assess and approve the results of exams and final examinations (WHW, Section 7.12b, paragraph 1 under b).

To grant exemptions from taking one or more exams (WHW, Section 7.12b, paragraph 1 under d).

To take measures in the event of fraud (WHW, Section 7.12b, paragraph 2).

To assign assessors to conduct exams and determine the results thereof (WHW, Section 7.12c).

To award certificates and related diploma supplements, as proof that a final examination was passed (WHW, Section 7.11, paragraphs 2 and 4).

To grant permission for a student to follow a flexible study programme, for which the final examination leads to the award of a qualification (WHW, Section 7.12b, paragraph 1 under c).

To issue a transcript stating the exams passed to persons who have passed more than one exam but to whom no certificate can be awarded (WHW Section 7.11, paragraph 5).

To draw up an annual report of its activities (WHW, Section 7.12.b, paragraph 5).

Below, these ten tasks / activities are further explained in more detail.

Determining, in an objective and expert manner, if a student meets the requirements set down in the TER in respect of the knowledge, insight and skills necessary to be awarded a qualification  
 This means that the Examination Board must assess if the student has achieved the attainment level of the programme. If a student has passed all components of the exam in accordance with the provisions of the TER, the qualification can be awarded. The sum of the learning objectives of the individual components should, after all, lead at a minimum to the set attainment levels being achieved. The Examination Board can determine that passing the individual programme components will be closed with a final exam. In such cases, this requirement must be set down in the TER. Within this task, the Examination Board is also the body that is authorised, in special cases, to deviate from the provisions of the TER. Several examples of this are:

permitting a modified exam or final examination format;

extending the period of validity of an exam result;

replacing individual units of study with a different unit of study that has the same learning objective;

deviating from possibly mandatory participation in a practical exercise;

applying progression requirements set out in the TER differently in special cases.

Moreover, the Examination Board can determine, under conditions to be set by the Examination Board itself, that not every exam needs to be passed in order to record a pass for the final examination.

Assuring the quality of exams and final examinations  
 WHW, Section 7.12b, paragraph 1 under a states that the Examination Board has the task of assuring the quality of exams and final examinations. This provision was included in the Act based on the findings of the Education Inspectorate that Examination Boards spend too little time on “quality assurance and the quality policy involving exams and final examinations”. In practice, this task includes:

Assessing the quality and level of examination papers and related composite examinations.

Determining (possibly) whether the examination assignments and exams meet those final programme and /or test objectives in the presence of final programme and/or test objectives.

The assessment of the results of the grade examinations.

Conducting consultations about determining the result/assessment standard (caesura/cutting scores) of the exams.

The handling of complaints about the made examinations (including the outcome).

Identifying risks that threaten the quality of examinations and making proposals to the institution to limit those risks.

Following developments within and outside the organisation that may influence the quality or topicality of the examination.

Drawing up and implementing an assessment policy is an important tool in assuring the quality of exams and final examinations.

Setting down guidelines and instructions within the framework of the TER in order to assess and approve the results of exams and final examinations  
 The Examination Board should advise the Executive Board annually on the TER. In this way, the Examination Board can add substance to the quality assurance of the programme’s assessment programme.

Granting exemptions from taking one or more exams  
 The Examination Board has the power to grant a student an exemption for one or more subjects. The way in which this task is carried out is set down in the TER of the programme concerned. Whether or not an exemption can be granted is assessed against the TER. It is therefore necessary that the grounds for granting exemptions are set down in the TER of the relevant programme. Section 7.13, paragraph 2 under r of the WHW clearly states that these grounds could lie in exams or final examinations taken earlier in the higher education system, and in knowledge and skills acquired outside the higher education system.  

Broadly speaking, one condition for granting an exemption is that the replacement component has roughly the same learning objective as the component of the curriculum for which an exemption is granted. This task can be mandated to a person or a Board, but the Examination Board remains responsible. The Examination Board should set down who is authorised to sign such transcripts and under what conditions (e.g. the procedure and the delivery of a report to the Examination Board on an annual basis). In addition, it is possible for the Examination Board to have included in the TER components that are frequently used to replace standard components of the curriculum. In such instances, a request for an exemption is ‘automatically’ approved.

Taking measures in the event of fraud  
 The Examination Board is the body that must take measures in the event of fraud or other forms of academic misconduct.

Assigning assessors to conduct exams and determine the results thereof  
 An important instrument available to the Exam Committee for its task of assuring the quality of exams and final examinations, is the assigning of assessors. By law, this task lies with the Exam Committee.

Awarding certificates and related diploma supplements, as proof that an exam was passed  
 The Examination Board is the body that awards the certificate to the student once it has been determined that the student has met the requirements (cf. 1). The certificate and diploma supplement must be signed by the Chair of the Examination Board. In special cases, one of the other members of the Examination Board (with the exception of the external member) may sign the certificate and diploma supplement. The official secretary or members of staff who are not members of an Examination Board are not permitted to sign the certificate and diploma supplement.

Granting permission for a student to follow a flexible study programme, for which the exam leads to the award of a qualification  
 Team Academy currently has no flexible study programmes. The expectation is that in the future such programmes will be possible (expansion of the modular structure of programmes). The prior approval of the Examination Board will then be a condition for determining if a flexible programme is of the required level and if the study load and attainment levels of the programme are satisfactory.

Issuing a transcript stating the exams passed to persons who have passed more than one exam but to whom no certificate can be awarded  
 The Examination Board has the power to issue a transcript to students who have passed more than one exam, but to whom no certificate can be awarded. Such a transcript lists the exams that were passed. This can be important for students if, for example, they are transferring from one programme to another and are entitled to an exemption from a component of the new programme based on the results achieved earlier. This task can be mandated to a person or a Board, but the Examination Board remains responsible. The Examination Board should set down who is authorised to sign such transcripts and under what conditions.

Drawing up an annual report of its activities  
 The Examination Board should submit an annual report on its activities to the Executive Board. The aim of this annual report is:

to ensure the accountability of the Examination Board to the Executive Board;

to deliver input for the Programme Director, Associate Dean of Degree Programmes and the Executive Board on any improvements to the quality of the education;

to provide management information. This information is often requested in the accreditation procedure of a programme.

1F: Decisions of the Examination Board  
 The Examination Board may make decisions on many different matters. How the Examination Board makes its decisions will be set out in the house rules (cf. the Recommendation on p. 17). Examples of decisions set down in the WHW include:

Decisions to grant exemptions.

Approval of the examination schedules of students.

Decisions to award a qualification. In other words, the decision that a student has achieved the attainment levels of the programme.

Decisions to award a distinction, such as Cum Laude.

Decisions to allow a different form of examination for a student with a disability.

Decisions involving the general assessment policy of a programme, for example, approving an assessment form for final projects.

Drawing up guidelines for assessors.

Imposing sanctions for fraud and plagiarism.

Assigning assessors.

Decisions on requests for an extension of the period of validity of an exam.

Decisions on permitting additional opportunities to take an exam.

Determining alternative programme composition (for example, when a curriculum revision is concerned).

Approval of study units successfully completed abroad.

Decisions to approve a flexible study programme.

Not every decision needs to be made by the full Examination Board. The Chair can make several of the decisions referred to above independently. The decision-making process, however, must be transparent. It is therefore up to the Examination Board to set down in the regulations who is authorised to make what type of decision. The guidelines below can be used to this end:

The gravity of the decision plays a role. In the event that an appeal may be lodged against a decision, for example, against the imposition of sanctions for fraud or plagiarism, it is desirable that the decision is made by the full Examination Board.

Decisions involving the assessment or examination policy of a programme (determining alternative final projects, guidelines for assessors), should always be made by the full Examination Board.

A request that has never arisen earlier, will at least be submitted to the Chair.

The official secretary can deal with standard decisions (e.g. replacing a subject that has been passed earlier). Decisions of this type must be well documented, for example in a separate document entitled “Decisions of the Examination Board”.

In the event that a decision must be made by the full Examination Board, the decision will be made by a majority vote. If the vote is tied, the Chair’s vote will break the tie. If the decision is to be sent to the party or parties involved, the Chair must sign the decision. In the event of so-called ‘standard decision’, the decision can be signed by another person with the designation i.o. (on behalf of) or b.a. (in the absence of).

1G: Complaints Handling  
 Students should, in the first instance, submit any complaints concerning exams or final examinations to the Examination Board of the programme involved, with the exception of objections or appeals. When handling complaints, the Examination Board applies the principle of the right to a hearing and to present counter-arguments: both the student and lecturer will be heard. At least one member of the Examination Board, in addition to the Chair, should be involved in handling complaints. If a student submits a request or complaint to the Examination Board that concerns an assessor who is a member of the Examination Board, the assessor concerned may not take part in handling the request or complaint.

A student has, on the basis of the Burgerlijk Wetboek (Civil Code) the right to challenge (“wraking” in Dutch) a member of the Examination Board if s/he has the impression that this member is not impartial to the case. The Central Examination Board decides upon this challenge. Should a member of the Examination Board be challenged then s/he is excluded from taking part in the decision.

Should a student want to appeal a decision taken by the Examination Board, they have the right to appeal decisions of the Examination Board through the independent NRTO disputes and appeals procedure.

Final note on Accreditation  
 The way in which the Examination Board performs its statutory tasks plays an increasingly more important role in accreditations, including that of the Accreditation Organization of the Netherlands and Flanders (NVAO).

## Section 3: Assessment of Learning

### Article 3.1 Types of Assessment

Assessment in the Bachelor of Science in Applied Computing follows a competency-based and programmatic assessment approach.

Learners build a portfolio of evidence throughout the programme through multiple assessment moments distributed across the learning journey.

Assessment evidence may include, but is not limited to:  
a. workshop exercises;  
b. weekly tasks  
c. peer and self-assessments;  
d. learning reflections;  
e. group and module projects;  
f. presentations;  
g. reports;  
i. final project deliverables.

Assessments are organised across three stake levels:  
a. low-stake assessments, primarily to support learning through feedback and feedforward;  
b. medium-stake assessments, which provide more formal evidence of learner development;  
c. high-stake assessments, including certification VIVAs, portfolio reviews by a panel, and the final project.

The Examination Board safeguards the quality, fairness, and integrity of assessment and final academic decision-making within the programme.

### Article 3.2 Assessment Design and Variation

Assessments are designed as evidence-building events that collectively demonstrate learner mastery of programme competencies over time.

The design of assessments must ensure:  
a. alignment with programme outcomes, certification learning outcomes, module learning outcomes, and assessment tasks;  
b. relevance to professional practice and current industry needs;  
c. a balanced and longitudinal mix of low-, medium-, and high-stake tasks;  
d. use of transparent and criterion-referenced rubrics;  
e. clear communication to learners about assessment type, stake level, and contribution to the portfolio.

During assessment design, each task is entered into a stake-level mapping and alignment matrix linking programme competencies, certification learning outcomes, module learning outcomes, and assessment tasks.

Before release, assessments must go through a four-eye check within the curriculum team to review validity, workload, inclusiveness, and clarity of language.

Assessment instructions and rubrics should be published in the LMS at least 7 days before the start of a module.

The Examination Board advises on whether the range and variation of assessment types are sufficient to support valid and fair assessment of the intended learning outcomes.

### Article 3.2.1 Extension

Learners who are unable to meet a high-stake assessment deadline because of special circumstances may request an extension through the standard online form.

Extension requests must explain the relevant circumstances clearly.

Supporting evidence may be required where relevant.

Requests are handled in accordance with the applicable programme procedure, the TER, and the delegated responsibilities within the programme.

Where the request concerns a high-stake assessment, a progression decision, or the final project, the Examination Board may be involved or may decide the matter directly where required by the TER or the applicable programme procedure.

### Article 3.3 Determination and Announcement of Results

Assessment results are determined on the basis of communicated criteria and rubrics.

Assessments may be evaluated by an assessor, a peer, or an automated system based on a rubric, and feedback is recorded in the LMS.

For high-stake assessments, including the certification VIVA, portfolio review, and final project, a three-member panel conducts the assessment and records the decision.

LMS-based submission and time-stamping form part of the formal assessment process and portfolio evidence base.

All submissions, recordings, rubrics, and panel minutes are securely archived in accordance with the TER and GDPR requirements.

The Examination Board oversees compliance with assessment policy, resolves disputes, and handles irregularities and appeals.

In the final project, the final judgement is based on the project as a whole, including the written submission, supporting deliverables, and the oral presentation and defence.

### Article 3.4 Progression Principles

Formal progression decisions are taken at the relevant certification checkpoints and at programme completion.

The learner’s portfolio functions as the central evidence hub for progression decisions.

Each learning outcome within a certification should be supported by:  
a. at least three low-stake evidence points; and  
b. at least two medium-stake evidence points.

Progression requires that the relevant learning outcomes are demonstrated at or above the Sufficient level and that the required high-stake decision point, including the VIVA where applicable, is passed.

Conditional progression may be granted where a learner has a limited shortfall that can be remedied within a defined period and where progression remains justifiable.

No progress may be decided where development is insufficient across multiple low- and medium-stake data points or where remediation conditions are not met.

By the end of the programme, every learning outcome must be passed. Failure to do so precludes the award of the diploma.

High-stake progression decisions are safeguarded by a three-assessor panel, and disputes are resolved by absolute majority vote.

### Article 3.5 Improvement, Resubmission, and Reconsideration

Feedback is a continuous and multi-directional part of the assessment process. It:  
a. clarifies the expected standard;  
b. explains current performance;  
c. outlines next steps for improvement.

Feedback may come from faculty, peers, and learner reflection, depending on the nature of the assessment.

Formal feedback is used especially at medium- and high-stake moments to inform progression decisions.

Formal feedback is:  
a. linked directly to learning outcomes;  
b. structured  
c. written and archived in the LMS.

Where revision or resubmission is allowed, it takes place according to the relevant programme procedures and timelines.

In the final project, feedback and revision may form part of the process, and only students whose written submission is judged sufficient to proceed may move to the oral presentation and defence.

### Article 3.10 Certificates and Diploma

A learner is awarded a certification when:  
a. all core learning outcomes for that certification are demonstrated at or above the Sufficient level;  
b. the certification VIVA panel records a direct progression;  
c. any previous conditional items have been cleared.

A learner is eligible for the diploma only if the learner:  
a. passes all certifications;  
b. completes and defends the final project;  
c. achieves at least Sufficient on the final project criteria;  
d. passes the Final VIVA / QA session.

After the panel decision, the Examination Board conducts the final TER compliance check and formally confers the diploma.

## Section 7: Hardship Clause

If demonstrable unreasonableness or unfairness of a predominant nature occurs in the application of this TER, the Executive Board or the Examination Board may decide to deviate from one or more provisions of these regulations.

A decision under the hardship clause is reasoned and communicated in writing to the learner concerned.

## APPENDIX A: Academic Appeals Procedure

This appendix explains the academic appeals procedure for learners in the Bachelor of Science in Applied Computing. It should be read together with the applicable TER, the Assessment Policy, and the Rules and Guidelines of the Examination Board. Where those documents conflict, the TER prevails.

### 1\. What an academic appeal is

An academic appeal is a procedure through which a learner may request a review of an academic decision relating to assessment, progression, certification, or award.

A learner may submit an appeal, for example, in relation to:  
a. a progression decision that prevents continuation in the programme;  
b. a decision requiring interruption of studies;  
c. a decision not to award a certification or diploma;  
d. another academic decision that materially affects progression or graduation.

In accordance with the TER, a learner may appeal where there is reason to believe that:  
a. a procedural irregularity occurred;  
b. prejudice or bias affected the outcome; or  
c. another material error affected the decision.

A learner may not appeal solely because the result is lower than expected or because they disagree with the academic judgement of the assessor.

### 2\. Grounds for appeal

Acceptable grounds for appeal include:  
a. circumstances that materially affected the learner’s performance and were not known to the relevant academic body at the time of the decision and could not reasonably have been made known earlier;  
b. evidence that a procedural irregularity occurred in the assessment or decision-making process and that this may have affected the result;  
c. evidence of prejudice or bias on the part of evaluators or within the assessment process.

A procedural irregularity may include, for example:  
a. an error in grading;  
b. an administrative mistake;  
c. incorrect calculation of marks;  
d. failure to consider mitigating circumstances submitted on time.

Where the appeal is based on personal or medical circumstances, the learner is expected to have notified the programme as early as reasonably possible and to provide supporting documentation. Unless there are very good reasons why this could not be done at the time, an appeal may be disallowed.

### 3\. Evidence

Any claim made in an appeal must normally be supported by evidence.

Acceptable evidence may include:  
a. medical certificates;  
b. statements from academic staff about specific events;  
c. copies of relevant regulations;  
d. detailed personal statements;  
e. confirmation from relevant authorities or third parties where applicable.

Contemporaneous, independent, and medical evidence generally carries greater weight.

Evidence should clearly indicate the significance of the impact on the learner and relate to the relevant period.

It is the learner’s responsibility to obtain and submit the evidence. The institution will not contact doctors, employers, or other persons on the learner’s behalf.

### 4\. How to submit an appeal

Appeals must be submitted in writing to the Examination Board through the Programme Manager, unless another formal route is specified in the applicable regulations or policies.

In line with the institutional appeal procedure adapted from the master appendix, the learner should complete the official Academic Appeal form and submit it together with supporting evidence.

The deadline for submission is 10 institute working days from the date on which the relevant result or decision was released. Appeals submitted after this deadline will normally be disallowed.

Programme Managers may support learners in understanding the procedure, but may not draft the appeal on the learner’s behalf.

Medical evidence must come from a qualified practitioner. If the learner is residing outside the Netherlands, medical evidence should be submitted in English or accompanied by a certified translation.

The institution may conduct checks to verify the authenticity of submitted evidence.

### 5\. Initial review of the appeal

The Examination Board first assesses whether the appeal contains eligible grounds.

To support this decision, the Examination Board may request information, clarification, or a report from the relevant faculty member, assessor, panel member, or Programme Manager.

If the appeal does not demonstrate eligible grounds, the learner will be informed in writing and may, where appropriate, be invited to provide additional information within a stated timeframe.

If the learner believes that the refusal to proceed was itself procedurally irregular or that evidence was not fully considered, the learner may request a review of that decision.

### 6\. Academic Appeal Panel / hearing

Academic appeals are initially handled by the Examination Board.

Learners do not attend Examination Board meetings as a matter of course.

If the Examination Board considers it necessary, it may invite:  
a. the learner;  
b. the involved educator, assessor, or other relevant person  
to a hearing.

Cases are normally considered on the basis of the documentation submitted.

### 7\. Appeal decision

The learner will normally receive formal written notification of the outcome within 7 working days after the relevant panel meeting or final consideration.

The decision letter will explain the reasons for the outcome.

If the learner believes that a procedural irregularity occurred in the handling of the appeal or that relevant evidence was not fully considered, the learner may request a review in accordance with the institutional procedure.

If the learner wishes to appeal a final decision of the Examination Board, the learner may do so through the independent NRTO disputes and appeals procedure where applicable.

Once the internal appeal procedure has been completed, the learner will receive written confirmation that the institutional procedure has been concluded.

## APPENDIX B: Academic Misconduct Consequences Guidelines

Academic integrity is essential to the quality, credibility, and trustworthiness of the Bachelor of Science in Applied Computing. Within a competency-based and programmatic assessment model, learners continuously build evidence of their development. Academic misconduct undermines the authenticity and validity of that evidence and may therefore affect progression, certification, final project decisions, and diploma decisions.

### 1\. What counts as academic misconduct

Academic misconduct includes any act or omission that prevents an accurate assessment of the learner’s own knowledge, understanding, skills, or professional conduct.

This includes, but is not limited to:  
a. plagiarism;  
b. fraud;  
c. unauthorised copying;  
d. submitting another person’s work as one’s own;  
e. falsification of evidence;  
f. fabrication of data, sources, or results;  
g. duplicate evidence or self-plagiarism;  
h. collusion on work designated as individual;  
i. conspiracy or facilitation of another learner’s misconduct;  
j. misrepresentation;  
k. improper use of AI tools or other digital tools without required disclosure, accountability, or permission;  
l. improper online behaviour in relation to assessment.

Plagiarism applies to text, code, images, designs, models, datasets, and all other forms of evidence.

In the final project, misconduct may also include unauthorised collaboration, misrepresentation of authorship, or falsification of findings or outputs.

### 2\. Why academic misconduct is serious

Academic misconduct undermines:  
a. the integrity of the programme;  
b. the value of degrees and certifications;  
c. fairness and equity between learners;  
d. research integrity and professional trustworthiness;  
e. ethical and professional formation;  
f. confidence in progression and graduation decisions;  
g. the institution’s legal and accreditation obligations.

Team Academy therefore treats academic misconduct as a serious matter that may affect both individual assessment outcomes and high-stake academic decisions.

### 3\. Reporting and investigation procedure

If an assessor has good reason to suspect plagiarism or academic misconduct, the matter must be reported immediately to the Programme Manager and the Examination Board.

The report must be made in writing and should include:  
a. the learner’s name;  
b. the name of the assessment or evidence concerned;  
c. a detailed description of the suspected misconduct;  
d. any relevant supporting material.

The Programme Manager may request clarification from the assessor if needed.

The learner must be informed of the allegation and invited to respond.

In line with the adapted institutional procedure, the Programme Manager should normally invite the learner to a hearing within one working day after receiving the report, and the hearing should normally take place within three working days.

The Programme Manager records the findings of the hearing and forwards:  
a. the assessor’s report;  
b. the Programme Manager’s report;  
c. any relevant supporting material;  
d. a non-binding advice  
to the Examination Board.

The Examination Board makes the final institutional decision on the case.

### 4\. Decision-making

The Examination Board must give the learner the right to:  
a. be informed of the allegation;  
b. review the evidence where appropriate;  
c. provide a written explanation;  
d. be heard by the Examination Board or a delegated committee where required.

In line with the adapted institutional procedure, the Examination Board should normally make a final decision within two weeks after receiving the relevant reports and evidence.

The final decision must be communicated in writing to the Programme Manager, who then informs the learner and the relevant assessor.

### 5\. Sanctions

Sanctions are determined proportionally, taking into account:

the seriousness of the misconduct;

the learner’s intent;

the impact on the validity of the evidence;

the stage of the programme;

whether there has been previous misconduct.

#### Level 1: Formal Warning

A formal written warning recorded in the learner’s file.

This level is appropriate where:  
a. it is a first occurrence;  
b. the misconduct is minor;  
c. the misconduct was accidental or due to misunderstanding;  
d. the impact on evidence validity is limited and does not materially affect progression or certification decisions.

A Level 1 sanction may be accompanied by formative guidance or an integrity coaching conversation.

#### Level 2: Evidence Invalidation and Additional Learning Requirements

Evidence affected by the misconduct is declared invalid and cannot be used in progression, certification, or graduation decisions.

The learner must provide new, corrected, or replacement evidence within a specified period.

This level is appropriate where:  
a. the misconduct affects the validity of one or more evidence points;  
b. the misconduct is more than minor, or repeated;  
c. the misconduct was deliberate but limited in scope;  
d. the learner can still reasonably remediate within the cohort or certification structure.

Consequences may include:  
a. additional or replacement evidence;  
b. an alternative task;  
c. loss of the opportunity to use certain AI or digital tools for designated tasks;  
d. increased supervision or monitoring.

#### Level 3: Certification or Progression Impact

This level applies where the misconduct materially compromises the learner’s ability to demonstrate the required outcomes within the current certification or progression period.

Consequences may include:  
a. postponement of the certification or progression decision;  
b. requirement to continue evidence development in the next certification period;  
c. requirement to retake the relevant certification or phase with a later cohort;  
d. additional conditions attached to the retake, such as a reflective report or increased supervision.

Previously submitted evidence that remains valid may continue to be used unless misconduct occurred in that evidence.

#### Level 4: Suspension or Exclusion

This level applies in cases of very serious misconduct that severely compromises integrity, trust, or safety.

Consequences may include:  
a. temporary suspension from the programme;  
b. removal from learning activities, projects, or internships;  
c. temporary loss of access to systems or tools;  
d. permanent exclusion from the programme in the gravest cases;  
e. notation in the diploma supplement in the gravest cases where institutionally permitted;  
f. legal action where relevant, such as document forgery.

### 6\. Repeated misconduct

In case of repeated misconduct, the Programme Management may, after consultation with the Examination Board, request permanent termination of the learner’s registration.

Such a request must be supported by a documented file containing the relevant facts, assessments, and prior decisions.

The learner must receive a formal written notice of the decision.

### 7\. Appeal against a misconduct decision

A learner may appeal a misconduct decision in accordance with the programme’s academic appeals procedure.

In line with the adapted institutional procedure, the learner may submit a written appeal to the Chair of the Examination Board within six weeks after announcement of the decision.

The Examination Board will acknowledge and handle the complaint or request within a reasonable period. In the adapted master procedure, the general term is a maximum of six weeks, extendable once by four weeks if necessary.

Where applicable, the learner may subsequently use the independent NRTO disputes and appeals procedure.
