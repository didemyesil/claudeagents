## **Bachelor of Science in Applied Computing**

  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  

# **1. INTRODUCTION**

## **1.1 What this guide is for**

This guide explains how you will complete your final project in the Bachelor of Science in Applied Computing. It describes the purpose of the project, the two project phases, the timeline, the deliverables, the assessment process, and the main rules that apply during graduation.

You should use this guide together with the relevant templates, assessment forms, reporting instructions, and any track-specific guidance that applies to your project. The final project belongs to one Applied Computing programme across the three tracks and Team Academy contexts.

## **1.2 What your final project is**

Your final project is the concluding bachelor-level assignment of the programme. In it, you will show that you can perform independently in a relevant professional context and at the required higher professional education level.

Your final project is not only a technical build. It is an integrated professional assignment in which you will analyse a relevant challenge, define and justify a project direction, design and develop an appropriate digital solution, evaluate the results, and reflect critically on both the outcome and your own professional practice.

Your project will take place in a real or realistic professional context. This may be a Team Academy project or another approved external context. In all cases, your project must address an authentic need, involve relevant stakeholders, and lead to a substantiated professional contribution.

## **1.3 What kind of project you will do**

Your final project is an individual graduation project. You may work within a wider team, organisation, or Team Academy setting, but you will define, execute, document, and defend your own project. Your assessment will therefore focus on your own contribution, your own judgement, and your own demonstrated level.

Your project must fit the profile of Applied Computing, be relevant to your chosen track or professional context, and be feasible within the available timeframe. Before you move into implementation, your project topic, scope, and intended outputs must be approved.

  

# **2. PROGRAMME OUTCOMES YOU WILL DEMONSTRATE**

Your final project is one of the main moments in the programme where you will demonstrate the exit level of Applied Computing. You will do this in an integrated way across the six programme outcomes below.

|  |  |
| :-: | :-: |
| \*\*Programme outcome\*\* | \*\*What you will demonstrate\*\* |
| Analyse | You will inquire into and analyse a complex professional challenge and define justified directions for solution development. |
| Advise | You will provide substantiated advice to relevant stakeholders based on findings, alternatives, and implications. |
| Design | You will design a fit-for-purpose digital solution that responds to the professional context. |
| Build | You will implement, test, refine, and validate a professional digital product or solution. |
| Manage & Control | You will organise and monitor your work, make responsible decisions, and reflect on your development and impact. |
| Communicate & Collaborate | You will communicate effectively with stakeholders and act responsibly in professional interaction and collaboration. |

# **3. ACTION INQUIRY IN YOUR FINAL PROJECT**

Action Inquiry is a methodological foundation of your final project. Throughout the project, you will learn systematically from action by working with the cycle **Plan – Act – Observe – Reflect**. This helps you make decisions explicitly, gather evidence, and improve both your project and your professional practice.

In your final project, you will work with three connected levels of inquiry. You will reflect on your own actions and assumptions. You will learn through purposeful dialogue with stakeholders. Where relevant, you will also learn from the wider team or organisational context in which your project is situated.

This means that your final project is not only about building something. It is also about showing ownership, judgement, and learning in action.

# **4. HOW YOUR FINAL PROJECT IS STRUCTURED**

Your final project has two phases: the **Design Phase** and the **Implementation Phase**.

In the Design Phase, you will define and justify your project in its professional context. You will explore the challenge, engage stakeholders, formulate your inquiry question, define scope and requirements, and prepare the project for implementation.

In the Implementation Phase, you will develop, test, evaluate, and complete your solution in the professional context. You will work iteratively and continue using Action Inquiry to improve both your project and your own professional practice.

# **5. TIMELINE OF YOUR FINAL PROJECT**

By the end of Season 3, you should identify the professional context in which you want to carry out your final project. This context may be a Team Academy project or another external professional context. It serves as the professional practice context required for graduation, provided that it meets the programme’s expectations for relevance, scope, authenticity, and learning opportunities.

## **5.2 Implementation Phase**

### **Duration: 21 weeks**

During the Implementation Phase, you will carry out your project in the professional context. You will build iteratively, gather evidence from practice, monitor quality and progress, reflect on what emerges, and refine your solution accordingly.

|  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| \*\*Period\*\* | \*\*Main focus\*\* | \*\*What you will do\*\* |
| Weeks 1–4 | Act | You will begin implementation and develop the first working parts of your solution. |
| Weeks 5–8 | Observe | You will continue development and gather evidence from the product, stakeholder conversations, and context. |
| Weeks 9–10 | Reflect | You will review progress, interpret what is emerging, and adjust your direction. |
| Weeks 11–14 | Iterate | You will refine and extend your solution based on what you have learned. |
| Weeks 15–17 | Observe and validate | You will assess the quality, usefulness, and readiness of your solution against requirements and stakeholder needs. |
| Weeks 18–21 | Reflect and complete | You will finalise your reflection, report, submission package, and final presentation or VIVA. |

This implementation structure follows the Action Inquiry logic of acting, observing, reflecting, and improving.

# **6. HOW YOUR FINAL PROJECT WILL BE ASSESSED**

Your final project is assessed as a competency-based graduation assignment. This means that your work is judged against the programme outcomes and expected bachelor level, not only against one final product. This is consistent with Team Academy’s wider assessment approach, which is competency-based and evidence-based.

Your final project also adopts selected elements of programmatic assessment. This means that your final judgement is based on multiple pieces of evidence gathered across time, not on one single assessment moment only. In your final project, that evidence may include your design deliverables, inquiry reflections, stakeholder validation, implementation artefacts, final report, and final VIVA.

Throughout the project, you will therefore build a coherent body of evidence that shows your reasoning, your development, and the quality of your final performance.

# **7. STAGE 1 DELIVERABLES**

During Stage 1, you will prepare and justify your project for implementation. Your work in this phase will be assessed through the deliverables below.

|  |  |
| :-: | :-: |
| \*\*Deliverable\*\* | \*\*Weight\*\* |
| Project Proposal | 10% |
| Project Plan | 20% |
| Requirements Specification | 25% |
| Design Specification | 25% |
| Reflective Assignments on the Inquiry Process | 10% |
| Presentation / VIVA | 10% |

### **What you will submit in Stage 1**

You will write a **Project Proposal** in which you define the professional context, the challenge, the purpose of the project, and the intended contribution.

You will prepare a **Project Plan** in which you explain how you will organise, manage, and monitor the project.

You will develop a **Requirements Specification** in which you define and justify the requirements that guide the project.

You will produce a **Design Specification** in which you explain the proposed solution direction and the main design choices.

You will complete **Reflective Assignments on the Inquiry Process** in which you show how your learning, stakeholder engagement, and observations shaped your project direction.

Finally, you will present and defend your proposal in a **Presentation or VIVA**. This is the point at which you show that your project is relevant, focused, feasible, and ready for implementation.

# **8. STAGE 2 EVIDENCE**

During Stage 2, you will implement, validate, document, and complete your project. Your final assessment will be based on the body of evidence you produce in this phase together with the approved outcome of Stage 1.

|  |  |
| :-: | :-: |
| \*\*Evidence component\*\* | \*\*What it shows\*\* |
| Implemented solution and technical artefacts | What you built and how it responds to the challenge and requirements |
| Validation and testing evidence | How you evaluated quality, usefulness, and readiness |
| Final report | How you document the challenge, inquiry process, development, results, evaluation, and reflection |
| Action Inquiry reflections | How you learned through action, observation, feedback, and iteration |
| Final presentation or VIVA | How you explain and defend your project and its results |

This reflects the expectation that graduation includes more than a repository and requires a formal report and a final high-stakes judgement based on multiple evidence points.

# **10. SUPPORT DURING YOUR PROJECT**

During your final project, you will receive structured support that helps you work independently while continuing to develop professionally.

You may receive supervision sessions, team coaching, mid-VIVA meetings. The exact format may differ by track, cohort, or project context, but you remain responsible for your own progression and performance.

Your supervisors will support you by discussing your progress, challenging your thinking, giving feedback, and monitoring whether your project remains at the required level and scope. They will not take ownership of your project for you.

# **11. SUBMISSION AND PROCEDURE**

You will submit your deliverables through the designated programme systems and according to the communicated deadlines. A submission is only complete when all required components have been submitted in the required form.

You are expected to meet all deadlines. If special circumstances affect your ability to submit on time, you must communicate this as early as possible and follow the applicable programme procedure for extensions or special circumstances.

Oral assessment moments such as a VIVA will be organised in a clear and transparent way. During such moments, you will present your work, answer questions, explain your decisions, and demonstrate ownership of your project. Where required, authorship and identity checks may form part of this process.

# **12. PASS STANDARD**

You will only proceed from Stage 1 to Stage 2 if your Stage 1 work shows that your project is professionally relevant, sufficiently complex, inquiry-driven, and feasible for implementation.

Your final project will only be passed when the assessors judge that you have demonstrated the required bachelor-level performance across the relevant criteria and programme outcomes. The final decision will be based on the complete evidence set of your graduation process, including your report, your project artefacts, your validation evidence, and your final VIVA.

This is the high-stakes judgement in your graduation process. In line with the programmatic assessment logic adopted here, that judgement is informed by multiple evidence points collected across time rather than by one isolated assessment moment.

# **13. FEEDBACK, REVISION, AND RESUBMISSION**

Throughout your final project, you will receive feedback that supports both your learning and the quality of your project. Some feedback moments are mainly formative and help you improve while you are working. Other feedback moments are more formal and contribute to decisions about progression or final judgement.

Where revision or resubmission is allowed, this will take place according to the relevant programme procedures and timelines. If you are asked to revise your work, you are expected to respond to feedback in a clear and responsible way.

# **14. YOUR FINAL REPORT**

Your final report will document your project as an integrated professional bachelor-level assignment. It is not only a description of what you built. It is a substantiated account of the challenge, the inquiry process, the development process, the results, the evaluation, and your professional learning.

Your report should make visible the professional context and relevance of the project, the way you defined the problem, the requirements and design choices, the implementation and validation process, the quality of the final result, your judgement and reflection, and the role of Action Inquiry in your process.

# **15. FINAL ASSESSMENT**

Your final assessment evaluates your project as a whole. This includes the quality of your deliverables, your final product or solution, your report, your defence, and the professional performance you demonstrate throughout the project.

The final assessment will focus on whether you demonstrate bachelor-level performance, achievement of the programme outcomes, a justified and professionally relevant process, sufficient technical and professional quality, reflective and responsible practice, and clear communication of your choices and results.

The official criteria, rubrics, and grading rules linked to this guide determine the exact pass decision.

# **16. INTEGRITY, ETHICS, AND USE OF TECHNOLOGY**

Throughout your final project, you are expected to act with integrity. This includes responsible handling of information, transparent use of sources, honest reporting of work completed, and proper acknowledgement of contributions made by others.

You must also take relevant ethical considerations into account, including privacy and data protection, responsible use of AI and data, professional standards in communication, and the broader impact of your solution in its context.

You may use technology, including AI-supported tools, where this is allowed and appropriate, but you remain responsible for the quality, correctness, authorship, and integrity of your work.

# **17. APPEALS, IRREGULARITIES, AND MISCONDUCT**

If there is doubt about the authenticity, integrity, or fairness of any part of your assessment, the matter may be reviewed through the applicable programme and Examination Board procedures.

Academic misconduct may include plagiarism, submitting work that is not your own, misrepresenting the use of AI tools, unauthorised collaboration, or falsifying evidence or results. If you disagree with a formal assessment outcome, the relevant appeal and review procedures of the programme and Examination Board apply.

# **18. QUALITY ASSURANCE OF THE FINAL PROJECT**

The quality of your final project assessment is safeguarded through the programme’s wider quality assurance approach. This includes alignment between outcomes, deliverables, and criteria, the use of explicit rubrics, review and improvement of assessment materials, and oversight by the Examination Board.

This follows Team Academy’s broader assessment approach, in which assessment quality is monitored through cycles of design, implementation, evaluation, feedback, and revision.

## **Appendix 5: Final Project Report**

### **Introduction**

Your final project report presents and justifies your final bachelor project in Applied Computing. It should clearly explain the professional context, the challenge addressed, the approach taken, the solution developed, the results achieved, and the lessons learned.

The purpose of the report is to help the reader understand:

  - what you did
  - why you did it
  - how you approached it
  - what results you achieved
  - what you learned from the process
  - what the project means for future practice or development

Write clearly, logically, and professionally. Use simple and precise language. The report should be understandable to readers from the programme, the professional field, and the assessment panel.

The report should be about **6000 words** in total. The word counts below are indicative and may be adjusted where appropriate. You may use tables, screenshots, diagrams, graphs, or other figures to support your explanation, but these should support the report rather than replace it.

### **1. Project Context and Direction (approximately 1500 words)**

#### **1.1 Motivation and Relevance**

Explain the background and motivation for the project. Describe why the project is relevant in its professional context and why it is worth addressing.

You may include:

  - the professional context
  - the origin of the project idea
  - the relevance for stakeholders or users
  - your personal or professional motivation

#### **1.2 Project Definition and Inquiry**

Explain how the project was defined in the design phase. Describe how you explored the challenge, involved stakeholders, and arrived at the project direction.

You may include:

  - the main inquiry question
  - stakeholder or user input
  - how the project proposal was developed
  - how the requirements or design direction were established

#### **1.3 Scope and Objectives**

Clearly state the scope and objectives of the project. Explain what the project set out to achieve, what is included, and what is outside the scope.

### **2. Approach and Development Process (approximately 1500 words)**

#### **2.1 Methodology and Working Approach**

Describe the methods and approaches used during the project. Explain why these were appropriate for the project goals and context.

You may include:

  - the development approach
  - project management approach
  - relevant methods, tools, or frameworks
  - how Action Inquiry informed the work through planning, action, observation, and reflection

#### **2.2 Data Collection, Input, or Evidence Base**

Explain what information, feedback, data, or other input informed your project. This section should fit the nature of your project.

You may include:

  - stakeholder interviews or consultations
  - user feedback or testing input
  - technical findings or prior analysis
  - relevant datasets, sources, or documentation
  - challenges encountered in gathering or using this input

#### **2.3 Iterative Development and Decision-Making**

Describe how the project developed over time. Explain important changes, adjustments, and design or implementation decisions, including why these were made.

#### **2.4 Collaboration and Professional Interaction**

Describe the collaboration and communication involved in the project. Even in an individual project, explain how you worked with clients, users, supervisors, or other stakeholders.

You may include:

  - communication with stakeholders
  - feedback moments
  - collaboration in the project context
  - how this influenced project progress or outcomes

### **3. Solution, Results, and Evaluation (approximately 2000 words)**

#### **3.1 Solution Description**

Present the final digital solution clearly and concisely. Describe the main components, features, or outputs of the project.

#### **3.2 Results Overview**

Present the main results of the project in a clear and organised way. Use figures, tables, screenshots, or charts where relevant.

Ensure that the results are connected to:

  - the project objectives
  - the requirements
  - the intended users or stakeholders
  - the professional context

#### **3.3 Evaluation, Successes, and Challenges**

Evaluate the solution and the project outcomes. Discuss what worked well, what challenges occurred, and how you responded to them.

You may include:

  - testing or validation outcomes
  - feedback from users or stakeholders
  - evidence of whether requirements were met
  - scope changes or compromises
  - lessons from setbacks as well as successes

#### **3.4 Reflection on Learning**

Reflect on what you learned through the project. This may include technical learning, professional development, project management, communication, or judgement in practice.

## **Appendix 7: Evaluation Process of the Final Project**

The final project is evaluated through a structured assessment process. This process is intended to ensure that the student’s work is reviewed fairly, consistently, and in line with the programme outcomes, the final project requirements, and the expected bachelor-level standard.

The evaluation process includes:

  - a formal submission check
  - review of the submitted work by assessors
  - feedback and, where needed, a revision opportunity
  - an oral presentation and question-and-answer session
  - a final holistic judgement on the project as a whole

### **1. Submission of the final project**

The student submits the final project through the designated programme procedure and by the stated deadline.

The final submission must include all required components of the final project. These include the final report and the required supporting deliverables relevant to the project.

### **2. Formal submission check**

After submission, the Programme Manager checks whether:

  - all required components have been submitted
  - the required format has been followed
  - the submission is complete
  - the submission deadline has been met

The Programme Manager records any missing items or formal issues and communicates these as part of the assessment process.

### **3. Review by assessors**

If the submission is complete, the final project is passed to the assessors.

The assessors review the submitted work against:

  - the final project requirements
  - the relevant guidelines and templates
  - the programme outcomes
  - the assessment criteria for the final project
  - the expected bachelor-level standard

The assessors review the project as a whole. Their task is not only to check individual parts, but also to consider whether the submission demonstrates sufficient quality, coherence, and professional level.

### **4. Feedback and opportunity for improvement**

Following the review, the student receives feedback from the assessors.

If improvements are required before the student can proceed, the student may be given the opportunity to revise the submission within a set deadline.

This revision opportunity is intended to support improvement in response to feedback and to strengthen the final project where necessary.

### **5. Preliminary judgement**

After reviewing the original or revised submission, the assessors determine whether the project is of sufficient quality to proceed to the oral presentation.

At this stage, the judgement is expressed as:

  - sufficient to proceed
  - not yet sufficient to proceed

Only students whose written submission is judged sufficient to proceed may move to the oral presentation and defence.

### **6. Oral presentation and defence**

The oral presentation takes place after the written submission has been judged sufficient to proceed.

During the oral presentation and question-and-answer session, the student is expected to:

  - present the project clearly and professionally
  - explain and justify key choices and decisions
  - demonstrate understanding of the project, its context, and its outcomes
  - reflect on the process, the results, and their own professional learning

The oral component forms part of the final judgement of the project.

### **7. Final judgement**

After the oral presentation and defence, the assessors determine the final outcome of the final project.

The final judgement is based on the project as a whole, including:

  - the written submission
  - the supporting deliverables
  - the oral presentation and defence

The final judgement is expressed holistically and is not based on a single numerical score for one isolated component.

### **8. Role separation and fairness**

The final project assessment must be carried out with clear role separation and fairness in judgement.

Those involved in the assessment process are expected to distinguish clearly between:

  - support during the project process
  - formal judgement during assessment

The final judgement must be based on the submitted evidence and the oral defence, and not on informal impressions or prior supervisory contact alone.

### **9. Communication of the outcome**

The student is informed of the final outcome after the assessment process has been completed in accordance with programme procedures.

The student also receives the relevant feedback and assessment documentation connected to that outcome.

### **Notes for students**

Students should be aware that:

  - a complete submission is required for the assessment process to begin
  - feedback and revision may form part of the process
  - progression to the oral presentation depends on the written submission being sufficient
  - the final judgement is based on the project as a whole
  - the final project is assessed in relation to programme standards and expected bachelor-level performance
