## The shape of a deep pass

A standard is ready for production when its map answers four things. Standard 1
below is the worked example for the nine that follow.

**1. The appendices this standard requires.** Named documents, not evidence
types. Several Guidelines requirements and Sample Questions usually resolve to
one real document — group them. For each: its scope from the scope register
(`INSTITUTION`, `MASTERS-LEVEL`, `BACHELORS`, `PROGRAMME`) and its status:

- *on file* — exists, cite it as it stands
- *needs adapting* — exists institution-wide or for another programme, has to
  be extended or issued for the one that lacks it
- *to draft* — does not exist and must be written

**2. Absences, classified.** Every absence is one of three kinds, handled
differently:

- *a missing document* — the practice exists but is not written down. It gets
  produced. Never reported as a gap.
- *a missing institutional fact or decision* — only Didem can supply it. Write
  the specific question, not a general flag.
- *a missing track record* — the mechanism is adopted but has not run, because
  the programme has not launched. Legitimate ex-ante; noted so that nobody
  tries to manufacture a document for it.

**3. Decisions taken.** Questions that have been answered, with the answer, so
that no later standard re-asks what this one settled.

**4. Severity**, and what drives it.

Two rules hold throughout. A cluster-wide fact gets **one** appendix, never one
per programme — if a document's scope covers several programmes, that means one
file. And an absence left unclassified becomes a hedge in the report, which is
what this pipeline exists to prevent.

  14 ("Does the EO have a gender equality/equal opportunities concept?").

  full evidence pool for "gender," "equal opportunit*," "discrimination

  `folder-developer`'s three-case rule, not a remaining open question).

- **QA policy revision-history / stakeholder-satisfaction assessment
