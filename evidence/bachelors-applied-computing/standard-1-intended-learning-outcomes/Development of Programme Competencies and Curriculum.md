# Development of the Programme Competencies & Curriculum in the Bachelor's in Applied Computing

## Introduction

This document explains how the competencies and curriculum of the Bachelor's in Applied Computing were developed. The programme was designed as a multi-track HBO bachelor within the applied ICT domain and consists of the following tracks: Applied Software Engineering, Applied Data Science, Applied AI / Machine Learning.

The name **Applied Computing** was chosen because it reflects the HBO focus of the programme and the evolving nature of the field — practice, professional application, and relevance in real contexts, as AI broadens and reshapes the role of the programmer.

Five stages were followed to develop programme competencies and curriculum:

1. Desktop Research on Industry Needs
2. Benchmark Analysis
3. Framework Integration
4. Internal Competency Development
5. External Validation of the Programme Tracks

## Stage 1: Desktop Research on Industry Needs

- **Labour-market demand** — EU ICT specialists (software, AI, data science) grew 62.2% between 2014–2024; in the Netherlands ICT specialists were 6.9% of employment in 2023, yet the 2025 country report still warns of shortages.
- **Future roles and skill shifts** — WEF: by 2030, 22% of jobs disrupted, 170M new roles, 92M displaced; Big Data Specialists, AI/ML Specialists, and Software/Applications Developers among fastest-growing roles; 86% of employers expect AI/information-processing technologies to transform their business by 2030.
- **Technical and human capability** — WEF: top core skills beyond analytical thinking include resilience, flexibility, agility, leadership and social influence — interpersonal capability valued alongside problem solving.
- **Need for a skills-based approach** — NACE (2025): students with experiential learning show faster career progression, higher satisfaction, stronger mentor/network access, and ~$15,000 higher average annual salary; NACE stresses connecting experiential learning explicitly to career outcomes and competencies.

**Conclusion:** the labour market requires graduates combining up-to-date technical capability (software, AI, data) with adaptable human skills. The programme responds by combining high-demand technical skills with interpersonal skills development via an action-inquiry approach (self-inquiry for self-leadership, one-to-one inquiry for interpersonal leadership, team inquiry for group leadership capacity), and adopts project-based learning reflecting professional practices (squads, weekly stand-ups).

## Stage 2: Benchmarking Analysis

- **Comparators:** Saxion University of Applied Sciences, Hanze University of Applied Sciences, Windesheim, University of Twente, NOVI.
- **Positioning:** most comparable HBO programmes offer a broad ICT structure or combine data science and AI into a single track; a clearly separated three-track structure (Software Engineering, Data Science, Machine Learning) is uncommon in the Dutch context — a potential differentiator.
- **Industry engagement:** leading HBO programmes (Saxion, Hanze) integrate real client/industry collaboration from early curriculum stages; partner organisation *Tech Me Up* (network of ~20 companies) will strengthen industry engagement via hackathons and employer meet-ups.
- **Common foundations:** all comparators start broad before specialising — Data Science/ML foundations: programming, statistics, data handling, modelling; Software Engineering foundation: core programming, software design, system-level thinking.
- **Modularity and programmatic assessment:** Windesheim and other comparators show a strong shift toward modular education and flexible, evidence-based assessment (projects, reports, portfolios) rather than only traditional exams — supports the programme's modular learning outcomes + programmatic assessment choice.
- **Interpersonal skills as a distinctive feature:** comparators generally embed collaboration/teamwork within projects rather than as an explicit curriculum line — supports the programme's explicit, dedicated-module focus on interpersonal skills.
- **Pressure from alternative providers:** MOOCs/bootcamps emphasise transparent skills development, portfolio-based outcomes, and employability — reinforcing the need for higher education to demonstrate applied competencies clearly.

**Conclusion:** the programme should combine a shared foundational competency base with clearly differentiated tracks (especially Data Science vs AI/ML); maintain continuous industry engagement through project-based learning and partnerships; adopt modular/programmatic assessment; retain its explicit focus on interpersonal skills.

## Stage 3: Framework Integration

- **HBO-i as structuring framework** — the HBO-i Domain Description 2023 structures ICT education around professional activities (analyse, advise, design, realise, manage & control) plus architectural layers and professional skills; nationally recognised reference for HBO ICT exit qualifications.
- **Workflow logic** — the programme uses the professional workflow (analyse → advise → design → realise → manage & control) as the backbone for structuring competencies at bachelor level within HBO-i.
- **NLQF level 6 alignment** — competencies aligned with NLQF level 6 descriptors (advanced knowledge, problem-solving ability, responsibility, professional communication).
- **Programme outcomes** — Analyse, Advise, Design, Build, Manage & Control, and Communicate & Collaborate, integrating HBO-i professional tasks, HBO-i professional skills (investigative ability, targeted interaction, personal leadership), and NLQF skill categories.
- **"Communicate & Collaborate" made explicit** — though embedded in HBO-i/NLQF descriptors, the programme made this a separate, assessable outcome to reflect the importance of stakeholder interaction, teamwork, and professional responsibility.
- **"Build" vs "Realise"** — the programme uses "Build" as a more accessible term for its target group while aligning in content with HBO-i's "Realise" (implementation, testing, validation, iteration).
- Each programme outcome is explicitly mapped to HBO-i professional tasks, HBO-i professional skills, NLQF skill categories, and HBO-i bachelor proficiency level (Level 3).

**Conclusion:** a coherent set of programme outcomes fully aligned with both the HBO-i framework and NLQF level 6.

## Stage 4: Internal Competency Development by the Curriculum Team

- **Translation into track competencies** — industry analysis, benchmark findings, HBO-i integration, and NLQF level 6 alignment were translated into concrete competencies per track, reflecting the professional workflow (analyse, advise, design, build, manage & control).
- **Graduate profile** — across all tracks, graduates analyse, advise, design, build, and manage digital solutions in a professional context, communicating/collaborating effectively and taking responsibility for their work; a shared structure ensures consistency while allowing track-specific depth.
- **Track-specific competencies** — e.g. Software Engineering track: software requirements analysis, software architecture design, programming, software development, data structures and algorithms, process management.
- **Modular learning outcomes** — track-specific competencies translated into action-oriented, assessable learning outcomes (using Bloom's Taxonomy) focused on what students can do in a professional context.
- **Alignment with project-based learning** — learning outcomes achieved through authentic, track-specific projects combining multiple outcomes (analysing requirements, designing solutions, building/testing systems, managing development processes).
- **Agile and action inquiry integration** — learning environment structured around agile methods (sprints, stand-ups, retrospectives); action inquiry (self-inquiry, one-to-one inquiry, team inquiry) supports research and vertical leadership development through reflection, communication, and collaboration.
- **Constructive alignment** — competencies define intended level/scope, learning outcomes operationalise them into assessable elements, and project-based learning provides the context for demonstrating and developing them in an integrated format.

**Conclusion:** a coherent, structured set of track-specific competencies and modular learning outcomes, aligned with recognised frameworks and grounded in professional practice, directly integrated into the project-based learning environment.
