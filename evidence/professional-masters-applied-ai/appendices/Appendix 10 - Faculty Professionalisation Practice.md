# Faculty Professionalisation Practice

### A Dynamic, Inquiry-Driven Approach to Professional Development

## Overview

We, as the programme team, value professional development because it strengthens our collective capacity for impactful leadership, growth, and enhances learner achievement. This commitment is in line with our Leadership Manifesto, which emphasises responsibility, collaboration, and shared leadership. By investing in the growth of our faculty, we aim to foster environments where leadership is accessible, relational, and driven by collective intelligence, and ultimately improves the learning experience and outcomes for our students.

For us, faculty development is **not a one-time event** but a **continuous, reflective, and action-based** process that strengthens our collective ability to **design impactful learning experiences, implement meaningful assessments, and facilitate student engagement**.

This professional development model is designed to be:
- **Inquiry-driven**: Faculty experiment, reflect, and iterate.
- **Collaborative**: Growth is shaped through shared leadership and collective learning.
- **Adaptable**: Faculty drive their own learning while aligning with institutional goals.

Our proposed plan empowers faculty to be reflective practitioners, adaptive educators and proactive professionals in competencies such as:

- curriculum design,
- assessment & giving effective feedback to learners,
- facilitative teaching.

## Intended Outcomes

Faculty members will be encouraged to:
- **Enhance their reflective practice** by continuously questioning, testing, and refining their teaching approaches.
- **Improve faculty practices in the target competencies,** such as assessment and giving effective feedback
- **Experiment with different methodologies or tools** to enhance student learning experiences
- **Strengthen collaboration and shared leadership** by engaging in faculty learning communities.

## The Professional Development Cycle

This model is **cyclical and non-linear** and allows faculty to move between phases as needed. Each phase includes **structured reflection, experimentation, and group learning**. Each step is facilitated by an experienced facilitator who meets the group once for each step.

Each cycle may address different areas of competencies. The cycle below aims to provide a sample process which can be adapted based on the focus of development. It could also be shortened based on the needs and timeline in the programme.

### 1. Strategic Intent & Outcomes: Setting the Foundation

- **Objective:** Align individual professional goals with institutional priorities.
- **Key Questions:**
  - *What kind of educator do I aspire to be?*
  - *How can I enhance student learning through curriculum design, assessment, or facilitation?*
  - *Where do I need to grow?*
- **Practice:**
  - Faculty self-assess their current teaching & assessment strategies.
  - Define key focus areas (e.g., assessment methods, facilitative teaching etc).
  - Set **strategic learning goals** that connect to student learning outcomes.
  - Use **peer mentoring** to refine these goals collaboratively.

### 2. Inquiry as a Dynamic Process: Experimenting & Learning

- **Objective:** Test new teaching or assessment methods through inquiry-driven learning.
- **Key Questions:**
  - *What changes can I experiment with to improve my teaching?*
  - *What can I learn further to improve my teaching?*
  - *How can I integrate different methods & tools into my practices effectively?*
  - *How do students respond to my methods?*
- **Practice:**
  - Faculty select a **specific challenge or opportunity** in their practice.
  - Engage in **micro-experiments**—small, testable changes in curriculum design, assessment, or student engagement strategies.
  - Use — for instance — AI-assisted feedback tools, new grading rubrics, or alternative instructional methods.
  - Take an online course & MOOC to learn more.
  - Capture observations through **teaching logs or faculty discussion groups**.

### 3. Practice in Action: Implementing & Observing Change

- **Objective:** Apply and analyse new strategies in real-world teaching.
- **Key Questions:**
  - *How are students responding to changes?*
  - *Are my teaching and assessment methods improving engagement and learning?*
  - *What adjustments need to be made?*
- **Practice:**
  - Faculty **implement changes** in their practices.
  - Use **student engagement data, peer observations, and self-reflection** to measure impact.
  - Observe behaviours and unintended consequences (e.g., student confusion, increased motivation).
  - Adjust teaching methods based on real-time observations.

### 4. Reflection & Learning: Analyzing Impact & Adjusting Strategies

- **Objective:** Evaluate outcomes and refine approaches.
- **Key Questions:**
  - *What worked? What didn't?*
  - *What insights can I apply to future teaching?*
  - *How can I share my learning with colleagues?*
- **Practice:**
  - Faculty analyse **data from student feedback or peer insights**.
  - Conduct **structured self-reflection** on teaching experiments.
  - Identify areas for **adjustment and future experimentation**.

### 5. Emergent Learning & Opportunities: Scaling & Sustaining Growth

- **Objective:** Move beyond personal practice to **collective faculty development**.
- **Key Questions:**
  - *How can I contribute to faculty learning and leadership?*
  - *What long-term innovations can we implement as a team?*
- **Practice:**
  - Faculty **mentor new colleagues** in their areas of expertise.
  - Engage in **collaborative projects**, such as designing new assessment methods in the AI-driven world.
  - **Lead workshops** to share innovative practices with peers.
  - Establish **sustained faculty inquiry groups** for long-term learning and support.
