## Standards / criteria list

### A — Initial accreditation, Extensive framework (source A, §3.3)

Ex-ante assessment of a new programme's plans. Standard 11 (achieved
learning outcomes) is only assessed "in exceptional cases, and if the
assessment concerns existing programme components" — most initial
accreditation runs stop at Standard 10 plus the final conclusion.

#### Standard 1 — Intended learning outcomes
"The intended learning outcomes tie in with the level and orientation of the
programme; they are geared to the expectations of the professional field,
the discipline, and international requirements."

Demonstrably describe the level (Associate Degree/Bachelor's/Master's per
the Dutch Qualifications Framework) and orientation (professional/academic).
Tie in with regional/national/international professional-field and
discipline requirements. In accordance with relevant legislation where
applicable. Chime with the institution's educational philosophy and profile.
Periodically evaluated.

#### Standard 2 — Curriculum: orientation
"The curriculum enables the students to master appropriate (professional or
academic) research and professional skills."

Ties in with current (international) developments, requirements and
expectations in the professional field and discipline. Academic/research
skills and/or professional competencies substantiated befitting the
programme's orientation and level.

#### Standard 3 — Curriculum: content
"The contents of the curriculum enable students to achieve the intended
learning outcomes."

Learning outcomes adequately translated into educational objectives of
(components of) the curriculum.

#### Standard 4 — Curriculum: learning environment
"The structure of the curriculum encourages study and enables students to
achieve the intended learning outcomes."

Designed to be conducive to achieving intended learning outcomes;
student-centred approach; chimes with the institution's educational
philosophy. Non-Dutch language of instruction (including a foreign-language
programme name) must be justified.

#### Standard 5 — Intake
"The curriculum ties in with the qualifications of the incoming students."

Admission requirements realistic with a view to the intended learning
outcomes.

#### Standard 6 — Staff
"The staff team is qualified for the realisation of the curriculum in terms
of content and educational expertise. The team size is sufficient."

Teachers have sufficient subject-matter and teaching-method expertise and
sufficient command of the language of instruction; staff policy conducive;
sufficient staff to teach and tutor students.

#### Standard 7 — Facilities
"The accommodation and material facilities are sufficient for the
realisation of the curriculum."

Accommodation and facilities keep pace with intended learning outcomes and
the teaching-learning environment.

#### Standard 8 — Tutoring
"The tutoring of and provision of information to students are conducive to
study progress and tie in with the needs of students."

Appropriate tutoring (including for students with a functional impairment);
adequate information provision.

#### Standard 9 — Quality assurance
"The programme has an explicit and widely supported quality assurance system
in place. It promotes the quality culture and has a focus on development."

Effective periodic feedback supporting achievement of intended learning
outcomes; evaluation/measurement activities feed development and
improvement; accountability for contribution to institutional strategic
goals; programme committee, examination board, staff, students, alumni, and
the relevant professional field actively involved; design/recognition/QA
processes keep with the European Standards and Guidelines (ESG); accurate,
accessible published quality information.

#### Standard 10 — Student assessment
"The programme has an adequate student assessment system in place."

Valid, reliable, sufficiently independent assessments; quality of interim
and final examinations statutorily safeguarded; examining board exerts its
legal authority; tests support students' own learning processes.

**Final conclusion** (weighted and substantiated).

#### Standard 11 — Achieved learning outcomes (exceptional cases only)
"The programme demonstrates that the intended learning outcomes are
achieved."

Only assessed if NVAO determines the initial accreditation procedure covers
existing programme components with final projects available for review —
same evidence/selection method as under the existing-programme framework
(minimum 15 recent graduates' final projects, or all if fewer, balanced and
diverse selection).

## Required evidence types (per standard, framework A)

| Standard no | Required evidence type |
|---|---|
| 1 | Info dossier narrative: intended learning outcomes vs. Dutch Qualifications Framework level/orientation, professional field/discipline requirements, relevant legislation |
| 2 | Info dossier + curriculum outline showing alignment with current (international) developments in the field/discipline |
| 3 | Curriculum outline mapping intended learning outcomes to educational objectives of curriculum components; first 60 ECs elaborated in full detail |
| 4 | Info dossier describing curriculum structure, student-centred design, draft teaching and examination regulations; language justification if not Dutch |
| 5 | Info dossier describing admission requirements and fit to intended learning outcomes |
| 6 | Composition of the (intended) staff team: qualifications, language proficiency, staff policy, team size |
| 7 | Info dossier description of accommodation/facilities relative to the learning environment (only assessed if programme-specific) |
| 8 | Info dossier description of intended student tutoring and information provision |
| 9 | Info dossier description of the intended QA system: evaluation/measurement plans, stakeholder involvement design, planned published quality info |
| 10 | Draft teaching and examination regulations; info dossier on intended assessment validity/reliability/independence and examining board authority; tests elaborated for several components |
| 11 | (exceptional only) Selection of ≥15 recent final projects, or all if fewer, diverse across modes/locations/specialisations |

## Format / presentation rules (framework A)

- Information dossier: **maximum 15 pages (limited framework) or 20 pages
  (extensive framework)**, excluding appendices.
- For the first 60 ECs of the programme: full information on content and
  student assessment must be available in draft form, with tests elaborated
  for several components.
- Site visit: in principle **one day**; panel meets the development
  team/intended teaching staff.
- Panel: minimum 4 members, at least one a higher-education student;
  independence requirements apply (no ties to the institution for 5+ years,
  no consultancy work for the programme for 5+ years).

## Verdict scale

NVAO's own terminology (source A, §3.3.3, extensive framework, initial
accreditation):

| NVAO term | Maps to system default |
|---|---|
| Meets the standard | Meets |
| Partially meets the standard | At Risk |
| Does not meet the standard | Does Not Meet |

NVAO's own **final-conclusion** combination rule (distinct from this
system's per-standard synthesis in Stage 4 — kept for reference when
comparing our mock report against NVAO's actual gatekeeping logic):

- **Positive**: programme meets all standards.
- **Conditionally positive**: programme meets Standard 1 and partially meets
  a maximum of **five** standards, with the panel recommending conditions
  (feasible to remedy within 2 years).
- **Negative**: programme fails one or more standards; OR partially meets
  Standard 1; OR partially meets one to five standards without conditions
  being recommended; OR partially meets six or more standards.

## Language / terminology notes

- Use **"programme"** (UK spelling), matching NVAO's own English
  translation — not "program."
- "Generic quality" is a defined term in source A: *"The quality that, from
  an international perspective, may reasonably be expected from a higher
  education Associate Degree, Bachelor's or Master's programme."* Use it,
  don't paraphrase it.
- Say **"intended learning outcomes,"** not "learning objectives."
- This is an **ex-ante** assessment — write about plans and pre-conditions,
  not achieved results, except where Standard 11 explicitly applies.
- The information dossier, not a "self-evaluation report," is the correct
  term for initial accreditation (the latter term belongs to the
  existing-programme framework).
- The Dutch text of source A is legally binding; source A's English config
  content is built from NVAO's own translation, which "has no formal
  status" per NVAO's own disclaimer.
