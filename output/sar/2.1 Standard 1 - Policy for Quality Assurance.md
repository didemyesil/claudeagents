**2.1 Standard 1. POLICY FOR QUALITY ASSURANCE**

*Institutions should have a policy for quality assurance that is made public and forms part of their strategic management. Internal stakeholders should develop and implement this policy through appropriate structures and processes, while involving external stakeholders.*

Team Academy has one policy for quality assurance, formally adopted by our Executive Board, applying to the institution as a whole and therefore to all three programmes in this cluster. Because the policy, the structures carrying it and the regulations operationalising it are common to the three, we describe them once. Two matters genuinely differ — the documented record of external stakeholder involvement and the maturity of the programme-level regulatory framework — and we report those per programme.

No programme has yet admitted a cohort. As this is an initial accreditation, the requirement for three to five years of achievement data does not apply. Where the standard concerns matters evidenced only through operation over time — revision history, satisfaction results, completed review cycles — we describe the mechanism we have adopted and state plainly that it is unexercised. We present no retrospective data, because we have none.

**Our policy and its formal status**

Our quality assurance policy is set out in the *Policy for Quality Assurance at Team Academy* (Annex 1.1). We structured it around the ten ESG standards, and it describes our quality assurance objectives, the organisation of our quality assurance system, how that system is evaluated, and the multi-layered approach through which we assure quality at activity, module, certification and programme level.

The policy has formal status in our governance. It is approved by the Executive Board and sits at the top of our documentary hierarchy: each programme's Teaching and Examination Regulations, Policy on Assessment and Examination Board Handbook refer back to it, and where a programme handbook conflicts with the Regulations, the Regulations prevail. We file the policy as an appendix under each programme; the three copies are substantively identical, differing only in the programme named in illustrative passages and, for the Bachelor's and the Tech MBA, in one paragraph mapping the generic activity, module, certification and programme levels onto that programme's curriculum.

On public availability we are direct. The policy is at present internal. We provide it to staff, faculty, the Educational Council, which represents our learners, and our Group of Field Representatives, but we have not published it on our website, because none of the three programmes has launched and there is as yet no published programme information for it to accompany. We have recorded in the policy, under ESG 1.8, our intention to publish it once the cluster is operational. We recognise that the guideline asks for a policy both formally adopted and publicly available, and that we satisfy the first condition and answer the second with a dated plan rather than present practice.

**How we organise quality assurance**

Our quality assurance system operates in two layers, described in section 1.1.4 of the policy and in the *Institution Organisational Structure* (Annex 1.2).

At institute level, we operate as a Recognised Legal Entity for Higher Education under the Dutch Higher Education and Research Act (WHW), within the regulatory framework of the Ministry of Education, Culture and Science and the regime of NVAO. Our Executive Board sets strategic direction and holds final approval authority over programmes, major curriculum changes and revisions to the quality assurance system.

At programme level, quality assurance is carried by our Curriculum, Management and Admissions Teams, our faculty and our learners, coordinated by the **QA Team** — programme managers led by a QA Lead, holding primary responsibility and reporting to the Executive Board. Our **Examination Board** safeguards the standard and integrity of assessment independently of programme management; our **Educational Council** gives learners a standing advisory channel; our **Group of Field Representatives** brings the external perspective described below. Competences follow from this division: the Curriculum Team designs and develops programmes with faculty and field representatives, and the Executive Board formally approves programmes and major changes. This is the same for all three programmes.

We assure quality through the **Plan–Do–Check–Act cycle** at each of the four levels the policy identifies. At Plan, our curriculum team, faculty and field representatives design curriculum and set objectives; at Do, we deliver learning activities, assessment and coaching; at Check, we gather internal and external feedback systematically — learner reflections and surveys, examination board reviews, performance reviews — against our programme outcomes; at Act, we make the resulting improvements, formally approved by the QA Team and the Executive Board.

**Quality culture and the responsibility of our internal stakeholders**

Our policy names every internal stakeholder group as bearing responsibility for quality: management, faculty, the curriculum and admissions teams, and our learners. Learner responsibility is not rhetorical in our design — learners are represented structurally through the Educational Council and procedurally through feedback loops built into each activity and module, and our policy requires that this feedback be acted upon, not merely collected. We describe this as a designed quality culture rather than a demonstrated one: no cohort has yet completed a module, so we hold no learner satisfaction data and no record of feedback-driven change at module level.

**How we involve external stakeholders**

Our standing mechanism for external stakeholder involvement is the **Group of Field Representatives**, governed by the *Responsibilities and Guidelines for the Field Representatives* (Annex 1.4): an advisory, non-voting body of at least three members drawn from the employer market and academia, selected against explicit diversity criteria covering status, gender, race, ethnicity, age and geography. It convenes at programme design and at annual review, charged with validating the level of the programme and recommending curriculum enhancements. As all three programmes are oriented towards employability in the technology sector, this group is our principal channel for labour-market currency. The mechanism is common to the three programmes; the record of its operation is not, and we report it per programme.

*Bachelor's in Applied Computing.* Here the mechanism has been exercised and documented. The *Consolidated Field Representative Feedback* (Annex 1.5) records eighteen verbatim comments on the Programme Brochure and the Modular Learning Outcomes, each carrying our explicit response — accepted, partially accepted, or referred for further validation — and the resulting action; our *Action Planning* document (Annex 1.6) schedules those actions against dates, towards a target application date of October 2026. Together they show the feedback-to-decision-to-action loop of our policy operating in practice, before launch, and are the strongest evidence we hold for this element of the standard.

*Professional Master's in Applied Artificial Intelligence.* The standing guidelines are in force and the group is constituted. We do not yet hold a dated record of a completed review cycle for this programme.

*Tech MBA.* The standing guidelines are in force. As with the Master's, we do not yet hold a dated record of a completed field representative review.

**Academic integrity, academic freedom and vigilance against fraud**

We regulate academic integrity not in the quality assurance policy itself but in each programme's *Teaching and Examination Regulations* (Annex 1.7), *Policy on Assessment* (Annex 1.8) and *Examination Board Handbook* (Annex 1.9). Across the three programmes these documents share the same structure and, in the relevant articles, substantially the same text.

Article 3.11 of the Regulations, and the corresponding article of the Examination Board Handbook, define academic misconduct, place a reporting duty on our faculty and set out the sanctions available to our Examination Board. Our definition of plagiarism and misconduct expressly covers undisclosed or unreflective use of artificial intelligence tools, which we consider directly material for a cluster of technology programmes. The Policy on Assessment adds identity and integrity verification at the viva and governs permitted technological support in assessed work. The procedure for contesting a finding runs from the Programme Manager to the Examination Board and, externally, to NRTO, identically across the three programmes.

*Tech MBA.* Until we prepared this application, the Tech MBA operated with a Programme Handbook and a Supervision Guide but without its own Regulations, Policy on Assessment or Examination Board Handbook. We have resolved that it operates under the same regulatory framework as the Professional Master's and have issued the three documents accordingly (Annexes 1.7 to 1.9). Existing practice corroborates this rather than conflicting with it: the programme's Final Project Agreement Form already requires disclosure of artificial intelligence use, the obligation the adopted regulations now formalise.

We operate **one Examination Board for all three programmes**, not a board per programme. It is installed by the Executive Board and composed of a chair, a vice-chair, an official secretary and one or more members for each programme, with a minimum of three members on the permanent examination committee and at least one member independent of programme management. This is the composition our Examination Board Handbook prescribes, and a single board carrying programme-specific membership is what allows us to apply one standard of assessment integrity consistently across the Master's, the Bachelor's and the Tech MBA while keeping programme expertise on the board for each.

**Equal opportunities and protection against discrimination**

Our commitments on equal treatment are set out in the *Gender Equality and Equal Opportunities Statement* (Annex 1.3), which consolidates provisions previously distributed across our admissions, assessment and governance documents: non-discriminatory admission; an inclusive and accessible learning environment; accommodations for learners with disabilities, designed to promote equal opportunity of success; fairness and cultural neutrality in assessment; and diversity criteria in the composition of our Group of Field Representatives and Examination Board. The statement records our commitments as they stand. We do not claim in it a dedicated equality office, a discrimination-specific complaints channel separate from our general complaints and appeals procedure, or equality monitoring data, because we do not presently maintain any of these.

**How our programmes align with our mission and our regulations**

Our mission is to address the skills gap in the technology sector by delivering professional programmes that produce labour-market-ready graduates; all three programmes here are technology-field, employability-oriented professional programmes, so the alignment between programme objectives and institutional mission is direct. Compliance with our own regulatory documents is structurally assured: for each programme the Examination Board advises the Executive Board annually on the Teaching and Examination Regulations, and the Regulations take precedence over the programme or educational handbook in the event of conflict. This hierarchy now applies identically to all three.

**Transparency of information to our learners**

Each programme's Educational Handbook, or in the case of the Tech MBA its Programme Handbook, makes the curriculum structure, the learning outcomes and the assessment strategy available to all learners. Our Teaching and Examination Regulations require the Programme Manager to inform learners of their rights, their obligations and the guidance available to them. These arrangements are identical in structure across the cluster.

**The relationship between research, teaching and learning**

For the Professional Master's we document the connection between faculty research activity and teaching in *Faculty Research* (Annex 1.10), describing the scholarly and applied research of our teaching staff and how it informs curriculum content. We do not hold an equivalent for the Bachelor's or the Tech MBA. Both are applied, professionally oriented programmes in which we maintain currency principally through practitioner faculty and our Group of Field Representatives rather than academic research output. We acknowledge that we evidence this element less fully for those two programmes, and identify it below as a point for development.

**Subcontracted activities**

We do not subcontract the design, delivery or assessment of our programmes. We do, however, rely on external providers and external bodies for parts of the educational infrastructure and for specific assurance functions, and we treat these as falling within the scope of this standard.

**Our learning platform.** All three programmes are delivered through Canvas, a hosted learning management system supplied by an external provider, supported by Google Workspace for collaboration. Learning content, assessment briefs, submissions and feedback all pass through this platform. Our programme teams are responsible for what we publish on it and for the learning design it carries; the provider is responsible for availability, security and the processing of personal data under our data-processing arrangements. Platform provision does not transfer any academic responsibility: assessment decisions are taken by our examiners and our Examination Board, never by the platform or its supplier.

**Content and knowledge partners.** Our quality assurance policy names Knowledge Partners and content partners among the external players in our system, and their role is to help keep curriculum content current. For the Bachelor's in Applied Computing, part of the technical practice content in the Applied Software Engineering, Applied Machine Learning and Applied Data Science tracks is drawn from the Qwasar platform. Where we use partner content, our Curriculum Team selects and maps it against our own modular learning outcomes, and our faculty remain responsible for the learning outcomes achieved. Partner content is an input to our curriculum, not a substitute for it, and it is reviewed in the same module-level and programme-level cycles as material we author ourselves.

**External bodies in assurance and appeals.** Several assurance functions sit deliberately outside our own staff. Our Examination Board must include at least one member independent of programme management. Where two internal examiners cannot agree on a pass or fail for a final project, an **External Examiner** is appointed to review the work against the criteria and give an independent judgement. Learners who wish to take a complaint beyond our Examination Board may take it to **NRTO**, the external body for the Dutch private education sector. Our Group of Field Representatives provides external validation of programme level and curriculum currency. Above these, we operate within the regulatory framework of the **Ministry of Education, Culture and Science** and the accreditation regime of **NVAO**. These are not subcontracting arrangements; we describe them here because they are the points at which judgements material to our quality are made by parties other than ourselves, and we regard their independence as a strength of the system rather than a gap in it.

**How we monitor these arrangements.** Responsibility for the platform relationship and for partner content sits with the QA Team, within the same Plan–Do–Check–Act cycle we apply to everything else: partner-sourced content and platform-supported delivery are evaluated at module and programme level alongside our own material, and problems surface through the same learner feedback and review routes. We acknowledge that our quality assurance policy currently names these external players without setting out, in its own text, an explicit clause on activities carried out by other parties and how adherence to our standards is assured in them. We are extending the policy to state this expressly, and we identify it below as a point for development.

**How we implement, monitor and revise the policy**

Responsibility for evaluating our quality assurance system rests with the QA Team, and the Executive Board approves the resulting changes; we take all feedback from internal and external participants, our learners included, into that evaluation.

The policy document itself is subject to its own review cycle, distinct from the programme, module and activity cycles in section 1.1.6. Our QA Team reviews it annually, ahead of each academic year, proposing revisions arising from regulatory change, from Field Representative or Educational Council input, or from our experience applying it; revisions require formal Executive Board approval. Because the policy has not yet been applied to a running cohort, we have no revision history and no satisfaction results specific to it. Once the programmes are operational, we will assess satisfaction with the policy document itself — as distinct from programme-level learner satisfaction, gathered under ESG 1.7 — within that annual review, drawing on our faculty, programme management, the Educational Council and the Field Representatives. The mechanism is designed and recorded in the policy, and as yet unexercised.

**Our analysis and the points we intend to develop**

Weighing the above, we consider that we meet this standard: we have a coherent, formally adopted, ESG-structured quality assurance policy; a governance structure that carries it; regulatory documents operationalising academic integrity and equal treatment across all three programmes; and a constituted external stakeholder mechanism we have demonstrably exercised for one of the three. We do not claim a strong position, for four reasons, each of which we intend to address:

1. **Public availability of the policy.** The policy is formally adopted but not yet published. We will publish it to coincide with the launch of the programmes in this cluster.
2. **The research–teaching linkage outside the Master's.** We document the connection between staff scholarly activity and curriculum for the Professional Master's, and not yet for the Bachelor's or the Tech MBA. We will either produce the equivalent documentation or record explicitly that we meet this element through practitioner currency for those programmes.
3. **Exercising the external stakeholder mechanism across all three programmes.** The Bachelor's has a dated, documented record of field representative review producing curriculum decisions. The Master's and the Tech MBA have the mechanism constituted but no comparable record. We will run and document a review cycle for both before launch.
4. **An explicit clause on activities carried out by other parties.** Our policy names our external players but does not yet contain a clause setting out how we assure adherence to our standards in our learning-platform and content-partner arrangements. We are adding one.

According to the standard "Policy for Quality Assurance", the educational programmes "Professional Master's in Applied Artificial Intelligence", "Bachelor's in Applied Computing" and "Tech MBA" have a "satisfactory" position. We give one position for the cluster rather than three, because the policy, the structures carrying it and the regulations operationalising it are common to the three programmes and we identified no difference between them material enough to warrant a different position for any one programme.
