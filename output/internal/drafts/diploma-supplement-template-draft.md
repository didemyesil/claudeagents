# Diploma Supplement – Schoolvision (institution template)

*This Diploma Supplement model was developed by the European Commission, the Council of Europe and UNESCO. The purpose of the supplement is to provide sufficient independent data to improve the international transparency and fair academic and professional recognition of qualifications (diplomas, degrees, certificates, etc.). It is designed to provide a description of the nature, level, context, content and status of the studies that were pursued and successfully completed by the individual named on the original qualification to which this supplement is appended. It is free from any value judgements, equivalence statements or suggestions about recognition. Information in all eight sections is provided. Where information is not provided, an explanation gives the reason why.*

Schoolvision issues one Diploma Supplement for every graduate of each of its programmes. The Examination Board produces it centrally, automatically and free of charge, in English, together with the diploma. Fields marked [programme] are completed from the programme entries at the end of this template; fields marked [learner] are completed for each graduate.

## 8. Information on the national higher education system

Higher education in the Netherlands has a binary system: research-oriented education at research universities (wo) and professionally oriented education at universities of applied sciences (hbo). Both use a three-cycle bachelor–master–doctorate structure and the European Credit Transfer and Accumulation System (ECTS); one credit represents 28 hours of study and one year of full-time study is 60 credits.

Professionally oriented bachelor's programmes (hbo) comprise 240 credits and lead to level 6 of the Dutch Qualifications Framework (NLQF), which corresponds to the first cycle of the QF-EHEA. Professionally oriented master's programmes comprise 60 to 120 credits and lead to NLQF level 7, which corresponds to the second cycle of the QF-EHEA. Graduates of a master's programme have access to doctoral programmes, subject to the admission requirements of the institution concerned.

Quality assurance of higher education in the Netherlands is the responsibility of the Accreditation Organisation of the Netherlands and Flanders (NVAO). [Diagram of the Dutch higher education system, as provided by Nuffic, to be inserted.]
