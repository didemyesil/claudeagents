## Standards / criteria list

Transcribed verbatim from source §III (pp. 12–22) — 10 standards, each with
its normative **Standard** statement, **Guidelines**, and the source's own
**Sample questions for assessment** (these aren't separately binding
criteria, but are the source's own operationalisation of the standard —
useful for `folder-developer` to know what evidence will actually be
probed).

### Standard 1 — Policy for Quality Assurance
**Standard:** Institutions should have a policy for quality assurance that
is made public and forms part of their strategic management. Internal
stakeholders should develop and implement this policy through appropriate
structures and processes, while involving external stakeholders.

**Guidelines:** Policies and processes are the main pillars of a coherent
institutional quality assurance system that forms a cycle for continuous
improvement and contributes to the accountability of the institution. It
supports the development of quality culture in which all internal
stakeholders assume responsibility for quality and engage in quality
assurance at all levels of the institution. The policy has a formal status
and is publicly available. Quality assurance policies are most effective
when they reflect the relationship between research and learning &
teaching and take account of both the national context and the
institutional context. Such a policy supports: the organisation of the QA
system; departments/schools/faculties/institutional leadership/staff/
students taking on their QA responsibilities; academic integrity and
freedom, vigilance against academic fraud; guarding against intolerance or
discrimination; involvement of external stakeholders in QA. How the policy
is implemented, monitored and revised is the institution's decision. The
QA policy also covers any elements of an institution's activities that are
subcontracted to or carried out by other parties.

Sample questions: What documents reflect the QA policy? Where published —
open or internal only? Available to teaching staff, employees, students,
employers? Do other institutions/stakeholders participate in designing it?
How did the QA policy change over time? Results of assessing stakeholder
satisfaction with the QA policy? Does the EP match the stated mission/
strategy of the EO? How is research–teaching–learning linkage reflected?
Does the QA policy include business/scientific community/staff/student
interaction — mechanisms, examples? Are competences and decision-making
processes for EP development defined? How transparent is educational
process information to students? Are there EP objective/concept revision
procedures? Do developed EPs comply with the EO's regulatory documents?
Does the EO have a gender equality/equal opportunities concept? Describe
the university's QA system and its application/continuous improvement
within the EP. What activities are outsourced and how is adherence
monitored?

### Standard 2 — Design and Approval of Programmes
**Standard:** Institutions should have processes for the design and
approval of their programmes. The programmes should be designed so that
they meet the objectives set for them, including the intended learning
outcomes. The qualification resulting from a programme should be clearly
specified and communicated, and refer to the correct level of the national
qualifications framework for higher education and, consequently, to the
Framework for Qualifications of the European Higher Education Area.

**Guidelines:** Study programmes are at the core of the HEI's teaching
mission — academic knowledge and transferable skills. Programmes: are
designed with overall objectives in line with institutional strategy and
explicit intended learning outcomes; are designed involving students and
other stakeholders; benefit from external expertise and reference points;
reflect the four purposes of higher education of the Council of Europe;
enable smooth student progression; define expected student workload (e.g.
ECTS); include well-structured placement opportunities where appropriate;
are subject to a formal institutional approval process.

Sample questions: Goal of the EP / general qualification / target group?
Are qualification objectives adequately set out in governing documents and
the Diploma Supplement? What skills/methodological competencies are
taught? What skills (incl. foreign languages) are developed? Opportunities
for successful learning/participation in society? How does the university
monitor changes since the last QA procedure — recommendations, decisions,
implementation? How are changes accounted for ahead of the next external
assessment? Are professional-environment requirements reflected? Does the
programme have a special profile (double degree, dual education, distance,
etc.) properly described/justified? How is content–learning-outcomes
correspondence to level (bachelor/master/doctoral) ensured? Reasonable
compulsory/elective/optional module volumes? Mobility window (e.g. a
semester abroad)? How does student-assessment correlate with ECTS? Is the
EP harmonious re: expected outcomes — do discipline contents ensure
achievement? Course names match content? Modern science reflected?
Classroom-vs-self-study ratio appropriate? Module descriptions complete
and informative? Is the EP technically feasible re: student workload? Are
EP development/approval procedures defined and documented? Is there
external examination of the EP — by whom, what requirements?

### Standard 3 — Student-Centred Learning, Teaching and Assessment
**Standard:** Institutions should ensure that the programmes are delivered
in a way that encourages students to take an active role in creating the
learning process, and that the assessment of students reflects this
approach.

**Guidelines:** Student-centred learning and teaching plays an important
role in stimulating students' motivation, self-reflection and engagement.
Implementation: respects and attends to student diversity, enabling
flexible learning paths; considers/uses different delivery modes where
appropriate; flexibly uses varied pedagogical methods; regularly evaluates
and adjusts delivery/methods; encourages learner autonomy with adequate
guidance/support; promotes mutual respect in the learner-teacher
relationship; has appropriate procedures for student complaints. For
assessment: assessors are familiar with testing/examination methods and
receive support developing their skills; assessment criteria and marking
criteria published in advance; assessment lets students demonstrate the
extent intended outcomes are achieved, with feedback linked to learning
advice; where possible, more than one examiner; regulations account for
mitigating circumstances; assessment consistent, fairly applied, per
stated procedures; a formal student-appeals procedure is in place.

Sample questions: Respect/attention to different student groups, flexible
trajectory? How are student needs factored into EP formation? What
trajectory-forming opportunities do students have? Equal opportunities
across student groups for achieving outcomes? Does EP delivery account for
individual student peculiarities? Own research in teaching of EP subjects
(examples)? Feedback system on teaching methods/outcome assessment? How is
student academic freedom ensured? Consistency/transparency/objectivity of
the outcome-assessment mechanism per EP? How is the knowledge/skills/
competency assessment mechanism implemented? What teaching/learning forms
used, including innovative (e.g. online)? Sufficient variability? Do
didactic concepts ensure career-oriented competency formation? Are
assessment tools competency-oriented, sufficiently variable across
qualification criteria? Modular-oriented, combined assessment tools? Do
normative/training documents reflect examination procedures and types? Do
they account for students with disabilities? Complaint/appeal procedures
in the EP? What learning-outcome assessment methods are used? How are
evaluators trained/professionally developed?

### Standard 4 — Student Admission, Progression, Recognition and Certification
**Standard:** Institutions should consistently apply pre-defined and
published regulations covering all phases of the student "life cycle",
e.g. student admission, progression, recognition and certification.

**Guidelines:** Providing necessary conditions/support for student academic
progress is in everyone's interest. Fit-for-purpose admission, recognition
and completion procedures are vital, particularly for mobile students.
Access policies, admission processes and criteria must be implemented
consistently and transparently; induction to the institution and programme
is provided. Institutions need processes and tools to collect, monitor and
act on student-progression information. Fair recognition of qualifications,
study periods and prior learning (incl. non-formal/informal) relies on:
institutional recognition practice in line with the Lisbon Recognition
Convention; cooperation with other institutions, QA agencies, and the
national ENIC/NARIC centre. Graduation documentation must explain the
qualification gained, including achieved learning outcomes and the
context/level/content/status of studies completed.

Sample questions: How is the student contingent formed, what additional
applicant requirements exist? How do students/applicants learn about
contingent-formation procedures (admission, transfer, re-crediting,
expulsion, etc.)? How does the university evaluate admission-process vs.
subsequent progress correspondence? Is there Lisbon-Convention-compliant
recognition of prior learning outcomes/qualifications? Are training
requirements transparent to all target groups — what information channels
exist? Is there a mechanism for recognising academic-mobility results and
additional/formal/non-formal education results? Requirements for prior
learning recognition — examples? What normative document regulates
academic-mobility-result recognition? Is there an opportunity to prepare
students for professional certification — what types are possible?

### Standard 5 — Teaching Staff
**Standard:** Institutions should assure themselves of the competence of
their teachers. They should apply fair and transparent processes for the
recruitment and development of the staff.

**Guidelines:** The teacher's role is essential to a high-quality student
experience and knowledge/competence/skill acquisition. HEIs have primary
responsibility for staff quality and a supportive working environment.
Such an environment: sets up clear, transparent, fair recruitment and
employment-condition processes recognising teaching's importance; offers
and promotes professional development opportunities; encourages scholarly
activity linking education and research; encourages teaching-method
innovation and new-technology use.

Sample questions: Are human resources sufficient to implement the EP and
guarantee its profile? What documents reflect personnel policy —
management transparency? How are promotions made? Does teacher personnel
potential correspond to university strategy and EP specifics? How is
teacher competence level defined relative to professional standard,
industry framework, EQF? Requirement differences by position skill level?
How do teacher qualification requirements differ by EP level (BA/MA/PhD)?
Requirements for experience, field, teaching quality? Is teaching/
examination load balanced? Is an interdisciplinary approach implemented?
Measures for professional development/staff qualification? How is
teachers' professional/personal development stimulated — self-development,
innovative methods, integration of scientific activity and education? Do
teachers use ICT (online education, e-portfolio, MOOC)? How has teaching
quality/graduate competency changed with new technologies? Are industry
practitioners involved in teaching, how are they selected? Academic
mobility dynamics/results of EP teachers over the past 5 years and
contribution to quality/EP development? How is teacher involvement in
research work conducted?

### Standard 6 — Learning Resources and Student Support
**Standard:** Institutions should have appropriate funding for learning
and teaching activities and ensure that adequate and readily accessible
learning resources and student support are provided.

**Guidelines:** Institutions provide a range of resources to assist student
learning — physical (libraries, study facilities, IT) and human (tutors,
counsellors, advisers). Support services matter particularly for student
mobility. The needs of a diverse student population (mature, part-time,
employed, international, disabled) and the shift toward student-centred,
flexible learning are accounted for when allocating/planning/providing
resources and support. Internal QA ensures resources are fit for purpose,
accessible, and students are informed of available services. Support and
administrative staff need to be qualified with development opportunities.

Sample questions: Are financial resources/educational infrastructure
sufficient for EP goals? Do material-technical/information resources
ensure planned EP results — how is material-resource development planned?
What information systems are used, what do they solve/serve? How do
students access subject information? How is the website used to inform
students/employees/stakeholders — what's published, how often updated, is
current information accessible? Do information resources match EP
specifics — plagiarism checks on research/graduation works/theses? Access
to educational internet resources, functioning Wi-Fi? Are library
resources sufficient, long opening hours? Are online technologies used —
their expediency? Are individual support/consulting appropriately
regulated/planned (EP manual, consulting hours, teaching-aid support)? Are
students assisted with accommodation, internships, semesters abroad? What
are procedures for supporting various student groups, including
information/consulting? Do support programmes exist for disabled students
and special life situations, do they meet requirements? Are cooperative
relations established with other organisations for professional practice?
Is educational equipment/software similar to industry equivalents? How
does the EO ensure safety-requirement compliance in learning? How are
diverse student-group needs accounted for in the EP context?

### Standard 7 — Information Management
**Standard:** Institutions should ensure that they collect, analyse and
use relevant information for the effective management of their programmes
and other activities.

**Guidelines:** Reliable data is crucial for informed decision-making.
Effective information collection/analysis about programmes and other
activities feeds the internal QA system. Information gathered depends on
institution type/mission; of interest: key performance indicators; student
population profile; progression/success/drop-out rates; student
satisfaction with programmes; learning resources/support available;
graduate career paths. Various collection methods may be used; students
and staff should be involved in providing/analysing information and
planning follow-up.

Sample questions: What information systems improve the internal QA
system? How is EO/EP activity effectiveness and efficiency assessed? What
quality-system assessment processes exist (teaching assessment, satisfaction
monitoring, graduate employment/career analysis, information collection/
processing)? What information-management processes exist, how are
stakeholders involved in collecting/analysing information and
decision-making? How constantly is processed information used to improve
internal QA? How are risks identified/projected from information analysis?
How is internal reporting on information management conducted? How do
tools change when information requirements change? What communicative
mechanisms implement the EP plan and changes? How does the EO protect
information?

### Standard 8 — Public Information
**Standard:** Institutions should publish information about their
activities, including programmes, which is clear, accurate, objective,
up-to-date and readily accessible.

**Guidelines:** Information on institutions' activities is useful for
prospective/current students, graduates, other stakeholders, and the
public. Institutions provide information about their activities, including
programmes offered and selection criteria, intended learning outcomes,
qualifications awarded, teaching/learning/assessment procedures used, pass
rates, learning opportunities, and graduate employment information.

Sample questions: What stakeholder information processes are defined in
the EO? Does the EO have a dedicated information resource (website, portal)
distributing EP-development-plan information? What distribution channels
(media, information networks) inform the public/stakeholders? Does the EO
publish information meeting stakeholder needs (programmes, expected
outcomes, qualifications, teaching/learning/assessment procedures, passing
points, tuition fees, study opportunities, teacher information, employment
opportunities, partner cooperation, financial statements)? How is
satisfaction with received-information quality/completeness investigated?
Is there a graduate community/association — how does it participate in EO
activities?

### Standard 9 — On-going Monitoring and Periodic Review of Programmes
**Standard:** Institutions should monitor and periodically review their
programmes to ensure that they achieve the objectives set for them and
respond to the needs of students and society. These reviews should lead
to continuous improvement of the programme. Any action planned or taken as
a result should be communicated to all those concerned.

**Guidelines:** Regular monitoring/review/revision aims to keep provision
appropriate and maintain a supportive, effective learning environment.
Includes evaluation of: programme content vs. latest discipline research
(currency); changing societal needs; student workload/progression/
completion; effectiveness of student-assessment procedures; student
expectations/needs/satisfaction; learning environment and support-service
fitness for purpose. Programmes are reviewed/revised regularly involving
students and other stakeholders; information collected is analysed and
the programme adapted. Revised programme specifications are published.

Sample questions: How are programmes monitored/evaluated at the
university? How is EP objective achievement tracked? How is the need for
curriculum/EP content change determined (labour market, employer
requirements, societal demand)? Are students represented in collegiate
bodies, involved in further learning development? How are students/
employers/other stakeholders involved in EP review? Are survey results
factored into evaluating/revising the EP? Does programme content reflect
the latest science in the discipline? How is professional-practice
satisfaction/quality monitored and its results used? How is achievement of
professional-practice goals and compliance with upcoming professional
activity guaranteed? How is student/community-needs monitoring conducted —
demonstrate results. How is the learner's personal development monitored
during the programme, what methods, where recorded? Are students and
graduates surveyed? Do surveys account for student-load checks?

### Standard 10 — Cyclical External Quality Assurance
**Standard:** Institutions should undergo external quality assurance in
line with the ESG on a cyclical basis.

**Guidelines:** External QA in its various forms verifies internal QA
effectiveness, acts as a catalyst for improvement, and offers new
perspectives, assuring the institution and the public of quality.
Institutions participate in cyclical external QA taking account, where
relevant, of their legislative framework — it may take different forms and
focus at different organisational levels (programme, faculty, institution).
QA is continuous — institutions ensure progress made since the last
external QA activity is taken into account when preparing for the next.

Sample questions: Do the EO and EP participate in external evaluation
procedures? How regularly do they undergo external assessment — what were
the results of national/international evaluations the EO took part in
(links if published)? What decisions followed the last external QA
procedure, how/to what extent are recommendations implemented, how are
changes accounted for ahead of the upcoming procedure? What role does
external evaluation effectiveness play in developing the EO's internal QA
system?

## Format / presentation rules

- **Self-assessment report (SAR)**: must be presented in **English**,
  officially in electronic version. **Must not exceed 50–60 pages**
  (without annexes). Large supporting documents may stay in the original
  language with a short English summary.
- Format: Times New Roman, font size 12, 1.5 line spacing, ≤6pt paragraph
  spacing before/after titles, automatic table of contents, page numbers,
  A4 portrait (annexes may be landscape).
- Structure: title page, reliability statement signed by the head of
  institution, table of contents, symbols/abbreviations, Introduction
  (organisation/EP profile, presentation, previous accreditation if any),
  Main section (evidence + analysis per standard, 30–40 pages), Conclusion
  (SWOT analysis — institutional accreditation only, not programme; plus
  the "Conclusion of the Self-Evaluation Committee" table), Annexes.
- **Introduction** must include, per Annex 2's Table 1/Table 2: general
  institution/programme info, levels per NQF and QF-EHEA, ECTS credits,
  entry requirements, learning outcomes, specialisation, tuition fees,
  employability/career fields, dates of site visit.
- **Cluster deliverable shape**: for this application (3 programmes, one
  cluster, one EEP visit), the SAR is **one document**, not three. Table 1
  (general institution info) appears once. Table 2 (per-programme profile)
  is filled **once per programme** — 3 instances, one per EP. The Main
  section (Standards 1–10) is written **per standard**, and within each
  standard's write-up, content genuinely common to all 3 programmes is
  stated once; content that differs by programme is broken out into a
  labelled subsection per programme — this is `folder-developer`'s call to
  make per standard, grounded in the actual evidence, not a fixed split
  decided in advance. The "Conclusion of the Self-Evaluation Committee"
  table likewise needs a position (strong/satisfactory/suggests
  improvement/unsatisfactory) per standard **per programme** wherever that
  standard was broken out per programme, and a single position where it
  was genuinely shared.
- Achievement data for the **last 3–5 years** is required for each
  standard, **except initial accreditation** — the current application, so
  this requirement is relaxed for the 3 programmes (ex-ante assessment).
- Every standard's write-up ends with a self-assessed position: strong /
  satisfactory / suggests improvement / unsatisfactory (see Verdict scale).
- **Submission timing**: SAR + applications sent to IAAR **≥8 weeks**
  before the EEP visit. IAAR sends it to experts **≥6 weeks** before the
  visit. Expert review returned within **10 calendar days**.
- **EEP composition**: independent experts including foreign experts
  experienced in teaching/QA, an employer representative, and a student
  community representative (1 per cluster). Cluster rule: ≤5 EPs per
  cluster, ≤20 EPs per EEP visit.
- **Site visit**: 3–5 days. Structured per Annex 1's recommended programme
  — includes meetings with management, structural-unit heads, accredited-EP
  heads/chairs, teaching staff, students, undergraduates/doctoral students,
  graduates, and employers, plus a visual inspection of facilities specific
  to the EPs under accreditation.

## Verdict scale

IAAR's own terminology for this track (source, "Conclusion of the
Self-Evaluation Committee" and EEP profile-parameters table):

| IAAR term | Meaning | Maps to system default |
|---|---|---|
| Strong | High-level indicator, example of good practice for other EOs | Meets |
| Satisfactory | Average-level indicator, compliance with the standard | Meets |
| Suggests improvement | Low-level performance of the standard | At Risk |
| Unsatisfactory | Does not meet the standard | Does Not Meet |

**Accreditation-term decision** (Accreditation Council, by % of standards
requiring improvement — note this threshold table is specific to *this*
international/specialised track and differs slightly from the domestic
institutional-accreditation thresholds in `iaar-institutional.md`):

| Accreditation Council decision | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Accredit — 7 years | ≤20% of standards requiring improvement, ≥20% strong standards, best-practice examples present |
| Accredit — 5 years | ≤20% of standards requiring improvement, strong standards present |
| Accredit — 3 years | 20–40% of standards requiring improvement, strong standards present |
| Accredit — 1 year | 40–60% of standards requiring improvement, no strong standards |
| Refusal of accreditation | ≥1 standard rated "unsatisfactory," OR ≥60% requiring improvement |

## Language / terminology notes

- **EO** = Educational Organisation, **EP** = Educational Programme (or
  cluster of programmes), **EEP** = External Expert Panel, **AC** =
  Accreditation Council, **SAR** = Self-Assessment Report.
- Use **"programme"** (UK spelling), matching the source.
- This track is explicitly **ex-ante** for initial accreditation — write
  about intentions/design/plans, and don't force in achieved-outcomes
  language (the 3–5 year track record requirement is explicitly waived for
  initial accreditation).
- The source is ESG-based — its 10 standards map closely (often near
  word-for-word) onto the ESG 2015 standards; if cross-referencing other
  ESG-based accreditor configs (e.g. an NVAO one, if ever restored), expect
  structural overlap but never assume identical wording — always quote
  this source's own text.
- "Specialised accreditation" = programme-level (what this file covers);
  don't conflate with "institutional accreditation" (`iaar-institutional.md`,
  not the active track for the 3 programmes).
