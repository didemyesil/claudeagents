## 1. Approach

1. The programme is a 4MAT cycle at two levels: Session 1 is itself built as a full 8-step cycle (faculty experience it), and 4MAT is then made explicit as the tool to design their own student sessions.
2. Each step has two layers: what faculty experience (the activity) and what we name afterwards (the design move behind it). Naming happens at the end of each quadrant, briefly, so it does not break the experience.
3. Content is anchored in each faculty member's own engagement challenge from the Pre task, so the 2 hours work on real material, not generic exercises.
4. Playcentre-origin examples (parent-and-child play, early childhood settings) are replaced with higher-education equivalents: lecture-heavy sessions, silent online rooms, group-work free riding, feedback that students do not use. Source examples stay only as an acknowledged origin.
5. Co-facilitation: Didem holds the 4MAT design thread and the naming moments; Shamir holds the experiential energy (check-ins, energizers, coaching of groups), drawing on his resource pool. Both model facilitative behaviour; we say so out loud.
6. Between sessions faculty apply the cycle to one workshop plan, test it with students, and bring a short reflection. Session 2 turns those reflections into shared learning and a final revised plan.
7. Light evidence only: artefacts the programme already produces (challenge statements, plans, reflections), no extra burden on faculty.

## 2. Pre task

**Purpose:** get a real engagement challenge from each faculty member, and prepare them to arrive with material. Time: about 20 to 30 minutes, due 2 to 3 days before Session 1. (Assumption on timing.)

**Prompt (short template):**

> Think of one teaching session in the coming months where you want students to be more engaged. In 5 to 8 lines:
> 1. Course, level, group size, format (on campus / online / hybrid).
> 2. What does "low engagement" look like in this session? Describe what you actually see or hear, not a judgement.
> 3. What have you already tried? What happened?
> 4. What would "better engagement" look like, in one observable sentence?
> 5. (Optional) One thing you are curious or unsure about in facilitative teaching.
>
> Bring one document you currently use for this session (slide, plan, activity sheet) if you have one.

**Facilitator use:** Didem and Shamir read all challenges before Session 1, cluster them into 3 to 4 themes (e.g., participation, preparation, group work, feedback; themes are to be derived from real responses, not assumed), and prepare the grouping for Step 4. If student workshop results exist, add 2 to 3 anonymised student voices to Step 2 (see Open questions).

## 3. Session 1: Interactive workshop (4MAT, about 2h10 incl. 10 min buffer or break)

Working title: "Designing for engagement: experience the cycle, then use it."
Participants work on their own challenge from the Pre task throughout.
Lead: D = Didem, S = Shamir. Quadrant totals: Q1 25 min, Q2 27 min, Q3 35 min, Q4 33 min = 120 min, plus 10 min buffer or break between Q2 and Q3.

### 3.1 Design card (faculty keep, one page)
Columns: Quadrant | Student need (in my words) | Activity I will use | Time | Observable sign it worked.
Rows: Connect, Attend, Image, Inform, Practice, Extend, Refine, Perform. Plus top line: course, group, learning outcome.

### 3.2 Activity pool (starting point from the 4MAT source, adapted; to be checked and extended by Shamir)
All adapted from the Brooks-Harris and Stock-Ward categories and the source's icebreaker list; Playcentre-specific contexts removed.
- Connect/Attend: icebreaker linked to the topic ("good or new"), continuum line on a statement, pair interview, quick poll followed by "burning questions" board, short personal story.
- Image/Inform: lecturette with prior prediction, case study from the discipline, demonstration, group survey, sorting task, short handout.
- Practice/Extend: structured discussion, worksheet, scenario or simulation, role play of a client or student situation, create a resource for peers, peer teaching.
- Refine/Perform: action plan, goal setting, peer feedback, portfolio entry, one question for next session, "light bulb" round.
Online variants (A1): polls, breakout rooms, collaborative board, chat waterfall, silent writing in shared doc.

## 4. Post task: Apply and reflect

**Time:** about 2 to 4 hours spread over 2 to 3 weeks (assumption A5), including running the student workshop.

**Steps for faculty:**
1. Take the design card from Session 1 and turn it into a full workshop plan for one student session (A6).
2. Share it with one peer for a 15-minute feedback chat (optional but encouraged; Shamir can host a coaching slot).
3. Run it with students.
4. Capture one piece of student evidence (see section 5, light).
5. Complete the reflection template and submit the revised plan before Session 2.

**Workshop plan template (one page):**
Course and group | Learning outcome | Planned 4MAT steps with minutes | Materials | What I expect to see if students are engaged.

**Reflection template (short, about 300 words):**
1. What happened? Describe in 3 to 4 lines what you did and what you saw (facts first).
2. Where did students engage most? Which quadrant was that?
3. Where did it fall flat? What do you think the reason was?
4. What surprised you, about students or about yourself as a facilitator?
5. What would you change in the plan? List up to 3 revisions.
6. One question to bring to Session 2.

## 5. Session 2: Reflective workshop (about 2h10 incl. 10 min break)

Working title: "What we learned about engagement in practice."
Input: each faculty member's reflection and revised plan, collected before the session. D and S read all and prepare a short pattern summary (themes in "what worked", "what fell flat", "surprises"). Session 2 follows the cycle in a lighter form, so faculty see the 4MAT rhythm again, this time applied to reflection.

## 6. Success criteria and light evidence

**Programme-level success criteria (proposed, to be agreed):**
1. Every participating faculty member produces one revised workshop plan that uses all 4 quadrants and has an observable engagement sign.
2. Every participant has tested the plan with students and submitted a reflection.
3. Participants can explain 4MAT in their own words and say which quadrant their session under-served before.
4. At least some faculty report intending to keep using the design card (self-report, no target number set; set only if Didem and Shamir want one).
5. Participants rate the sessions useful and applicable (feedback form).

**Light evidence (minimal burden):**
- Pre: challenge statements (already collected).
- Session 1: design cards (already produced).
- Post: revised plan, reflection, optionally one student evidence item (e.g., short anonymous poll or 3 observation notes).
- Session 2: pattern summary, final plan, 3-question feedback form.
- Attendance record.

**Optional, flagged: accreditation evidence angle.** If useful for the AT accreditation folder, these artefacts can serve as evidence of faculty development linked to teaching quality, with anonymised excerpts. I have not mapped this to any specific standard; that requires the accreditation workflow and Didem's decision. Not part of the core design.
