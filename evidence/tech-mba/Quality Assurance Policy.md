## ESG 1.1. Policy for Quality Assurance

*"Institutes should have a policy for quality assurance that is made public and forms part of their strategic management. Internal stakeholders should develop and implement this policy through appropriate structures and processes, while involving external stakeholders."*

### 1.1.1. Context and Objectives

Team Academy's vision is to bridge the global skills gap; it develops and offers degree programmes in technology and business leadership, including the Tech MBA — a fully online professional master's degree preparing learners for technology-leadership and entrepreneurial roles. Through its quality assurance system the institute ensures: a quality culture maintained by all community members; relevant content/competencies/planning facilitating each learner's learning experience and employability; programmes developed in connection with global job-market needs; ongoing improvement complying with international accreditation requirements.

### 1.1.2. Concept of Quality

Quality is based on the extent to which processes/procedures establish and constantly improve an educational standard, and on the interaction/relationship between all parties and activities (staff, external partners, learners, learning resources, learning environment, support mechanisms) — applied to the Tech MBA's fully online delivery model as much as to campus-based programmes.

### 1.1.3. Guiding Principles

Adherence to common standards (ESG); Collaboration; Transparency; Innovation; Agility; Reflection — unchanged institution-wide.

### 1.1.4 Organisation of the Quality Assurance System

Two layers: **Institute Quality Assurance** (external players: Ministry of Education, Culture and Science; NVAO; internal: Executive Board, Management Teams) and **Programme Quality Assurance** (external: Field Representatives, Knowledge Partners; internal: Examination Board, Executive Board, Managing Director, QA Team, Programme Teams, Educational Council, Admissions Teams, Faculty, Learners) — see `Organisational Structure.md` (Tech MBA copy, this evidence folder) for the role descriptions underpinning this structure as applied to the Tech MBA.

The Tech MBA's programme-level QA is informed by the institute QA policy; the Field Representatives group and other external players connect the Tech MBA's academic profile to sector requirements, per `Responsibilities and Guidelines for the Field Representatives.md` (Tech MBA copy, this evidence folder). Internal organisation involves the Tech MBA's Programme Manager, faculty, and the curriculum team, coordinated by the institution's QA Team (led by a QA Lead), reporting to the Executive Board.

### 1.1.5 Evaluation of the Quality Assurance System

Ongoing evaluation of policies/procedures/guidelines in collaboration with the Executive Board and other teams; the QA Team holds main evaluation responsibility; the Executive Board approves iterative improvements; all stakeholder feedback (including Tech MBA learners) is incorporated.

**Review cycle and satisfaction assessment for this QA Policy document specifically:** *(see resolution below — same institution-wide addition as the shared document; carried into this copy for internal consistency.)* The QA Team reviews this QA Policy document itself on an annual cycle, with Executive Board sign-off, alongside its existing annual review of the programme-level QA system; stakeholder satisfaction with the QA Policy specifically will be assessed once the Tech MBA is operational, through the same learner-survey and performance-review mechanisms already described under §ESG 1.7, with a dedicated question on the QA Policy added to those instruments rather than a separate survey — this is a planned, ex-ante mechanism, not historical data, consistent with Tech MBA not yet having launched.

**Plan–Do–Check–Act (PDCA) Cycle:** Plan (curriculum team + faculty + field representatives design the curriculum, set strategic objectives); Do (deliver learning activities, module projects, coaching); Check (systematic internal/external feedback — reflections, learner surveys, Examination Board reviews, performance reviews — against KPIs/programme outcomes); Act (targeted improvements approved by the QA Team and Executive Board). Applied across the Tech MBA's activity, module, certification, and programme levels.

### 1.1.6. Multi-layered Approach to Quality Assurance — Tech MBA

**Activity level** — module projects, weekly learning activities, coaching, and Final Project supervision: designed by the curriculum/programme management teams and field experts; continuous learner feedback; immediate action on feedback.

**Module level** — the Tech MBA's four certifications (Transform your Business; Lead Your Business; Innovate and Grow Your Business; Leadership and Action Research) each group several modules focused on defined leadership/technology competencies: designed per programme objectives; learner feedback gathered per module and acted on; competency development evaluated via module project evidence and progression assessment points.

**Certificate/certification level** — designed and planned by the curriculum and programme management teams with field experts and external advisors, based on market needs; each certification concludes with a summative assessment (caesura of 60) evaluating learner mastery; alignment between the Tech MBA's academic profile and market needs is improved based on these outcomes.

**Programme level** — three success metrics, consistent with the institution-wide model: the extent to which the programme helps learners achieve intended technology-leadership competency development; the quality of the Final Project (assessed by Internal/External Examiners) as the capstone evidence of mastery; and learners' employability status upon completion. A typical improvement cycle applies: design/plan with field experts based on market needs; evaluate learner mastery through the four certifications and the Final Project; improve alignment between the Tech MBA's profile and market needs based on these success metrics.
