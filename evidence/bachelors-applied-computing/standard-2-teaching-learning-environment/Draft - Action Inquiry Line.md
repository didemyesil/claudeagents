# Action Inquiry Line in the BSc Applied Computing Programmes (Draft)

The Action Inquiry Line is an integrated educational and developmental line within the BSc Applied Computing programmes. It supports students in learning systematically from action in authentic professional and project-based contexts and strengthens the programme's agile, practice-based learning environment. Through this line, students learn to **plan, act, observe, and reflect** in a structured way, progressively improving both their work and their professional practice.

At the start of the programme, students are introduced to the process and mindset of action inquiry. From **Season 1 onward**, the line develops progressively through three levels of inquiry:

- **Self-inquiry** — students reflect on their own actions, choices, learning process, and professional behaviour.
- **One-to-one inquiry** — students learn to inquire in dialogue with peers or other relevant stakeholders.
- **Team inquiry** — students learn to reflect and improve collectively within project teams and agile working structures.

In later stages, action inquiry connects explicitly to project design, professional decision-making, and applied research in increasingly complex contexts; in the final year it is a methodological foundation for the final project.

The line integrates three mutually reinforcing components:

- **Action Inquiry** — the core cycle: Plan – Act – Observe – Reflect, for learning systematically from experience.
- **Agile practice** — squads, stand-ups, sprint cycles, retrospectives, peer feedback, team coaching; the structured professional context in which the cycle is continuously exercised.
- **Vertical leadership development** — the developmental pathway through which students grow in self-awareness, ownership, accountability, collaboration, perspective-taking, and professional judgement.

In the final project design phase, students integrate all three levels within an individual professional project: self-inquiry to examine their own learning/assumptions/decisions; one-to-one inquiry to engage clients/stakeholders in purposeful dialogue; team inquiry to understand and work with the wider collaborative/organisational context.

## How the Line Works Through the Seasons

**Week 1 of the programme:** Introduction to Action Inquiry as a core developmental/reflective line, the Plan–Act–Observe–Reflect cycle, and how it connects to programme objectives, the agile working structure, and leadership development.

### Season 1: Self-inquiry (5 weeks)

Students take a short Action Inquiry module learning: getting started with self-inquiry; using the Plan–Act–Observe–Reflect cycle in a sprint structure; identifying focus areas in individual practice; formulating simple self-inquiry questions; using reflection to improve learning/work habits.

Possible self-inquiry questions: How do I plan my work? What usually blocks my progress? How do I respond to feedback? What patterns do I notice in my learning or coding practice?

Methodological tools: inquiry questions, simple observation logs, journaling, reflection through stand-ups/retrospectives, micro-experiments at individual level.

**Seasonal application:** ~3 sprints as action inquiry cycles. Each sprint: Plan (define a small inquiry focus), Act (work on Qwasar projects, test actions), Observe (track outcomes/blockers/feedback/patterns, share in stand-ups & team coaching), Reflect (sprint retrospective analysing what happened/learned/should improve).

Weekly activities: Stand-ups (progress sharing, visibility, surfacing inquiry), Squad work (main environment for testing ideas collaboratively), Retrospectives (formal reflection at end of each sprint), Team coaching (sense-making of process, habits, team dynamics, development).

**Vertical leadership integration:** at the start of each year, students complete a vertical leadership assessment to identify their leadership profile; conversations tracked via the Proairesis Platform to measure development, with feedback on developmental areas. Programme goal: by graduation, students show development toward "Expert." In Season 1 this is supported through self-awareness, accountability, openness to feedback, team contribution, disciplined participation in agile practice.

### Season 2: One-to-one Inquiry

Students learn active listening, effective questioning, giving/receiving feedback, adapting communication, and using dialogue to support collaboration.

Possible questions: How well do I listen before responding? What kinds of questions help me understand the other person better? How do I give feedback usefully and respectfully? How do I respond to critical feedback? How do I adapt my communication to different people/situations?

Methodological tools: Active listening (Listen–Paraphrase–Check); Effective questioning (Open–Clarify–Focus); Giving feedback (Situation–Behaviour–Impact); Dialogue for collaboration (Frame–Inquire–Reflect–Check).

### Season 3: Team Inquiry (5 weeks)

Shifts the inquiry focus from 1-1 engagement to the team: how to frame an inquiry at team level; using stand-ups/squad work/retrospectives to generate useful observations; facilitating constructive team conversations (e.g. appreciative inquiry, applicable later to programme-wide or client conversations); reflecting on shared delivery, not just individual contribution; using team dynamics to develop insights about practice development.

Possible questions: How does our squad/team handle blockers? What makes our stand-ups useful or unhelpful? Where do communication problems affect delivery? How do handovers, code reviews, or planning influence our sprint outcomes? What recurring team pattern is helping or slowing us down?

Methodological tools: team-based inquiry questions, micro-experiments in collaboration, team observation logs, sense-making in retrospectives, appreciative inquiry (keep, improve, innovate).

### Final Project Design Phase (12 weeks)

By the end of Season 3, students should identify the professional context for their final project (a Team Academy project or an external professional context) — this counts as the traineeship required to graduate.

**Weeks 1–4 (Questioning in Context):** Week 1 Understanding/clarifying context; Week 2 Stakeholder Conversations; Week 3 Context Exploration; Week 4 Problem Clarification.

**Weeks 5–8 (Framing and Refining the Inquiry):** Week 5 Inquiry Question; Week 6 Scope and Boundaries; Week 7 Requirements Framing; Week 8 Stakeholder Validation.

**Weeks 9–11 (Planning Action, Observation, Reflection):** Week 9 Solution Design; Week 10 Observation and Reflection Design; Week 11 Delivery Planning.

**Week 12:** Proposal Consolidation and VIVA — students present the proposal, showing it is context-based, inquiry-driven, and ready for implementation.

### Final Project Implementation Phase (21 weeks)

- Weeks 1–4: Act — begin implementing in the real professional context, first working parts of the solution.
- Weeks 5–8: Observe — continue development while gathering evidence from product, conversations, stakeholder context.
- Weeks 9–10: Reflect — review progress, make sense of what emerged, adjust the solution.
- Weeks 11–14: Iterate the Solution — build on learning, refine through further development.
- Weeks 15–17: Observe and Validate — assess quality/usefulness/readiness against requirements and stakeholder needs.
- Weeks 18–21: Reflect and Complete — final reflection, documentation, submission preparation, final presentation/VIVA.

## Contribution to NLQF 6 Skill Categories

| NLQF 6 Skill Category | Contribution of the Action Inquiry Line |
|---|---|
| Applying knowledge | Enables applying knowledge in authentic software engineering contexts through iterative inquiry cycles embedded in agile project work and the final project |
| Problem-solving skills | Supports identifying, analysing, and responding to practical, often unpredictable technical or collaborative problems during sprint-based work |
| Learning and development skills | Explicitly develops self-reflection, self-assessment, and continuous improvement as part of professional formation |
| Information skills | Supports collecting, interpreting, and using relevant information from practice, feedback, and project outcomes in inquiry and applied research |
| Communication skills | Develops purposeful communication through regular participation in stand-ups, squad interactions, retrospectives, and team coaching |
| Responsibility and independence | Supports the gradual development of ownership, accountability, and shared responsibility within agile and team-based learning environments |
