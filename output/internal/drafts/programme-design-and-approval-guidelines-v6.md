# Guidelines for the Design and Approval of Programmes at Schoolvision

## 1. Purpose and scope

These guidelines set out how Schoolvision designs and approves its programmes. They apply to all programmes and form part of the quality assurance system described in the Policy for Quality Assurance. A programme is approved by the Executive Board only when it meets the design framework in section 2 and has completed the approval process in section 3.

## 2. Design framework

Schoolvision designs every programme along seven dimensions: alignment and qualification, competency-based structure, learner-centric approach, applied learning, programmatic assessment, employability, and continuous improvement. Each dimension states what must be ensured before the Executive Board approves a programme.

### 2.1 Alignment and qualification

A programme starts from what the field needs and from the level and purposes of the qualification it leads to. The programme's objectives are in line with the institutional strategy, and its intended learning outcomes are explicit. There is a complete alignment between what the job market needs, the programme's intended outcomes and the standards of the qualification level in the Dutch national qualifications framework (NLQF) and the Framework for Qualifications of the European Higher Education Area (QF-EHEA). Master's programmes contain at least 60 ECTS. The programme reflects the four purposes of higher education set out by the Council of Europe: preparation for sustainable employment, preparation for life as active citizens in democratic societies, personal development, and the development and maintenance of a broad, advanced knowledge base. The qualification resulting from the programme is clearly specified and communicated, including in the Diploma Supplement.

### 2.2 Competency-based structure

The structure of a programme follows its competencies: what learners must master determines what is taught, how it builds up and how much time it takes. All programmes are competency-based, which means learning and assessment are built on a specific set of competencies and learning outcomes. Competency development progresses from basic to advanced, taking learning science frameworks (e.g. Bloom's Taxonomy) into account, which enables smooth progression of learners through the programme. All programmes combine technical skills development and leadership development, and conclude with a final project. All programmes indicate the workload and the ECTS credits for each certificate and the entire programme, A programme may include a foundational module for the entry knowledge and skills, for example where it develops technical skills.

### 2.3 Learner-centric approach

Programmes are designed to help each learner take an active part in their own learning, in teams and with the support of the faculty. There is a balance between individual and group learning activities, stimulating and enhancing the social construction of knowledge and skills development (e.g. some of the assignments and projects are team-based). Learners are provided with first-hand insights and learning support from the faculty through workshop hours, mentoring or supervision hours. The workload and ECTS credits of each certificate and of the entire programme are made available to the faculty and the learners.

### 2.4 Applied learning

Learners develop their competence by working on real-life-like problems and contexts throughout the programme. Weekly assignments and module projects ensure that learning is applied in this way. There is a balance between direct instruction and opportunities for experiential learning and practice, especially in synchronous sessions or workshops. Where a programme includes a work-based component such as a traineeship, it is set out in an arrangement that defines its duration, supervision and assessment.

### 2.5 Programmatic assessment

Assessment shows what learners have mastered and guides their learning along the way. Learning is assessed programmatically, in line with the competency-based approach and the Policy on Assessment. Evidence of each learner's competence and progress is collected continuously across the programme through low-, medium- and high-stake assessments. Low-stake assessments drive learning through feedback and feedforward, medium-stake assessments are signed off by an official representative of the organisation, and high-stake decisions at progression moments are taken by a panel of examiners. The assessments are built on the programme's competencies and learning outcomes, and the learner's portfolio collects the learning evidence.

### 2.6 Employability

Programmes prepare learners for the labour market and help them show what they can do. All programmes contain a mandatory module (Talent Accelerator) to support learners' employability skills, and an optional and invitation-only module that offers opportunities to connect with potential employers. All programmes lead to portfolio development, so that learners can showcase learning mastery on a platform of their choice that suits the subject matter and the industry. The employability status of the learners is one of the success metrics of the programme.

### 2.7 Continuous improvement

A programme keeps improving after approval, using feedback and results. All programmes are subject to improvements and enhancements based on on-going formal and informal learner and faculty feedback, as well as the success metrics of the programme (e.g. competency development and employability status of the learners). After approval, each programme is monitored and reviewed annually under the Policy for Quality Assurance, and major changes return to the Executive Board for approval.

## 3. Approval process

The approval process has seven stages. Programme owners, who are the Head of Programmes and the Deans, lead stages 1 to 6. Field Representatives contribute to stages 1 and 3, and the Executive Board decides at stage 7.

### Stage 1. Job market research (Programme owners and Field Representatives)

Conducting research to identify the needs and the requirements of the job market (e.g. desktop research, consulting professionals and employers). Field Representatives are consulted at this stage.

### Stage 2. Competency development (Programme owners)

Development of the competency set and the specific learning outcomes based on the research.

### Stage 3. Validation of the competencies (Programme owners and Field Representatives)

Validation of the competency set with experts through surveys and workshops, as well as seeking advice from the Field Representatives. Learners and faculty take part in these surveys and workshops, so that the design reflects their perspective.

### Stage 4. High level design (Programme owners)

Development of the high level design of the programme based on the validated competency set.

### Stage 5. Low level design (Programme owners)

Development of the low level design of the programme (module development, including curation of the digital content, the assignments and projects).

### Stage 6. Assessment development (Programme owners)

Development of the programme's assessment approach and its assessments, in line with the Policy on Assessment. The low-, medium- and high-stake assessments are mapped to the validated competencies and learning outcomes, and the assessment methods and criteria are communicated to learners.

### Stage 7. Approval (Executive Board)

Approval of the programme by the Executive Board, and listing of the programme on the institute's website.

## 4. Approval record

Each approval is recorded in a dated decision of the Executive Board.
