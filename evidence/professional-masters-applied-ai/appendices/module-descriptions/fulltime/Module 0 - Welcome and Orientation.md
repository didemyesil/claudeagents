# Module 0: Welcome and Orientation (Full Time)

This module serves as your entry point into the Professional Master's in Applied AI. It provides both a practical introduction to the programme and an experiential immersion into its inquiry-based ethos.

The first part of the module focuses on orientation to the academic environment: understanding programme expectations, and meeting faculty and peers. You will be introduced to the structure of the Professional Master's, its assessment model, and the key values and practices that underpin it.

Alongside this, you will engage in community-building activities during an intensive on-campus period. Through dialogue, collaborative exercises, and shared reflection, you will explore the programme's values and begin to establish a supportive learning culture with your peers.

As a foundation for your Applied Research line, you will be introduced to the inquiry mindset, a way of approaching professional practice through curiosity, reflection, and open questioning. This mindset forms the bedrock of action research and will accompany you throughout the programme.

By the end of the module, you will be familiar with the programme's expectations and tools, connected to your learning community, and prepared to embark on the first certificate with an inquiry approach.

## Objectives of the Module

| Form of learning | Objectives |
|---|---|
| Face-to-face activities such as presentations, workshops, and trips | Understand the structure, expectations, and values of the Professional Master's programme; begin developing leadership self-awareness through group engagement and experiential activities; connect with peers, mentors, and faculty in a supportive learning community; articulate initial reflections on personal learning goals and leadership aspirations; apply action research methodology with qualitative and participatory inquiry approaches to design interventions in professional contexts. |

## What can you expect from this module?

- Canvas Orientation Module: self-paced orientation guides on the learning management system.
- On-Campus Activities: programme introductions, group activities, facilitated dialogue, reflective exercises, and peer engagement.
- Peer Introductions and Community Agreements.

## Learning Resources

Must-read resources for this module (specific weekly material shared on Canvas):

- Brydon-Miller, M., Greenwood, D., Maguire, P. and members of the editorial board of Action Research, 2003. Why action research? *Action Research*, 1(1), pp.9–28. SAGE Publications.
- Bradbury Huang, H., 2010. What is good action research? Why the resurgent interest? *Action Research*, 8(1), pp.93–109. SAGE Publications.
- Marshall, J. and Reason, P., 2007. Quality in research as 'taking an attitude of inquiry'. *Management Research News*, Special Issue on Action Research, June.
- McNiff, J., 2016. *Writing up your action research project.* London: Routledge.
