# Policy for Quality Assurance at Team Academy — Bachelor's in Applied Computing

*(This is Team Academy's institution-wide Quality Assurance Policy, adapted with Bachelor's-in-Applied-Computing-specific references. The same institution-wide policy also appears, in its Master's-programme-adapted form, at `/evidence/professional-masters-applied-ai/appendices/Appendix 21 - Quality Assurance Policy.md` and as the generic institution-wide version in `/evidence/_shared/Institution-wide Quality Assurance Policy (Team Academy).md`.)*

## ESG 1.1. Policy for Quality Assurance

*"Institutes should have a policy for quality assurance that is made public and forms part of their strategic management. Internal stakeholders should develop and implement this policy through appropriate structures and processes, while involving external stakeholders."*

### 1.1.1. Context and Objectives

Team Academy's vision is to bridge the global skills gap; it develops and offers Bachelor's degree programmes in technology, including the Bachelor's in Applied Computing, preparing learners for applied computing roles and further professional/academic development. Through its quality assurance system the institute ensures: a quality culture maintained by all community members; relevant content/competencies/planning facilitating learning experience and employability; programmes developed in connection with global job-market needs; ongoing improvement complying with international accreditation requirements.

### 1.1.2. Concept of Quality

Quality is based on the extent to which processes/procedures establish and constantly improve an educational standard, and on the interaction/relationship between all parties and activities (staff, external partners, learners, learning resources, learning environment, support mechanisms).

### 1.1.3. Guiding Principles

Adherence to common standards (ESG); Collaboration; Transparency; Innovation; Agility; Reflection.

### 1.1.4 Organisation of the Quality Assurance System

Two layers: **Institute Quality Assurance** (external players: Ministry of Education Culture and Science, NVAO; internal: Executive Board, Management Teams) and **Programme Quality Assurance** (external: Field Representatives, Knowledge Partners; internal: Exam Board, Executive Board, Managing Director, QA Team, Programme Teams, Educational Council, Admissions Teams, Faculty, Learners).

Team Academy is subject to oversight by the Ministry of Education, Culture and Science (OCW, regulatory framework) and NVAO (accreditation/institutional QA). The Institute Board sets strategic vision; management teams implement it; governance is collaborative/iterative with active input from faculty/staff/learners. Periodical performance reviews and monthly Executive Board/programme-team check-ins maintain transparency and agility.

Programme-level QA is informed by institute QA policy; external players (field representatives, global faculty, content partners) connect the academic profile to sector requirements. Internal organisation involves curriculum teams, management teams, admissions teams, faculty, and learners, coordinated by the QA Team (programme managers, led by a QA Lead), reporting to the Executive Board.

### 1.1.5 Evaluation of the Quality Assurance System

Ongoing evaluation of policies/procedures/guidelines in collaboration with the Executive Board and other teams; QA Team holds main evaluation responsibility; Executive Board approves iterative improvements; all stakeholder feedback (including learners) is incorporated.

**Review of this Policy document and stakeholder satisfaction with it (planned mechanism):** Beyond the ongoing evaluation of the QA *system* above, this Policy document is itself subject to a planned review cycle, distinct from the programme/module/activity-level cycles in section 1.1.6 — annual review by the QA Team ahead of each academic year, proposing revisions arising from regulatory change (ESG, NVAO, WHW), Field Representative or Educational Council input, or experience applying the policy; any revision formally approved by the Executive Board. The first annual review falls due ahead of the cluster's first academic year. Once the programme is operational, satisfaction with this policy document specifically (as distinct from programme-level learner satisfaction under ESG 1.7) will be assessed within the QA Team's annual review, drawing on feedback from faculty, programme management, the Educational Council (student representatives) and Field Representatives. Presented as a planned, ex-ante mechanism, not a record of reviews already conducted.

**Plan–Do–Check–Act (PDCA) Cycle:** Plan (curriculum team + faculty + field representatives design curriculum, set strategic objectives); Do (deliver learning activities, assessments, coaching); Check (systematic internal/external feedback — reflections, learner surveys, exam board reviews, performance reviews — against KPIs/programme outcomes); Act (targeted improvements approved by QA team and executive board). Applied across activity, module, and programme levels.

### 1.1.6. Multi-layered Approach to Quality Assurance

**Activity level** — workshops, learning resources, assignments, projects, team coaching, supervision: designed by curriculum/programme management teams + field experts; continuous learner feedback; immediate action on feedback.

**Module level** — focused learning episodes developing defined learning outcomes/competencies/technical foundations/applied professional skills: designed per programme objectives; learner feedback gathered and acted on; competency development evaluated via module-level assessment evidence, projects, portfolio evidence, and progression/high-stake assessment moments; alignment improved between academic profile, Bachelor-level competencies, and applied computing field needs.

**Programme level** — three success metrics: extent to which the programme helps learners achieve intended learning outcomes/Bachelor-level competency; quality of the final project as evidence of Bachelor-level professional/applied-computing capability; employability/further-study readiness. Cycle: design/plan with field experts based on labour-market needs and intended graduate profile; evaluate competency development via module assessments/portfolio evidence/final project; improve alignment based on success metrics.

## ESG 1.2. Design and Approval of Programmes

Curriculum Team designs/develops programmes with field representatives; Executive Board formally approves programmes and major changes; internal procedures govern design and approval. Curriculum team continuously reviews/improves curriculum with faculty, students, and field representatives per monitoring/review/improvement guidelines.

## ESG 1.3. Student-Centred Learning, Teaching and Assessment

Learners take an active role in their learning and the community's; faculty follow guidelines for learner-centred experience; teaching/examination rules align with competency-based standards per the Teaching and Examination Regulations. Curriculum team and programme management ensure: curriculum structure/assessment strategies are in educational handbooks and available to learners; learners know their responsibilities; extension/appeal procedures are defined and available.

## ESG 1.4. Student Admission, Progression, Recognition and Certification

**Student admission** — professional, transparent, non-discriminatory handling of all applicants; admissions consultants assess suitability per defined procedures.

**Learner progression** — each learner should reach/surpass intended mastery levels; support/intervention provided as needed; programme management ensures information availability; the Bachelor's programme follows its TER, Assessment Policy, and programme handbook for progression through modules, projects, and the final graduation phase.

**Recognition and certification** — Team Academy holds Recognised Legal Entity for Higher Education (RPHO) status in the Netherlands under the WHW; consistent certificate/diploma standards; programme management follows recognition/certification guidelines.

## ESG 1.5. Teaching Staff

Fair, transparent recruitment/onboarding/development processes; programme management selects/develops teaching staff per recruitment and professional-development guidelines.

## ESG 1.6. Learning Resources and Learner Support

Appropriate learning resources, learning environment, and learner support across programmes, to achieve the intended graduate profile.

**Learning Resources and Communication** — flexibility for diverse learners (part-time/full-time employed); learning resources/communication tools comply with relevant guidelines.

**Learner Support** — each programme has a programme manager coordinating faculty/learner interaction; regular Town Halls; learner support via team coaching, supervision, and squad groups; peer-learning/peer-feedback engagement; programme managers monitor progress and provide guidance; team coaches/supervisors follow programme management guidelines.

## ESG 1.7 Information Management

Institute management uses a performance review system to track organisational goals; programme management teams keep/provide data (learner progress, mastery, assessment results, drop-out rates) on the institute's LMS; survey results/reflections securely stored on the LMS; other data kept in internal documents. Data tracked includes: student population/profiles; completion/drop-out rates; number of degrees; number of students placed in jobs; learner workload; learning mastery scores; overall certificate scores; qualitative/quantitative learner satisfaction data; learner-to-academic-staff ratio.

## ESG 1.8. Public Information

Marketing team ensures the institute publishes: programme details (objectives, curriculum, target competencies, credits, faculty); admission criteria/steps; learning approach; programme brochures on request; online application forms.

**Publication status of this Policy document.** The information listed above is *programme*-level. This Policy document itself is currently an internal governance document — shared with staff, faculty, the Educational Council (student representatives) and Field Representatives, but not yet published on the institute's public website, It is published on the institute's website at the launch of the programmes. 

## ESG 1.9 On-going Monitoring and Periodic Review of Programmes

Constant monitoring/review to ensure programmes achieve intended learning outcomes/graduate profile and stay connected to the job market/society, per established guidelines.

## ESG 1.10 Cyclical External Quality Assurance

Deliberate effort to raise academic standards through accreditations such as NVAO; close formal/informal contact with field experts and employers to inform quality improvement.
