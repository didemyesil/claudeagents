### **1.2 Our Educational Philosophy**

At Team Academy, learning is not just about passing exams or collecting credits; it's about growing into the kind of professional who can lead in complex, evolving environments. We believe in education that is **practical, flexible, and rooted in real-world relevance**.

Our model is built on the idea of **full-stack education**. This means we integrate technical mastery, leadership development, and reflective practice into one coherent learning journey. You’ll build a strong foundation in applied artificial intelligence, while also strengthening the practitioner's research skills. Through the integration of **action research,** you’ll learn to approach professional challenges as cycles of inquiry and improvement. This means you won’t just apply AI tools, but will systematically investigate how your decisions, interventions, and solutions create impact within real organisational and social contexts. By combining data-driven experimentation with reflective practice, you’ll develop the capacity to learn from action, refine your methods, and generate evidence-based insights that advance both your own expertise and your organisation’s effectiveness. In doing so, you cultivate **leadership through reflection and responsible action**, and develop the mindset and capability to lead yourself and others in navigating complexity and change.

We follow a **competency-based approach**, which means your learning is measured by how well you can apply your knowledge in action, not just how well you can explain it on paper. Throughout your journey, you'll collect evidence of your growth, engage in critical reflection, and receive meaningful feedback that helps you move forward.

This programme is designed in close collaboration with industry, which means you're not learning in a vacuum. What you’ll do here is directly aligned with the skills and mindsets that employers and changemakers are looking for  today and in the future.

You won’t spend your time in passive lectures. Instead, your learning will be **hands-on and experience-based**. You’ll work on data, complete projects, and share your process and insights with peers and facilitators. You’ll explore digital tools, collaborate across cultures, and challenge yourself in a supportive but rigorous environment.

At Team Academy, we trust you to take the lead in your own learning, and we’re here to support you every step of the way.

### **1.3 Our Philosophy: Leadership as Inquiry in Action**

Leadership for us is not a separate skill to be taught, but a way of engaging with complexity; in how we learn, how we collaborate, and how we operate as a learning community. It shapes our actions, our relationships, and the impact we strive to create within and beyond our learning community.

We aspire to live by the same principles we teach. Leadership is practiced not only by learners but by faculty and all institutional teams, as we operate through shared responsibility, transparency, and continuous reflection. ### **1.4 Programme Outcomes**

The programme outcomes of the Professional Master's in Applied Artificial Intelligence at Team Academy are based on the current developments in AI and practitioner research with leadership development. They are shaped in close dialogue with the professional field to ensure that the curriculum remains responsive to the rapidly evolving AI landscape and the needs of society. In our view, a Professional Master's in Applied AI must enable graduates to work autonomously and responsibly on complex, real-world challenges, using advanced AI methods alongside reflective leadership and cycles of inquiry to create meaningful solutions. 

The curriculum therefore integrates technical expertise such as machine learning, deep learning, natural language processing, large language models, retrieval-augmented generation, computer vision, responsible AI, and MLOps, with action research methodology and its inquiry-based tools at the self, group and organisational levels. Through this integration, students not only gain mastery of applied AI techniques but also learn to apply them responsibly, collaboratively, and with impact. This holistic approach aligns with the **Dutch Qualifications Framework (NLQF) at level 7**, which emphasises mastery, research competence, interprofessional conduct, and effect in professional practice. 

Thus, the programme outcomes are identified as follows:

**1. Mastery***  
*Graduates demonstrate mastery of Applied AI by acting autonomously and ethically in complex and uncertain contexts, reflecting critically on their personal leadership practice, and integrating responsible AI principles into professional decision-making.

**2. Research Competence**

Graduates are able to design and conduct practitioner research that frames business problems into AI solutions, analyse and model data with advanced techniques, and generate insights through cycles of inquiry and reflection, which contributes to fresh thinking and applications in their professional practice.

**3. Interprofessional Conduct***  
*Graduates collaborate effectively across disciplines and organisational levels, facilitating dialogue and interventions in diverse professional and cultural contexts, and communicating complex AI concepts and research outcomes clearly to both specialist and non-specialist audiences.

**4. Effect***  
*Graduates deliver measurable impact in their professional practice by deploying AI systems in organisational settings with a sense of responsibility, transferring knowledge across domains, and helping to shape future-oriented strategies that have impact on their work and communities.

*The intended programme outcomes are aligned with the Dutch National Qualifications Framework (NLQF) at level 7, reflecting the standards expected of a professional master’s degree in the Netherlands.*

###  **1.5 The T-Shaped Professional**

The professional world no longer rewards only deep expertise - it demands the ability to work across boundaries, communicate across disciplines, and lead in complexity. At Team Academy , we embrace the **T-shaped professional** as the ideal graduate profile for our master's programme.

In this programme, the **vertical bar** of the “T” is deliberately extended, which represents your **advanced depth of expertise** in applied artificial intelligence. You will gain the analytical, methodological, and technical mastery required to design, implement, and critically evaluate AI-driven solutions within real-world contexts.

The **horizontal bar** complements this depth with your capacity to collaborate across disciplines, integrate practitioner research, and reflect on the human, organisational, and ethical dimensions of your work. You will develop the ability to engage stakeholders, facilitate inquiry, and translate complex insights into responsible action.

This combination of **deep technical mastery and broad reflective capability** defines the ideal graduate; a professional able to apply advanced AI knowledge with autonomy, ethical awareness, and impact.

### **3.3 Final Project: Integrating Your Learning in Practice**

The Final Project is the concluding and most integrative part of your master's. It is where your learning across all two competency lines comes together in a real-world, impact-oriented project.

This is your opportunity to demonstrate not only your technical ability, but also your leadership insight, reflective capacity, and ability to deliver meaningful change through structured action research inquiry.

### **Purpose of the Final Project**

The Final Project is a **work-based, inquiry-driven project** that brings together your learning from data science & AI, leadership, and practitioner research. Grounded in action research, its purpose is to help you investigate the question:

**“How can I improve my practice?”**

It enables you to:

  - **Apply** your competencies in a real-world context  
      
  - **Design** a change initiative grounded in data, AI and leadership practice  
      
  - **Iterate** through structured research cycles  
      
  - Deliver measurable and meaningful impact on a **societal or organisational challenge**

Refer to the **Final Project Learner Guidelines** for more information such as the research methodology and the evaluation criteria.

### **4.2 Assessment of Your Learning**

At Team Academy, assessment is not something that happens only at the end, it’s part of your learning from the very beginning. Every assignment, project, and reflection you complete is designed to help you demonstrate growth, receive feedback, and take your learning further.

Assessment is **competency-based**. This means your work is evaluated on how well you can apply it in real-world situations. Each task is mapped to specific competencies and learning outcomes from your learning lines, whether that’s building a machine learning model, contributing to a change process, or reflecting on your professional growth.

**What Gets Assessed**

You will be assessed through various types of assignments to demonstrate mastery in the target learning outcomes throughout the programme. Some of this learning evidence is low-stake while some is medium and high-stake assessments. **Each learning outcome should be supported by at least three low-stake, two medium-stake, and one high-stake evidence**. 

**Low-stake** means they are rather developmental and help you prepare for medium and high-stake assessments. For example, your weekly assignments or workshop exercises are low-stake assessments, and these do not count toward the learning mastery scores.

**Medium-stake** means they are evaluated officially and you receive formal feedback and feedforward on them. They are more comprehensive and integrate more learning outcomes. For instance, module projects and integrated group projects are medium-stake assessments. They count toward your learning mastery scores.

**High-stake** means an important progression decision moment where you present your whole certification portfolio to a panel of assessors and you join a VIVA meeting with them.

The Examination Board at Team Academy plays a critical role in safeguarding the academic standards and integrity of your programme. ## **When You Interact With the Examination Board**

You normally interact with the Examination Board in the following situations:

### **1. Appeals and Complaints**

You may contact the Examination Board if you wish to appeal or file a complaint about:

  - a progression decision
  - an intake or certification decision
  - a procedural error in examination or assessment

If you disagree with the Examination Board’s decision, you may appeal to the university’s Board of Appeals.

### **2. Exceptional Circumstances**

If personal circumstances have seriously affected your ability to study or complete assessments, you can apply for exceptional circumstances. These may relate to:

  - illness or medical reasons
  - serious personal or family situations
  - circumstances outside your control

  - retaking a certification
  - retaking a module
  - reconsideration of a progression decision  
      

Initial consultations take place with the Programme Manager. If your application is rejected, you may appeal to the Examination Board.

Learners who are unable to meet an assessment deadline may request an extension in advance by submitting the designated extension form to programme management. Extension requests for regular assignments must be submitted **at least four days** before the deadline indicated in Canvas. Supporting evidence (e.g., medical documentation) may be required depending on the nature of the request. The decision authority for **low- and medium-stakes assessments** lies with the Programme Manager; for **high-stakes assessments**, including major integrative assignments, the decision authority lies with the **Examination Board**. Decisions on extension requests must be communicated to the learner within **four days** of the request.

The Examination Board endorses the following structured approach for managing extension requests:

  - **First Request: Automatically Granted****  
    ** The first extension request for any assessment is approved automatically, provided it is submitted on time and through the designated process.
  - **Second Request: Requires Reflective Justification****  
    ** A second request requires the learner to provide a written reflection explaining why an additional extension is necessary, acknowledging the responsibilities of combining work, personal life, and part-time Master’s study, and outlining how they will prevent further extension needs.
  - **Third Request: Referral to Internal Confidential Advisor****  
    ** A third request triggers a mandatory referral to an **internal confidential advisor**, who can support the learner in addressing underlying challenges affecting study progress. The extension may be granted only after this consultation.

### **4. Exemptions and Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL)**

If you believe you have previously achieved the learning outcomes of a module through earlier study or professional experience, you may request an exemption (RPL). The Examination Board evaluates and decides on these requests.

### **5. Academic Integrity Cases**

The Examination Board handles cases involving:

  - plagiarism
  - unauthorised collaboration
  - fraud
  - breaches of academic integrity

Consequences depend on the severity of the misconduct and may include invalidation of an assessment or further measures.

### **6. Final Project Completion**

The Examination Board verifies that all academic and administrative requirements for graduation have been met before a diploma is issued. Certificates and diplomas are awarded only after the Board has confirmed that all requirements are fulfilled.

**How to Contact the Examination Board**

Requests, appeals, and applications should be submitted:

  - in writing (e-mail),
  - with supporting documents,
  - via the Programme Manager, who forwards them to the Chair of the Examination Board.

You will receive confirmation of receipt and a timeframe for the decision.

All communications with the Examination Board should be done using the designated channels, forms and following the procedures outlined in the TER.

# **Appendix 3: Failure and Second Chance Opportunity Procedure** 

Within a programmatic assessment model, progression is based on the continuous collection of evidence demonstrating competency development. Instead of defining failure at the level of individual assessments, the programme evaluates whether sufficient evidence has been provided to support a judgement at the certification decision point.

A certification is considered fail when, at the certification decision point:

• the total body of evidence across all learning outcomes and evidence points contributing to that certification does not demonstrate the required level of mastery

 • no or incomplete evidence has been submitted within the defined timeframe and no allowance has been approved under exceptional circumstances

 • academic conduct regulations have been breached, resulting in evidence being invalidated for decision making

The panel evaluates evidence holistically. A certification is not achieved when the collected evidence does not sufficiently demonstrate the required competency level.

Progression to the next certification despite not yet achieving the previous one:

When a student has not yet demonstrated sufficient evidence at the certification decision point, they may still proceed to the next certification, provided that:

• the next certification includes learning outcomes, projects or applied tasks that offer meaningful opportunities to further develop or demonstrate the outcomes that were not yet achieved

 • continuation is pedagogically appropriate and does not hinder the student’s overall learning trajectory

 • faculty identify any specific competencies that require additional focus during the following certification

This continuation does not replace the requirement to ultimately achieve the previous certification. Instead, it allows the student to benefit from naturally occurring evidence opportunities in later certification projects and applied activities.

A certification remains not yet achieved until sufficient evidence is demonstrated, and the final certification decision can be made independently of the student’s participation in subsequent certifications.

**Retaking a certification in a subsequent cohort**

Students who, despite given opportunities, still have not demonstrated sufficient evidence by the end of the next certification may retake the certification in a subsequent cohort.

**Retaking a certification means:**

 • continuing evidence development with the next cohort

 • following the learning activities, assessment opportunities and competency expectations of that cohort

 • generating new or updated evidence where requirements have changed or where existing evidence is insufficient

**Reflective requirements and approval process**

Students preparing to retake a certification must submit:

 • a reflective account explaining prior challenges and evidence gaps

 • a rigorous action plan describing how they will approach evidence development differently

Faculty/panel evaluates the request.

**Evidence validity**

Previously collected evidence remains valid unless:

• academic misconduct has taken place

 • competency standards have changed

 • the evidence has become outdated or insufficient for decision making

Students may be required to produce additional or updated evidence as needed.

**Finality of certification**

Once a certification has been achieved, students are not permitted to retake it or submit further evidence for it.

**Exceptional Circumstances**

Exceptional circumstances are personal circumstances that:

• are beyond the student’s control

 • could not reasonably have been prevented or accommodated

 • have had a significant and demonstrable negative effect on the student’s ability to generate evidence of learning

 • occurred during the period relevant to the affected certification or evidence-development period

All elements must be met for an exceptional circumstances application to be considered.

**Temporary Withdrawal**

• A student wishing to withdraw temporarily must obtain written approval for a specific period (maximum five years) and may need to satisfy conditions before re-enrolling.

 • Students who withdraw without permission or who fail to re-enrol by the agreed date may be considered permanently withdrawn.

 • Students who fail to maintain enrolment and do not respond to communication from their supervisor or the institute for the duration of one certification period will be withdrawn.

 • Students with approved temporary withdrawal may rejoin a later cohort and continue developing evidence toward certification.

**Application Procedure for Exceptional Circumstances**

**Initial contact**

 Students must contact their programme manager within two weeks of being informed that a certification was not yet achieved. The programme manager conducts an advisory consultation to review circumstances, potential evidence, and the application process. A submission deadline is agreed; missing it means forfeiting the opportunity to apply.

**Submission of the application**

 Students complete the exceptional circumstances form by the agreed deadline. The form includes the option to request a certification retake or temporary withdrawal.

**Review and decision**

 Students submit the application and supporting documentation to the program manager of the Professional master's in Applied AI programme. Students receive a decision within three weeks. The decision is final within the institutional framework. Students may pursue the external NRTO dispute and appeals procedure if applicable.

  

**Appendix 4:** **Academic Appeals Procedure**

This guide is intended to help explain the Academic Appeals process. If you are considering making an Academic Appeal, perhaps because you do not agree with your enrolment status or another decision of the programme management or faculty, you are encouraged to read this guide with care.

This guidance document has been prepared by the university and please remember that every Academic Appeal is considered individually, and general statements noted below may not apply in all circumstances. Academic Appeal might take several weeks to process.

  

**Academic Appeals**

An academic appeal is a procedure which allows you in certain circumstances to ask for a review of a decision relating to your academic progress or award. Circumstances in which you may submit an appeal include the following:

·       a decision on progress has been taken which prevents you from continuing your studies, usually a decision to terminate your registration

·       a decision has been taken which requires you to interrupt your studies

·       a decision has been taken not to make an award to you and the consequence is that your studies will be frozen or terminated

·       a decision to award a lower qualification to you than that for which you are registered, for example if you are registered for a master’s degree and are recommended for a certificate.

In an Academic Appeal, any claims that you make must, generally, be supported by compelling evidence. The type of evidence that is acceptable varies significantly, but generally includes medical certificates, statements by academic staff about particular events, copies of specific regulations, detailed personal statements, confirmation from authorities that you were involved in a particular incident. Contemporaneous, independent and medical evidence is very important and is often given greater weight by an Appeal Panel. It is also important that the evidence indicates the significance of the impact on you – and relates to specific time periods.

It is your responsibility to provide the evidence for the Appeal Panel to consider – the institute will not approach doctors or others on your behalf.

**Grounds for Appeal**

You may not appeal if your results are not as good as you had hoped or worse than you believe you deserve. Appeals which simply challenge the academic judgement of internal or external evaluators are not permitted. The grounds for appeal are summarised below:

·       there were circumstances materially affecting your performance, for which supporting evidence exists, which were not known to the Academic Panel or other academic body at the time of the decision and which were not reasonably practicable to make known beforehand

·       there appears to you to be evidence that there has been a procedural irregularity in the conduct of assessment or assessment procedures of such a nature as to create a reasonable possibility that the result may have been different if it had not occurred

*A ‘procedural irregularity’ means that the examining and assessment process was not conducted according to the institute’s guidelines and procedures. Examples might include errors in an assessment grading or with the administration of an examination, or a failure to calculate the marks correctly or to consider any mitigating circumstances submitted by you before the due date.*

·       there appears to you to be evidence of prejudice or bias on the part of the evaluators or in the assessment process

It is important to note that the regulations on mitigating circumstances require programme learners to notify their faculty of any matters which may be relevant to their academic performance, for example, personal or medical circumstances, at the time they occur and to supply supporting documentary evidence, such as medical certificates. Unless there were very good reasons why you were unable to notify your faculty members or supply evidence at the time it is likely that an appeal will be disallowed.

You should note that the institute may operate systems of double scoring or moderation to ensure that there are adequate checks on the accuracy and appropriateness of marking and to help prevent prejudice and bias. Additionally, scores are awarded to a learner’s work based on the academic judgement of the evaluators in conjunction with an agreed assessment criterion in the form of rubrics relevant to the assignment in question. It is also important to remember that the institute has a competency-based model learning which encourages the learners to always increase their mastery levels and scores through second submissions per the resubmission procedures stated in the Teaching and Examination Regulations.

**How to submit an appeal**

If you decide that you have eligible grounds for appeal, you should complete and submit an appeal form with supporting documentary evidence. Please ensure that you complete the [**Academic Appeal form**](https://elu2.typeform.com/to/vAkwfr) as required rather than submitting a statement or letter of appeal.

Your deadline to submit an appeal will be 10 institute working days from the results release date. Please note that not submitting it by the stated deadline will normally result in the appeal being disallowed.

You must appeal on your own behalf and it is your responsibility to obtain the required evidence. The institute will not contact doctors, your company or others on your behalf. If you require help in compiling your appeal, your community manager is able to provide support and advice but cannot write your appeal for you.

It is important that you submit evidence to support your appeal. Where medical evidence is submitted this must be from a qualified medical practitioner. If you are resident overseas you must ensure that any medical evidence is submitted in English. If it was not written in English, you must supply a certified translation. The institute undertakes checks to ensure that evidence submitted is genuine.

The Examination Board will check whether your appeal contains eligible grounds. Information or a report from your faculty members (facilitators or mentors) may be obtained to assist this decision. If your appeal does not demonstrate eligible grounds you will be notified in writing and either advised that the appeal cannot be accepted or offered the opportunity to supply more information or evidence within a stated timescale.

If you are informed your appeal does not demonstrate eligible grounds, then you will be given the opportunity to request a review of this decision. To do so you will have to show that there was a procedural irregularity in the way your appeal has been considered or it appears that evidence of an eligible ground of appeal has not been fully considered. Your request will be considered by the Appeal Panel who will either decide no valid ground for further review has been established and conclude the appeal process at this point.

  

Academic appeals are initially handled by the Examination Board of the programme. Programme learners can not attend exam board meetings, however in the procedure of an appeal, both the involved candidate as well as educator can be invited to a hearing, should the examination board deems this necessary. Cases are considered on the basis of the documentation they have provided.

**Appeal Decision**

You will usually receive formal notification of the outcome of your case within seven working days of the meeting. The outcome letter will explain the reasons for the formal decision. The outcome letter will also highlight the opportunity **to request a review** of the board’s decision, if you believe that there was a procedural irregularity in the way your appeal has been considered or that there is evidence within the appeal which it appears has not been fully considered. Should you want to appeal a decision taken by the Examination Board, you have the right to appeal decisions of the Examination Board through the independent NRTO disputes and appeals procedure.

Once the institute’s appeal procedures have been concluded you will be issued with a **“Completion of Procedures Letter”.**
