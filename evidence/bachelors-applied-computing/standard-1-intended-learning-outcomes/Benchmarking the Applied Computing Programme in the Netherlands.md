# Benchmarking a Dutch HBO Applied Computing Programme

## Executive Summary

The strongest primary comparator set for an HBO Applied Computing programme with three tracks (Software Engineering, Data Science, Machine Learning) is **Saxion**, **Hanze**, **Windesheim**, and **University of Twente**. Together these cover the relevant Dutch benchmark archetypes: an applied SE bachelor with AI adjacency (Saxion); a broad HBO-ICT labour-market benchmark (Hanze); a modular HBO-ICT programme with explicit Software Engineering and Applied Data Science & AI pathways (Windesheim); and a public-university benchmark for deeper computing/data/AI foundations (University of Twente). Reserve comparators: **Fontys ICT** and **UvA Artificial Intelligence**.

**Key strategic conclusion:** a standalone Machine Learning bachelor track remains unusual in the Dutch comparator set — Saxion embeds AI inside SE; Hanze has no dedicated DS/ML major; Windesheim combines DS and AI into one track; UT covers AI/data within an academic CS structure rather than a professional bachelor track. The three-track architecture (SE/DS/ML) is therefore a potential differentiator, especially if the ML track is framed as **machine-learning engineering** rather than only "applied modelling."

Saxion and Hanze foreground real external assignments from early in the programme; Windesheim is unusually strong on modularity/assessment design (extended time windows to demonstrate competences rather than exam weeks); UT is the clearest benchmark for ML-related depth.

**Highest-value recommendations:** keep a substantial common engineering core; separate Data Science and Machine Learning by professional role rather than buzzword; require visible work-integrated learning each year; publish a transparent programme map with modules/credits; diversify assessment into projects, reports, presentations, portfolios; build stackable short-form learning offers connected to the bachelor.

Six institutions: Saxion University of Applied Sciences, Hanze University of Applied Sciences Groningen, Windesheim, University of Twente, Fontys University of Applied Sciences, University of Amsterdam. Plus NOVI Hogeschool as a further reserve comparator for flexible/modular provision.

Note: Dutch higher education uses EC and ECTS interchangeably (1 EC = 28 study hours per Hanze).

## Benchmarking Matrix (Summary)

**Saxion – ICT Software Engineering:** SE very strong; DS moderate (Data & AI Engineering); ML moderate (AI focus, not separate track). 4-year English-taught HBO bachelor; HAVO/VWO/MBO-4 equivalent + IELTS 6.0/TOEFL iBT 80. Years 1–2: 120 ECTS incl. Introduction to Programming, Databases, Complex Data Structures, Parallel Computing, OOP, Requirements Engineering, Algorithms & Data Structures, DevOps, Software Development Principles, Web Basics, System Design, multiple projects. Later specialisations: Data & AI Engineering, IoT, Advanced App Development. Practical assignments with professionals from Year 1. 92% of graduates find work at their level within 1.5 years.

**Hanze – ICT:** SE strong; DS/ML weak-to-moderate (role destinations, not named majors). 4-year Dutch HBO bachelor; HAVO/VWO/MBO-4 + 21+/foreign-diploma routes. 240 ECTS total; Year 1 broad ICT base (programming, infrastructure, business understanding, real client project); after first half-year choose Software Engineering, Network & Security Engineering, or Business IT & Management. Assessment mixes multiple-choice exams, reports, presentations. Outcomes: 82% found work in field, 88% at degree level, est. start salary €3,200, ~3 months to first job, 71% fixed contracts, 4.2/5 career satisfaction.

**Windesheim – ICT:** SE strong; DS strong; ML moderate-to-strong (inside Applied Data Science & AI, not separate track). 4-year Dutch HBO bachelor. Broad common ICT year then specialisation from Year 2: Applied Data Science & AI, Software Engineering, Business IT & Management, Cloud & Security Engineering (8 modules of 30 study points each). Combines technical competence with ethical action, personal leadership, collaboration, communication. Markets "no stress of an exam week" — half a year to demonstrate competences (flexible, learning-outcomes-oriented modular assessment). Outcomes: 82% work in-field, 88% at degree level, €3,200 start salary, 70% say good labour-market basis, 86% would choose again.

**University of Twente – Technical Computer Science:** SE strong; DS strong; ML strong academically. 3-year English-taught WO bachelor; VWO-equivalent + Mathematics B + English + matching procedure; not numerus fixus. 12 themed modules across three years covering software, networks, systems, hardware, security, HCI, data, AI; Year 2/3 include game-design project and business-world project; 10-week/15-EC internship minor; double-degree option with Applied Mathematics. Student satisfaction ~3.9/5, 69% progression to Year 2. Most common next step: master's (56% Computer Science, 16% CS & Engineering, 5% Data Science and AI).

Reserve comparators: **Fontys ICT** — strongest reserve HBO benchmark for breadth/employer ecosystem (AI, Business Intelligence & Data, Software Engineering, Embedded Software Engineering, Robotics), 120 partner companies in Brainport/Midpoint Brabant. **UvA Artificial Intelligence** — strongest reserve public-university benchmark for AI/ML depth and curricular transparency (Machine Learning, ML Programming & MLOps, Neural Networks, Computer Vision modules; 30 EC electives + 18 EC graduation project; optional 8-week internship).

## Analytical Implications and Recommendations

| Dimension | What the Benchmark Shows | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Track architecture | No main HBO comparator has a clean SE/DS/ML three-track structure | Treat the separate ML track as a core differentiator; define it as engineering pathway (MLOps, deployment, evaluation, monitoring, responsible AI), not just predictive analytics |
| Shared common core | Strongest comparators preserve a broad first-stage foundation (Hanze Y1, Windesheim Y1, Saxion's early technical base, UT's broad foundation) | Design a visible common core (≥ Year 1, likely part of Year 2) in programming, databases, software design, statistics, algorithms, cloud/infrastructure, ethics, professional skills; then branch into SE/DS/ML |
| Distinguishing DS from ML | Windesheim's DS&AI + Google's analytics cert show business-facing analytics; UT/UvA/DeepLearning.AI/Le Wagon show model-centric side | Define DS as analytics, experimentation, BI, visualisation, stakeholder translation, applied statistics; define ML as ML engineering, feature/data pipelines, deep learning, model evaluation, LLMOps/MLOps, deployment |
| Industry engagement | Saxion/Hanze foreground real clients early; Fontys foregrounds ecosystem access; UT includes business projects + internship minor | Build an explicit work-integrated learning arc: Y1 studio/client challenge, Y2 track lab, Y3 minor/internship/industry project, Y4 company-linked graduation; publish it clearly |
| Assessment | Hanze publicises mixed methods; Windesheim differentiates via semester-long evidence windows; MOOCs/bootcamps foreground graded assignments/capstones/portfolios | Use a deliberate mix: project products, code reviews, presentations, reports, reflective evidence, oral defence, selective exams; avoid exam-dominant identity especially in DS/ML |
| Programme transparency | UvA and Windesheim comparatively transparent on module architecture; Fontys/parts of Saxion/Hanze stronger on marketing than later-year granularity | Publish a single public benchmarking-grade curriculum map: year-by-year modules, credits, learning outcomes, assessment forms, industry component, track transitions |
| Modular extension strategy | Hanze/Windesheim already translate bachelor content into stackable modules; bootcamps/MOOCs occupy the short-cycle upskilling market | Build microcredentials/short modules directly from SE/DS/ML tracks to protect degree relevance and open feeder routes |
| Language and market positioning | Split between Dutch-employer-facing (Hanze, Windesheim) and English/internationally positioned (Saxion, Fontys, UT) | Decide explicitly whether bilingual/international or primarily Dutch labour-market embedded; reflect this in positioning, curriculum, industry network design |

**Two strategic judgments:** the programme's likely strength is structural clarity if SE/DS/ML become genuinely differentiated professional pathways; the likely weakness if left unmanaged is track fragmentation, especially between DS and ML — solved by articulating the shared spine clearly and distinguishing tracks by role, toolchain, and deliverable, not merging them.
