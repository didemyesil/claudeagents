Principles of International Accreditation and Self-Assessment Report Structure 02:45 - 11:33

Saule shares her screen and presents the goals and principles of international program accreditation. Accreditation evaluates and recognizes the quality of organizations and programs against ESG-based standards and Bologna process principles, within the national context, so the on-site panel includes a representative of national organizations to explain local legislation. The agency uses a cluster approach with no more than five programs per cluster and no more than twenty programs per visit, assessed by an external expert panel. Core principles include voluntary participation, independence, objectivity, professionalism, transparency, credible information, and collective decisions made by panel vote with public outcomes. She then explains the self-assessment report: it must be structured, readable, analytical, critical, conclusive and evidence-based. Its content comprises an introduction (background and history, legal basis, governance structure, partnerships, international engagement, student numbers by year), a main section covering the ten ESG standards, a conclusion with a self-assessment committee table, and annexes with supporting documents including at least two pages describing program achievements. The introduction may cover the whole university or the faculty hosting the programs. She asks participants to review the ten standards for familiarity.

Standard 1: Quality Assurance Policy 11:35 - 29:00

Saule asks whether the standards are familiar and receives confirmation, then proposes a working mode: she explains each standard's requirements and main message, then discusses how it is realized at the university. For Standard 1, the policy must be public, support strategic management, build a quality culture, clarify responsibilities, protect academic integrity, involve external stakeholders such as industry, social and international partners, parents and the job market, and be monitored and revised. Alper Utku explains the institution has a QA policy proven through eleven years of NVAO accreditation in the Netherlands, two QA advisors (including Juliette for technology programs), and a change process in which the curriculum team proposes changes, QA advisors review them, and the independent examination board gives final approval. The policy is designed long-term but adapted flexibly. Satisfaction is measured through annual reviews, external and internal student feedback, and monthly town halls. Saule advises that the self-assessment report cite concrete surveys, named courses, bodies and annexed samples such as questionnaires and meeting minutes. Alper notes the new programs have no track record, and Saule says the report should show the planned system for data-driven assessment and updating.
