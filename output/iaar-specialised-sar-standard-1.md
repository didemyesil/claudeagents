## 2. Guidelines requirement: coherent QA system, cycle for continuous improvement, accountability

Team Academy's QA system is organised into an **institute-level** layer
(Institute Board, management teams, periodic performance reviews, monthly
Executive Board–programme team check-ins) and a **programme-level** layer
(QA Team, composed of programme managers and led by a QA Lead, reporting to
the Executive Board), operating through a **Plan–Do–Check–Act (PDCA)**
cycle applied at four levels of granularity — activity, module,
certificate, programme (QA Policy §1.1.4–1.1.6). This structure is
identical across the cluster; the concrete instances of "activity," "module"
and "certificate" differ by programme (e.g. the Tech MBA's four
certifications vs. the Master's three, vs. the Bachelor's track-based
module sequences), and those differences are documented in each
programme's own §1.1.6 elaboration cited above.

**Pedagogical assessment:** the four-level PDCA structure gives a coherent,
traceable line from individual learning activities up to programme-level
outcomes (competency development, final-project quality, employability),
consistent with what Standard 9 (on-going monitoring) will later probe in
more depth. **Technical assessment:** the QA Team's reporting line to the
Executive Board, and the Examination Board's parallel statutory oversight of
assessment quality, gives the system institutional teeth rather than being
purely aspirational — though, as noted throughout this section, none of
this has yet been exercised on a running cohort.

is stated plainly rather than papered over.

## 5. Guidelines requirement: how the policy is implemented, monitored and revised (institution's own decision) — Sample Questions 1, 5, 6

The QA Policy names the QA Team as holding main responsibility for
evaluating the QA system, with Executive Board approval of resulting
changes (§1.1.5). At Stage 2, this section was extended, at Didem's
instruction, with an explicit statement of **how the policy document
itself** (not just the QA system it describes) will be reviewed: the QA
Team reviews it annually, ahead of each academic year, incorporating
regulatory changes and Field Representative/Educational Council input,
with Executive Board sign-off on revisions.

**Sample Question 6 (stakeholder satisfaction with the QA policy):** no
satisfaction results exist yet — correctly, since no programme has run
under this policy. The QA Policy now states plainly that satisfaction with
the policy document itself will be assessed as part of the QA Team's annual
review once programmes are operational, drawing on the same stakeholder
groups (faculty, programme management, Educational Council, Field
Representatives) already named elsewhere in the document — distinct from
the programme-level learner-satisfaction data already covered under ESG 1.7
(Information management).

**Pedagogical/technical assessment:** this is a genuinely designed
mechanism, not an invented history; a panel evaluating an ex-ante
application should read this as adequate evidence of intent, while noting
(as this report does) that it is unexercised.
