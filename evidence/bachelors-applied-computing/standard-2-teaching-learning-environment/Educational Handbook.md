## **1. Developing Talent for the AI Era**

### **1.1 About Team Academy**

Welcome to Team Academy. As a student, you are now part of a vibrant, international learning community that is redefining what it means to learn, work, and grow in a rapidly changing digital world.

At Team Academy, we believe that education should be closely connected to the real world, so that you grow not only in knowledge, but also in action, reflection, and leadership. Our mission is to develop professionals who can contribute to the future through responsible innovation, collaboration, and lifelong learning.

We offer applied, competency-based, and flexible education that puts your development as a whole person at the centre. You learn in a dynamic environment that combines academic challenge with professional application, supported by faculty, peers, and connections to practice.

The bachelor programme is designed for learners who want to develop technical expertise, grow as professionals, and make a positive impact in organisations and society. It supports students in building a strong foundation for future roles in a broad and evolving computing field. This fits the HBO orientation of the programme and the broad applied focus of the field.

At Team Academy, you are an active learner. You take initiative, work with others, solve problems, and reflect on your practice. Our approach encourages you to become a curious, ethical, and adaptive professional who is ready to contribute in an ever-changing world.

### **1.2 Our Educational Philosophy**

At Team Academy, learning is not just about passing exams or collecting credits. It is about growing into the kind of professional who can act in complex, changing environments. We believe in education that is practical, flexible, and rooted in real-world relevance.

**Accountability****  
** We take responsibility for our actions and decisions, owning both successes and failures. We hold ourselves and each other to high standards and foster a culture of trust and reliability.

**Courage****  
** We show courage by making difficult decisions in uncertain situations. We support others in taking thoughtful risks and standing up for what they believe is right.

**Care****  
** We value the well-being and growth of others. We listen actively, offer support, and create an environment where people feel respected and understood.

**Collaboration****  
** We work together, value different perspectives, and promote open communication. We believe shared purpose and shared effort lead to stronger results.

**Participation****  
** We involve others in decision-making and create an inclusive environment where everyone feels able to contribute. We respect diverse input and encourage shared ownership.

Our model is built on the idea of full-stack education. This means we combine technical development, leadership development, and reflective practice in one learning journey. You build a strong foundation in applied computing while also learning how to develop yourself and work with others. Through the Action Inquiry line, you learn to improve your practice through simple cycles of **plan, act, observe, and reflect**. This starts with **self-inquiry**, where you reflect on your own learning and behaviour. It then develops into **one-to-one inquiry**, where you practise skills such as listening, asking questions, and using feedback in dialogue with others. Later, it expands into **team inquiry**, where you learn to reflect and improve together in project teams and agile ways of working. In this way, you develop the habits and skills needed to learn from action, collaborate effectively, and grow as a professional.

We follow a competency-based approach, which means your learning is measured by how well you can apply your knowledge in action, not just how well you can explain it on paper. Throughout your journey, you collect evidence of your growth, engage in reflection, and receive feedback that helps you move forward.

This programme is designed in close connection with the professional field, which means you are not learning in a vacuum. What you do here is aligned with the knowledge, skills, and attitudes that employers increasingly expect from graduates in computing and digital practice.

You will not spend your time in passive lectures only. Instead, learning is hands-on and experience-based. You work on projects, solve authentic problems, and share your process and insights with peers and facilitators. You explore digital tools, collaborate across cultures, and challenge yourself in a supportive but rigorous environment.

At Team Academy, we trust you to take the lead in your own learning, and we are here to support you every step of the way.

### **1.4 Programme Outcomes**

The programme outcomes of the Bachelor of Science in Applied Computing are based on the HBO-i framework and aligned with NLQF level 6. They reflect the HBO orientation of the programme and provide a shared outcome structure across the three tracks.

**Analyse****  
** Graduates are able to inquire into and analyse complex professional challenges, identify relevant requirements, information needs and constraints, and frame justified directions for further inquiry and solution development.

**Advise****  
**Graduates are able to provide substantiated advice to stakeholders by interpreting findings, weighing alternatives and implications, and taking account of professional, organisational, social and ethical considerations.

**Design****  
**Graduates are able to design fit-for-purpose digital solutions by selecting and structuring appropriate architectures, methods and models in response to professional requirements and context.

**Build****  
**Graduates are able to build and validate professional digital products by implementing, testing and improving solutions with appropriate technical rigour, quality awareness and responsiveness to requirements.

**Manage and Control****  
** Graduates are able to manage and control their professional work by organising processes, monitoring quality and progress, making responsible decisions in changing contexts, and reflecting on their development and impact.

**Communicate and Collaborate****  
** Graduates are able to communicate and collaborate effectively with relevant stakeholders by tailoring their communication to purpose and audience, contributing constructively to shared goals, and taking responsibility for their role in professional interactions and outcomes.

These programme outcomes are further developed in the three tracks through track-specific competencies and modules, while keeping a shared bachelor-level profile across the programme.

### **1.5 The T-Shaped Professional**

At Team Academy, we see the graduates of the bachelor programme as a **T-shaped professional**. This means that graduates combine depth in one professional track with the breadth needed to work across disciplines, communicate effectively, and continue developing in a changing field. This fits the nature of applied computing and the HBO orientation of the programme.

The **vertical bar** of the T represents technical depth in one of the programme tracks. Students develop focused expertise in their chosen area and learn to analyse, design, build, and improve professional computing solutions in that field.

The **horizontal bar** of the T represents the broader capabilities that all students develop across the programme. These include communication, collaboration, reflection, inquiry, professional responsibility, and the ability to work with stakeholders and teams in authentic contexts. They also include the ability to connect technical work to user needs, organisational context, ethics, and quality.

This combination of depth and breadth defines the graduate profile of the Bachelor of Science in Applied Computing: a professional with clear technical capability and the broader applied skills needed to contribute, adapt, and grow in the AI era.

## **2. Learning Lines**

### **2.1 Overview of Learning Lines**

Your programme is built around two interconnected learning lines: the **Professional Practice Line** and the **Research Practice Line**. Together, these lines support you in developing as an applied computing professional who can contribute to practice, learn from experience, and grow in increasingly complex professional contexts.

The **Professional Practice Line** helps you develop the technical knowledge, skills, and behaviours you need to work effectively in the field of applied computing. Through this line, you learn to analyse professional challenges, design and build technical solutions, communicate with stakeholders, and manage your work in professional contexts. It reflects the practical orientation of the programme by connecting your learning directly to practice and by preparing you for professional roles in a broad and evolving computing domain. Across the three tracks, this line gives you both a shared applied computing foundation and the opportunity to develop depth in your chosen area.

The **Research Practice Line** helps you learn systematically from action in project-based and professional contexts. This line is built around **action inquiry** as an educational approach and supports you in improving your practice through cycles of **plan, act, observe, and reflect**. It begins with **self-inquiry**, in which you reflect on your own actions, habits, choices, and learning process. It then develops into **one-to-one inquiry**, where you practise dialogue, listening, questioning, and feedback with peers and others. Later, it expands into **team inquiry**, where you learn to reflect and improve together within project teams and agile ways of working. In the later stages of the programme, this line becomes more explicitly connected to your graduation project design, professional decision-making, and applied research in increasingly complex situations.

Within this Research Practice Line, **vertical leadership development** becomes an important dimension. This means that you develop in how you think, reflect and take responsibility for your actions. Through this developmental pathway, you grow in self-awareness, ownership, accountability, perspective-taking, and professional judgement.

Together, the two learning lines help you develop both as a competent practitioner and as a reflective professional. The Professional Practice Line strengthens your ability to contribute through applied computing knowledge and skills, while the Research Practice Line strengthens your ability to learn from action, work with others, and continue developing throughout your studies and future career.

### **3.4 Final Project: Integrating Your Learning in Practice**

The Final Project is the concluding and most integrative part of your bachelor programme. It is where your learning across the **Professional Practice Line** and the **Research Practice Line** comes together in a real-world project.

This is your opportunity to demonstrate both your technical ability and your leadership growth.

**Purpose of the Final Project****  
** The Final Project is a practice-based and inquiry-driven project that brings together your learning from applied computing and action inquiry. Grounded in the programme’s research practice approach, its purpose is to help you investigate the question:

**“How can I address a real professional problem in my track through the design and development of an appropriate solution?”**

It enables you to:

  - apply your competencies in a real-world context
  - analyse a professional problem and develop an appropriate solution
  - work through structured cycles of action, feedback, and reflection
  - demonstrate your achievement of the programme outcomes at bachelor level
  - create meaningful contributions in an organisational or professional context

Refer to the **Final Project Learner Guidelines** for more information about the project process, methodology, and evaluation criteria.

## **4. How You Learn: The Team Academy Model**

### **4.1 Vision on Learning and Learning Accelerators**

At Team Academy, learning is not just about acquiring knowledge; it is also about developing the ability to act, reflect, collaborate, and grow in professional practice. Our learning philosophy and our leadership philosophy are closely connected. You do not only learn what to do, but also how to take responsibility, work with others, and continue developing in changing contexts.

“We believe in the power of participation, the willingness to change, and the ability to learn from each other.”  
 — *Our Leadership Manifesto*

Our vision on learning is built around five pillars that reflect the principles in our Leadership Manifesto:

**Competency-Based Learning****  
** We do not just teach concepts; we ask you to apply them. Every assignment, project, and challenge is an opportunity to demonstrate your development in real-world and practice-based contexts. Your progress is measured not only by what you know, but by how effectively you can apply, justify, and improve your work.

**Self-Directed****  
**You are trusted and expected to take an active role in your own learning. With flexible, self-paced content and a rhythm of individual and group work, you are encouraged to take responsibility for your progress and development.

**Collaborative****  
**Learning happens in the community. You are part of a cohort in workshops, squad meetings, standups or team coaching sessions, and shared reflection spaces where peer learning is central. Working with others is an essential part of your learning journey and professional development.

**Experiential and Reflective****  
**We believe experience is one of the most powerful ways to learn, especially when it is supported by structured reflection. You move through cycles of action, inquiry, and feedback, asking questions such as: What am I learning? How am I developing? What does this mean for how I work with others and contribute in practice?

**Ethical and Impact-Oriented****  
** Your learning is grounded in real professional challenges. You are expected to act with care, responsibility, and awareness of the wider impact of your work in professional and social contexts.

In sum, at Team Academy, how you learn is closely connected to how you develop as a professional. Your learning experience is structured to help you build the qualities needed for bachelor-level professional practice.

### **Learning Accelerators**

To achieve our educational vision, your learning is supported by a blend of high-impact methods built into the structure of the programme. These approaches ensure that your development is not just theoretical, but applied and aligned with professional practice.

**Self-Paced Digital Learning****  
** You work with asynchronous digital content through the digital learning environment. This can include readings, videos, and practical exercises. It gives you flexibility to learn in your own rhythm while helping you build foundational knowledge and skills between workshops and assignments.

**Workshops****  
** Our workshops are interactive and practice-oriented. Facilitators guide you through practical learning, real-time challenges, and collaborative problem-solving sessions. These workshops are designed to connect theory with action and deepen understanding through application.

**Squad Meetings****  
** Squads are small teams in which you collaborate on projects in a way that reflects professional practice. In your squad, you work together on shared tasks, integrate different competencies, and learn how to contribute effectively to team-based outcomes.

**Stand-up Meetings****  
** Stand-ups structure your learning in iterative cycles with continuous feedback and improvement. They help you make progress visible, surface blockers, share observations, and stay aligned with your team. In this way, they support both project delivery and team inquiry.

**Team Coaching****  
**Team coaching is a dialogical and experiential process that supports collective learning and development. These sessions connect learning with practice through reflection, dialogue, and experimentation. The coach facilitates inquiry and ownership rather than simply providing instruction, helping you and your team develop autonomy, adaptability, and collaboration.

**Projects****  
**Throughout the programme, you complete track projects connected to your modules and work on broader projects that require integration of your learning. Projects are designed to reflect authentic professional challenges and help you build a portfolio that shows your development across the programme.

**VIVAs**

They are **high-stake oral assessment moments** that take place at the end of a module. In a VIVA, you demonstrate your learning by presenting and discussing your work, your choices, and your development in relation to the intended learning outcomes. VIVAs help assess whether you have achieved the required level and can justify your work in a clear and professional way. 

**Hackathons****  
**Hackathons are intensive and collaborative learning experiences in which you work on real or realistic challenges within a limited timeframe. They help you apply your knowledge in action, strengthen teamwork, and experience a professional way of working under pressure.

**Town Halls****  
** Town Halls are community events hosted by programme management. These sessions create space to celebrate achievements, share ideas and feedback, hear updates, and strengthen the sense of community within the programme.

### **4.2 Assessment of Your Learning**

At Team Academy, assessment is not something that happens only at the end; it is part of your learning from the very beginning. Every assignment, project, and reflection you complete is designed to help you demonstrate growth, receive feedback, and take your learning further.

Assessment is project and competency-based. This means your work is evaluated on how well you can apply your learning in real-world and practice-based situations. Each project is connected to learning outcomes from the programme’s learning lines, whether that is analysing a professional problem, developing a digital solution, contributing to a project, or reflecting on your growth as a professional.

Assessment in the programme follows the principles of **programmatic assessment**. This means that your progress is judged on the basis of a growing body of evidence collected over time. Rather than relying on one single assessment moment, the programme looks at how different forms of evidence together show your development and achievement of the intended learning outcomes. Projects play an important role in this, because they allow you to integrate technical, professional, and reflective learning in authentic contexts.

### **What Gets Assessed**

You are assessed through different types of assignments and evidence to demonstrate your development in relation to the intended learning outcomes throughout the programme. Some of this evidence is **low-stake**, some is **medium-stake**, and some is **high-stake**. Together, they help make your development visible over time.

**Low-stake** means the assessment is mainly developmental and helps you prepare for more integrated assessment moments. Examples include weekly assignments, workshop exercises, reflections, and self- or peer-feedback. These support learning and development.

**Medium-stake** means the assessment is more comprehensive and assessed formally. Examples include end-of-module projects and integrated group projects. These provide stronger evidence of your progress toward the intended learning outcomes.

**High-stake** means an important progression or completion moment in which your broader body of evidence is reviewed. This includes major portfolio-based decisions and the Final Project. These moments help determine whether you have demonstrated the required level at that stage of the programme.

See the table below for examples of learning evidence at each level:

|  |  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| \*\*Stake Level\*\* | \*\*Example Evidence\*\* | \*\*Evidence Captured\*\* | \*\*When It’s Established\*\* |
| Low | Workshop exercises, standup or coaching reflections | Short written reflections; exercise outputs; peer/self-feedback; coding notebooks | During or after workshops/meeting |
| Medium | Track projects | Project code, reports, presentations, demos, peer review forms | During the modules |
| High | Portfolio-based progression moments (VIVAs); Final Project | Portfolio review; final project work and related evidence | At major progression or completion moments |

### **Final Evaluation and Graduation**

To graduate, you must successfully complete the required parts of the programme and pass your Final Project. By the end of the programme, you must be able to demonstrate that you meet the programme outcomes at bachelor level. After successful completion, you receive a bachelor diploma, a diploma supplement, and a transcript of records.

## **5. Staying on Course: Learner Responsibilities, Policies and Procedures**

  

### **5.1 Learner Responsibilities**

At Team Academy, you are a central and active participant in your own learning journey. As a member of our community, your engagement, presence, and commitment influence not only your own progress, but also the collective learning experience of your peers. In line with our principles of community learning, accountability, and professional growth, we expect you to take responsibility for your participation and development.

#### **Active Participation in Learning Activities**

Our programme is built around workshops, squad meetings, stand-up meetings, team coaching, project work, and other learning activities. These are not optional add-ons; they are core parts of your learning process. Each activity is intentionally designed to help you develop the intended learning outcomes of the programme and to generate learning evidence for your portfolio.

To support an open, collaborative, and learner-centred learning environment, you are expected to:

  - join all scheduled sessions punctually and be fully present
  - if online, keep your camera on unless agreed otherwise in advance
  - contribute actively to discussions, group work, stand-ups, squad activities, and shared reflections
  - bring questions, ideas, and reflections that enrich the collective learning experience

#### **Learning Evidence and Portfolio Responsibility**

Every learning activity you participate in gives you an opportunity to generate evidence that contributes to demonstrating your development in relation to the programme outcomes. Together, these form your portfolio throughout the programme.

Because of this structure, your active participation is essential. If you are absent, you miss the opportunity to engage with the learning outcomes in real time and to generate evidence connected to your development.

#### **In Case of Absence**

We understand that unforeseen circumstances may arise. If you cannot attend a session, you are expected to:

  - notify the Programme Manager as early as possible, and where possible before the session
  - review the topic, activities, and intended learning outcomes of the session
  - submit alternative learning evidence that demonstrates your engagement with the missed outcomes, such as a written reflection, a recorded explanation, a practical exercise, or another agreed evidence.

Absence without communication, or without replacement evidence where required, may affect your ability to demonstrate sufficient progress and achievement in your portfolio.

#### **Owning Your Learning Journey**

Being part of Team Academy means being an active and responsible learner. By showing up, contributing, and taking responsibility for your learning, you uphold the values of our community and support your own success. Your growth is a collaborative process, and it develops best when each person participates with accountability.

As a learner, your feedback directly influences how the programme is shaped and improved. You are an important participant in maintaining the quality of your education.

**Feedback Surveys****  
**Regularly during the programme, you are invited to reflect on your learning experience. These structured surveys help programme management and faculty understand what worked well, what can be improved, and how the programme can be further developed for future cohorts.

**VIVA Sessions****  
** At the end of each module, you take part in an oral VIVA. In addition to being a high-stake oral assessment moment, the VIVA also helps provide insight into how the module is supporting your learning and development in relation to the intended outcomes.

**Town Halls****  
** Hosted regularly by the Programme Management team, these open forums give you space to share feedback, hear updates, and engage in transparent dialogue with faculty, programme management, and fellow learners. Issues raised here can directly inform programme improvements.

**Programme-Wide Quality Reviews****  
** Your input, together with information about learning progress, achievement, and workload, is reviewed at programme level to evaluate:

  - how well learners are achieving the intended outcomes
  - whether the programme content remains aligned with the needs of the professional field
  - the quality and impact of support systems such as coaching and supervision

### **7.2 Examination Board**

The Examination Board at Team Academy plays an important role in safeguarding the academic standards and integrity of your programme. It operates independently from faculty roles and ensures that assessment, decisions, and procedures are fair, transparent, and aligned with the formal regulations.

#### **When You Interact With the Examination Board**

You normally interact with the Examination Board in the following situations:

**1. Appeals and Complaints****  
** You may contact the Examination Board if you wish to appeal or file a complaint about:

  - a progression decision
  - an intake decision
  - a procedural error in examination or assessment

Appeals must be submitted in writing with supporting evidence. The Examination Board reviews appeals and normally issues a decision within four weeks. If you disagree with the decision, you may appeal through the relevant formal procedure.

**2. Exceptional Circumstances****  
** If personal circumstances have seriously affected your ability to study or complete assessments, you can apply for exceptional circumstances. These may relate to:

  - illness or medical reasons
  - serious personal or family situations
  - circumstances outside your control

Exceptional circumstances may support requests for:

  - retaking a module
  - reconsideration of a progression decision
  - other appropriate measures under the applicable regulations

Initial consultation takes place with the Programme Manager. If needed, the matter may then be brought to the Examination Board.

**3. Extension Requests****  
** If you are unable to meet an assessment deadline, you may request an extension in advance by submitting the designated extension form to programme management. Extension requests for regular assignments must be submitted at least four days before the deadline shown in Canvas. Supporting evidence may be required depending on the nature of the request. Decision-making authority for low- and medium-stake assessments lies with the Programme Manager; for high-stake assessments, the decision authority lies with the Examination Board. Decisions on extension requests must normally be communicated within four days of the request.

**Three-Step Extension Process**

**First Request: Automatically Granted****  
** The first extension request for an assessment is approved automatically, provided it is submitted on time and through the designated process.

**Second Request: Requires Reflective Justification****  
** A second request requires you to provide a written reflection explaining why an additional extension is necessary and how you will try to prevent further delay.

**Third Request: Referral to Internal Confidential Advisor****  
** A third request leads to referral to an internal confidential advisor, who can help you address underlying challenges affecting your study progress. The extension may be granted only after this consultation.

**4. Exemptions and Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL)****  
** If you believe you have already achieved the learning outcomes of a module through previous study or professional experience, you may request an exemption or recognition of prior learning. The Examination Board evaluates and decides on these requests.

**5. Academic Integrity Cases****  
** The Examination Board handles cases involving:

  - plagiarism
  - unauthorised collaboration
  - fraud
  - other breaches of academic integrity

Consequences depend on the seriousness of the case and may include invalidation of an assessment or further measures under the applicable regulations.

**6. Final Project Completion****  
** Before a diploma is issued, the Examination Board verifies that all academic and administrative requirements for graduation have been met. Diplomas are awarded only after the Board has confirmed that all requirements are fulfilled.

#### **How to Contact the Examination Board**

Requests, appeals, and applications should be submitted:

  - in writing by e-mail
  - with supporting documents
  - via the Programme Manager, who forwards them to the Chair of the Examination Board

You will receive confirmation of receipt and an indication of the decision timeline. All communication with the Examination Board should use the designated channels, forms, and procedures described in the Teaching and Examination Regulations.

## **Appendix 3: Failure and Second Chance Opportunity Procedure**

Within a programmatic assessment model, progression is based on the continuous collection of evidence demonstrating competency development. Instead of defining failure at the level of individual assessments, the programme evaluates whether sufficient evidence has been provided to support a judgement at the certification decision point.

A certification is considered **not achieved** when, at the certification decision point:  
 • the total body of evidence across all learning outcomes and evidence points contributing to that certification does not demonstrate the required level of mastery  
 • no or incomplete evidence has been submitted within the defined timeframe and no allowance has been approved under exceptional circumstances  
 • academic conduct regulations have been breached, resulting in evidence being invalidated for decision-making

The panel evaluates evidence holistically. A certification is not achieved when the collected evidence does not sufficiently demonstrate the required competency level.

### **Progression to the next certification despite not yet achieving the previous one**

When a student has not yet demonstrated sufficient evidence at the certification decision point, they may still proceed to the next certification, provided that:  
 • the next certification includes learning outcomes, projects or applied tasks that offer meaningful opportunities to further develop or demonstrate the outcomes that were not yet achieved  
 • continuation is pedagogically appropriate and does not hinder the student’s overall learning trajectory  
 • faculty identify any specific competencies that require additional focus during the following certification  
 • the decision is explicitly documented and does not imply that the earlier certification has been achieved  
 • progression to a subsequent certification does not remove the requirement that all intended learning outcomes must be demonstrated at the required bachelor level before the final award can be granted

This continuation does not replace the requirement to ultimately achieve the previous certification. Instead, it allows the student to benefit from naturally occurring evidence opportunities in later certification projects and applied activities.

A certification remains not yet achieved until sufficient evidence is demonstrated, and the final certification decision can be made independently of the student’s participation in subsequent certifications.

### **Retaking a certification in a subsequent cohort**

Students who, despite given opportunities, still have not demonstrated sufficient evidence by the end of the next certification may retake the certification in a subsequent cohort.

Retaking a certification means:  
 • continuing evidence development with the next cohort  
 • following the learning activities, assessment opportunities and competency expectations of that cohort  
 • generating new or updated evidence where requirements have changed or where existing evidence is insufficient

### **Reflective requirements and approval process**

Students preparing to retake a certification must submit:  
 • a reflective account explaining prior challenges and evidence gaps  
 • a rigorous action plan describing how they will approach evidence development differently

The request is reviewed through the formal assessment governance process, with roles clearly separated between student support, academic advice, and decision-making.  
 The Examination Board safeguards that decisions are taken consistently and in line with the Teaching and Examination Regulations.

### **Evidence validity**

Previously collected evidence remains valid unless:  
 • academic misconduct has taken place  
 • competency standards have changed  
 • the evidence has become outdated or insufficient for decision-making

Students may be required to produce additional or updated evidence as needed.

### **Finality of certification**

Once a certification has been achieved, students are not permitted to retake it or submit further evidence for it.

### **Exceptional Circumstances**

Exceptional circumstances are personal circumstances that:  
 • are beyond the student’s control  
 • could not reasonably have been prevented or accommodated  
 • have had a significant and demonstrable negative effect on the student’s ability to generate evidence of learning  
 • occurred during the period relevant to the affected certification or evidence-development period

All elements must be met for an exceptional circumstances application to be considered.

### **Temporary Withdrawal**

• A student wishing to withdraw temporarily must obtain written approval for a specific period (maximum five years) and may need to satisfy conditions before re-enrolling.  
 • Students who withdraw without permission or who fail to re-enrol by the agreed date may be considered permanently withdrawn.  
 • Students who fail to maintain enrolment and do not respond to communication from their supervisor or the institute for the duration of one certification period will be withdrawn.  
 • Students with approved temporary withdrawal may rejoin a later cohort and continue developing evidence toward certification.

### **Application Procedure for Exceptional Circumstances**

#### **Initial contact**

Students must contact their programme manager within two weeks of being informed that a certification was not yet achieved. The programme manager conducts an advisory consultation to review circumstances, potential evidence, and the application process. A submission deadline is agreed; missing it means forfeiting the opportunity to apply.

#### **Submission of the application**

Students complete the exceptional circumstances form by the agreed deadline. The form includes the option to request a certification retake or temporary withdrawal.

#### **Review and decision**

Students submit the application and supporting documentation to the programme manager of the bachelor’s programme. The programme manager coordinates the process but does not take the formal academic decision independently.  
 The formal decision is taken within the designated governance structure and, where applicable, under the authority or oversight of the Examination Board.**  
** Students receive a decision within three weeks. The decision is final within the institutional framework. Students may pursue the external NRTO dispute and appeals procedure if applicable.

## **Appendix 4: Academic Appeals Procedure**

This guide is intended to help explain the Academic Appeals process. If you are considering making an Academic Appeal, perhaps because you do not agree with your enrolment status or another decision of the programme management or faculty, you are encouraged to read this guide with care.

This guidance document has been prepared by the institution, and please remember that every Academic Appeal is considered individually, and general statements noted below may not apply in all circumstances. Academic Appeals may take several weeks to process.

### **Academic Appeals**

An academic appeal is a procedure which allows you in certain circumstances to ask for a review of a decision relating to your academic progress or award. Circumstances in which you may submit an appeal include the following:  
 • a decision on progress has been taken which prevents you from continuing your studies, usually a decision to terminate your registration  
 • a decision has been taken which requires you to interrupt your studies  
 • a decision has been taken not to make an award to you and the consequence is that your studies will be frozen or terminated  
 • a decision to award a lower qualification to you than that for which you are registered, for example if you are registered for the bachelor’s degree and are recommended for an interim certificate or other lower exit award, where applicable

In an Academic Appeal, any claims that you make must, generally, be supported by compelling evidence. The type of evidence that is acceptable varies significantly, but generally includes medical certificates, statements by academic staff about particular events, copies of specific regulations, detailed personal statements, confirmation from authorities that you were involved in a particular incident. Contemporaneous, independent and medical evidence is very important and is often given greater weight by an Appeal Panel. It is also important that the evidence indicates the significance of the impact on you and relates to specific time periods.

It is your responsibility to provide the evidence for the Appeal Panel to consider; the institute will not approach doctors or others on your behalf.

### **Grounds for Appeal**

You may not appeal if your results are not as good as you had hoped or worse than you believe you deserve. Appeals which simply challenge the academic judgement of internal or external evaluators are not permitted. The grounds for appeal are summarised below:  
 • there were circumstances materially affecting your performance, for which supporting evidence exists, which were not known to the Academic Panel or other academic body at the time of the decision and which were not reasonably practicable to make known beforehand  
 • there appears to you to be evidence that there has been a procedural irregularity in the conduct of assessment or assessment procedures of such a nature as to create a reasonable possibility that the result may have been different if it had not occurred

A ‘procedural irregularity’ means that the examining and assessment process was not conducted according to the institute’s guidelines and procedures. Examples might include errors in an assessment grading or with the administration of an examination, or a failure to calculate the marks correctly or to consider any mitigating circumstances submitted by you before the due date.

• there appears to you to be evidence of prejudice or bias on the part of the evaluators or in the assessment process

It is important to note that the regulations on mitigating circumstances require programme learners to notify their faculty of any matters which may be relevant to their academic performance, for example, personal or medical circumstances, at the time they occur and to supply supporting documentary evidence, such as medical certificates. Unless there were very good reasons why you were unable to notify your faculty members or supply evidence at the time it is likely that an appeal will be disallowed.

You should note that the institute may operate systems of double scoring or moderation to ensure that there are adequate checks on the accuracy and appropriateness of marking and to help prevent prejudice and bias. Additionally, scores are awarded to a learner’s work based on the academic judgement of the evaluators in conjunction with an agreed assessment criterion in the form of rubrics relevant to the assignment in question. It is also important to remember that the institute has a competency-based learning model which encourages the learners to always increase their mastery levels and scores through second submissions per the resubmission procedures stated in the Teaching and Examination Regulations.

### **How to submit an appeal**

If you decide that you have eligible grounds for appeal, you should complete and submit an [appeal form](https://elu2.typeform.com/to/vAkwfr) with supporting documentary evidence. Please ensure that you complete the Academic Appeal form as required rather than submitting a statement or letter of appeal.

Your deadline to submit an appeal will be 10 institute working days from the results release date. Please note that not submitting it by the stated deadline will normally result in the appeal being disallowed.

You must appeal on your own behalf and it is your responsibility to obtain the required evidence. The institute will not contact doctors, your company or others on your behalf. If you require help in compiling your appeal, your community manager is able to provide support and advice but cannot write your appeal for you.

It is important that you submit evidence to support your appeal. Where medical evidence is submitted this must be from a qualified medical practitioner. If you are resident overseas you must ensure that any medical evidence is submitted in English. If it was not written in English, you must supply a certified translation. The institute undertakes checks to ensure that evidence submitted is genuine.

The Examination Board will check whether your appeal contains eligible grounds. Information or a report from your faculty members (facilitators or mentors) may be obtained to assist this decision. If your appeal does not demonstrate eligible grounds you will be notified in writing and either advised that the appeal cannot be accepted or offered the opportunity to supply more information or evidence within a stated timescale.

If you are informed your appeal does not demonstrate eligible grounds, then you will be given the opportunity to request a review of this decision. To do so you will have to show that there was a procedural irregularity in the way your appeal has been considered or it appears that evidence of an eligible ground of appeal has not been fully considered. Your request will be considered by the Appeal Panel who will either decide no valid ground for further review has been established and conclude the appeal process at this point.

Academic appeals are initially handled by the Examination Board of the programme. Programme learners cannot attend Examination Board meetings, however, in the procedure of an appeal, both the involved candidate as well as the educator can be invited to a hearing, should the Examination Board deem this necessary. Cases are considered on the basis of the documentation they have provided.

  

### **Appeal Decision**

You will usually receive formal notification of the outcome of your case within seven working days of the meeting. The outcome letter will explain the reasons for the formal decision. The outcome letter will also highlight the opportunity to request a review of the board’s decision, if you believe that there was a procedural irregularity in the way your appeal has been considered or that there is evidence within the appeal which it appears has not been fully considered. Should you want to appeal a decision taken by the Examination Board, you have the right to appeal decisions of the Examination Board through the independent NRTO disputes and appeals procedure.

Once the institute’s appeal procedures have been concluded you will be issued with a “Completion of Procedures Letter”.
