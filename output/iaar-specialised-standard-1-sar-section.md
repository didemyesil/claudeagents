## Standard 1 — Policy for Quality Assurance

**Standard statement (IAAR, 2018, §III):** *"Institutions should have a
policy for quality assurance that is made public and forms part of their
strategic management. Internal stakeholders should develop and implement
this policy through appropriate structures and processes, while involving
external stakeholders."*

### Cluster-mode framing

This standard's evidence is **overwhelmingly shared** across the three
programmes under this application — the Professional Master's in Applied AI,
the Bachelor's in Applied Computing, and the Tech MBA. Team Academy operates
one institution-wide Policy for Quality Assurance, one governance/
organisational structure, and one External Field Representatives mechanism
for all its programmes; none of the QA-policy content itself varies by
programme. Accordingly, this section is written **once**, covering all three
programmes together, with a single labelled subsection where the evidence
genuinely differs: **stakeholder-involvement evidence**, where the Bachelor's
programme has a materially stronger, dated illustrative record than the
Master's or Tech MBA currently have, even though the underlying mechanism
(the Field Representatives group) is the same shared mechanism across all
three.

### 1. A coherent institutional QA system, operating as a continuous-improvement cycle

Team Academy's Policy for Quality Assurance (`/evidence/_shared/
Institution-wide Quality Assurance Policy (Team Academy).md`) sets out an
ESG-aligned QA system organised at two levels — **institute-level QA**
(Executive Board, Management Teams, external oversight via the Ministry of
Education, Culture and Science and NVAO) and **programme-level QA** (QA Team,
Examination Board, Educational Council, Admissions Teams, Faculty, Learners,
informed by external players including Field Representatives, Knowledge
Partners and global faculty). The system operates as a **Plan–Do–Check–Act
(PDCA) cycle**, applied across four layers of educational design and
delivery: activity, module, certificate/certification, and programme level
(QA Policy, §1.1.5–1.1.6). This structure is identical across all three
cluster programmes; the underlying governance roles are documented in the
Institution Organisational Structure (`/evidence/_shared/Institution
Organisational Structure (Team Academy).md`), with local copies now filed
under each programme's own evidence folder for direct reference: the
Master's copy (Appendix 13), the Bachelor's copy (`standard-5-quality-
assurance/Organisational Structure.md`), and the Tech MBA copy
(`Organisational Structure.md`).

### 2. Quality culture and QA responsibility at all levels

The QA Policy's guiding principles (Adherence to common standards,
Collaboration, Transparency, Innovation, Agility, Reflection — §1.1.3) and
its multi-layered approach (§1.1.6) are designed so that faculty, programme
management, curriculum teams, learners and field experts each carry defined
QA responsibilities at the activity, module, certificate and programme
level. This applies identically to all three programmes; the Tech MBA's
newly adapted copy (`/evidence/tech-mba/Quality Assurance Policy.md`)
elaborates §1.1.6 specifically for the Tech MBA's four-certification, fully
online structure (Transform Your Business; Lead Your Business; Innovate and
Grow Your Business; Leadership and Action Research; Final Project), the same
kind of programme-specific elaboration already present in the Bachelor's
copy for its module level.

### 3. Formal status and public availability

The QA Policy has formal status within Team Academy's governance structure —
it is approved through the Executive Board / QA Team chain described in
§1.1.4–1.1.5 and referenced as binding by the Teaching and Examination
Regulations of all three programmes. **Publication status** (Sample
Questions 2–3): the QA Policy document itself is, at this stage, an
**internal governance document** — available to staff, faculty and Field
Representatives, but not yet published externally, since the cluster's
programmes have not yet launched. Public publication is planned once the
programmes become operational (QA Policy, §ESG 1.8, publication-status note
added in this drafting pass). This is stated plainly rather than claiming a
public presence that does not yet exist, consistent with this being an
ex-ante initial-accreditation application.

### 4. Relationship between research and learning & teaching

The QA Policy document itself is governance/process-focused and does not
directly address the research–teaching linkage. The strongest available
evidence is `Appendix 11 - Faculty Research.md` (Master's programme),
documenting Team Academy faculty's active research and publication record
across applied AI/ML, leadership and organisational-development, finance,
and education-research domains. Read together with the QA Policy's own
description of faculty's dual role — as both curriculum designers and
assessors within the PDCA cycle (§1.1.5–1.1.6) — this shows research
activity feeding directly into curriculum design and assessment practice
rather than sitting apart from it: the same faculty who conduct this
research also sit on the curriculum teams that design modules and on
assessment panels, which is how the "relationship between research and
learning & teaching" (Guidelines) is operationalised at Team Academy. This
faculty-research record is currently filed under the Master's programme
only; it documents individual faculty members' research activity rather
than programme-specific content, so the same evidence and the same
research–teaching linkage argument applies across all three programmes to
the extent these faculty teach across them.

**[EVIDENCE NEEDED — minor, does not block a "satisfactory" position]:** an
equivalent faculty-research inventory has not yet been compiled for
Bachelor's- or Tech-MBA-specific faculty, if any differ from the Master's
faculty pool. Not a blocker for this standard, but worth closing before
Standard 5 (Teaching Staff) is drafted, since that standard will need the
same evidence in more depth.

### 5. National and institutional context

The QA Policy is explicitly framed against the Dutch regulatory context — the
Ministry of Education, Culture and Science (OCW) as regulator and the NVAO as
the national accreditation body — while Team Academy pursues IAAR
specialised accreditation as an additional, international layer (§1.1.4,
§ESG 1.10). Team Academy holds Recognised Legal Entity for Higher Education
(RPHO) status under the Dutch WHW (§ESG 1.4), which applies to all three
programmes.

### 6. Organisation of the QA system, and departmental/staff/student QA responsibilities

Covered under points 1–2 above. The Organisational Structure document (now
locally copied into all three programmes' evidence folders) names the
specific bodies — Executive Board, Examination Board, QA Team, Educational
Council (student representation), Admissions Manager, Programme Manager(s),
Faculty, Head Coach/Team Coaches — through which departments, leadership,
staff and students take on their QA responsibilities, consistent across the
cluster.

### 7. Academic integrity and freedom; vigilance against academic fraud

The Master's and Bachelor's programmes each have a full, near-identically
structured set of regulatory documents carrying this requirement: Teaching
and Examination Regulations, Assessment Policy, and Examination Board
Handbook, each with a dedicated Academic Integrity article defining
plagiarism/misconduct (including undisclosed AI-tool use), a reporting duty,
a hearing process before the Examination Board, and a right of appeal via
the NRTO disputes and appeals procedure.

The **Tech MBA previously had no equivalent** — only a single passing
mention of "academic integrity" in its Supervision Guide, with no formal
procedure behind it. This has now been closed: this drafting pass adapted
the Master's Teaching and Examination Regulations, Assessment Policy, and
Examination Board Handbook into Tech-MBA-named copies
(`/evidence/tech-mba/Teaching and Examination Regulations (TER).md`,
`Policy on Assessment.md`, `Examination Board Handbook.md`), preserving the
shared institution-wide regulatory framework (WHW basis, Examination Board
composition and statutory tasks, academic-integrity procedure, appeals via
NRTO) while adapting the programme-structure articles to the Tech MBA's
actual on-file facts (fully online delivery, ~20 months, 60 ECTS across four
certifications plus a Final Project, Programme Manager role, and the
Internal/External Examiner mechanism already described in the Tech MBA
Supervision Guide for Final Project assessment). All three programmes are
now covered.

### 8. Guarding against intolerance or discrimination

Prior to this drafting pass, Team Academy's non-discrimination commitments
existed only as scattered provisions inside other documents: non-
discriminatory admissions language in the QA Policy (§ESG 1.4), inclusive-
assessment/disability-accommodation language in the Assessment Policy and
Teaching and Examination Regulations ("promoting equal opportunities for
success"), and diversity criteria (status, gender, race, ethnicity, age,
geography) in the Field Representative selection guidelines. Per Didem's
confirmation, these have now been consolidated into a single, shared,
institution-wide **Gender Equality and Equal Opportunities Statement**
(`/evidence/_shared/Gender Equality and Equal Opportunities Statement (Team
Academy).md`), grounded entirely in the commitments already on file, which
directly answers Sample Question 14 ("Does the EO have a gender
equality/equal opportunities concept?") for the first time as a named,
standalone concept rather than only as scattered provisions. This statement
is drafted and routed back to Didem for review before being treated as
adopted institutional policy (see Open Questions below — routine review, not
a gap).

### 9. Involvement of external stakeholders in QA

Team Academy's Field Representatives group is the shared, institution-wide
mechanism for this (`Responsibilities and Guidelines for the Field
Representatives`, adapted copies now filed for all three programmes):
external business/academic professionals advise the curriculum team on
programme-market fit, review programme objectives, and validate final-
project-level outcomes, meeting at design and annual-review stages, with
diversity criteria built into member selection.

**Programme-specific subsection — stakeholder-involvement evidence:**

- **Master's (Professional Master's in Applied AI):** has the standing Field
  Representative guidelines (Appendix 20) describing the mechanism, but no
  consolidated, dated record yet on file showing the feedback loop actually
  operating for this programme.
- **Bachelor's (Applied Computing):** has materially stronger evidence — the
  `Consolidated Field Representative Feedback.md` document records 18
  verbatim field-representative comments on the Programme Brochure and
  Modular Learning Outcomes, each with a decision/action/status ("Accept,"
  "Partially accept," "To validate," etc.), and the dated `Action Planning
  Bachelors TNO_V2.md` (target application October 2026) explicitly
  schedules and tracks the resulting actions, including "Adapt QA policy
  from the Master's programme (done)." Read together, this is a live,
  ex-ante-appropriate example of the shared stakeholder-feedback →
  decision → action mechanism actually operating — the strongest answer in
  this cluster to Sample Questions 4, 9, and 12 (EP objective/concept
  revision procedures).
- **Tech MBA:** previously had no external-stakeholder-involvement
  mechanism documented at all. This has been closed by adapting the Field
  Representative guidelines into a Tech MBA copy
  (`/evidence/tech-mba/Responsibilities and Guidelines for the Field
  Representatives.md`); no dated operating record (equivalent to the
  Bachelor's feedback log) exists yet for the Tech MBA, since it is the
  least advanced of the three programmes in this cycle.

**Recommendation for the narrative presented to the panel:** present the
shared Field Representatives mechanism once, using the **Bachelor's
consolidated feedback record as the illustrative instance** of the
mechanism in action (per Didem's brief), while noting that the Master's and
Tech MBA are covered by the same standing mechanism without yet having their
own equivalent dated feedback log.

### 10. How the policy is implemented, monitored and revised — the institution's own decision

Per the config's ex-ante note, historical revision data and satisfaction-
survey results are correctly not expected pre-launch. What was missing was
an explicit **planned mechanism** for reviewing the QA Policy document
itself (distinct from the general PDCA evaluation of the QA system it
already describes). This has now been added directly to the shared QA
Policy's §1.1.5: the QA Team reviews the QA Policy document itself annually,
with Executive Board sign-off, and stakeholder satisfaction with the policy
specifically will be assessed once programmes are running, via a dedicated
question added to the existing learner-survey/performance-review
instruments already described under §ESG 1.7 — a planned mechanism, not
historical data, appropriately framed for an initial accreditation.

### 11. Competences and decision-making for EP development; EP compliance with EO's regulatory documents

Covered by QA Policy §ESG 1.2 (Design and Approval of Programmes): the
Curriculum Team designs/develops programmes in collaboration with Field
Representatives, and the Executive Board formally approves programmes and
major changes — identical across the cluster. Programme-level regulatory
documents (TER, Assessment Policy, Examination Board Handbook) for each
programme are subordinate to and must comply with this institution-wide QA
Policy, as stated explicitly in each TER's General Provisions ("If the
handbooks conflict with the TER on any point, the TER takes precedence,"
and the TER's own subordination to institutional strategic/educational
policy per the Assessment Policy's "Reach" section).

### 12. Transparency of educational process information to students

Addressed at the programme level through each programme's Educational
Handbook and TER, made available to all learners (QA Policy §ESG 1.3);
Standard 8 (Public Information) will need to develop this further — flagged
for that standard's own evidence-mapping pass rather than resolved fully
here.

### 13. Outsourced/subcontracted activities

**[EVIDENCE NEEDED]:** neither the shared QA Policy nor the programme-level
documents reviewed for this standard identify specific institutional
activities that are subcontracted to or carried out by other parties, or
describe adherence-monitoring for such arrangements. This may genuinely be
a "none at this scale" answer (Sample Question 16), but that should be
confirmed by Didem rather than assumed, since the Guidelines explicitly
requires the QA policy to cover this. **Question for Didem:** does Team
Academy or any of the three programmes currently subcontract any part of
programme delivery, assessment, or admissions to an external party (e.g. a
platform vendor, an outsourced admissions service)? If none, a one-line
statement to that effect closes this item; if yes, it needs the same
"how is adherence monitored" detail the Guidelines asks for.

## Self-assessed position

**Satisfactory** (IAAR term: "average-level indicator, compliance with the
standard") for the cluster as a whole. Reasoning: the shared QA Policy,
Organisational Structure, and Field Representatives mechanisms are strong
and consistent across all three programmes; the Bachelor's stakeholder-
involvement evidence is a genuine strength worth highlighting to the panel
as an example of good practice, though not yet strong enough across the
whole cluster (Master's and Tech MBA still lack an equivalent dated record)
to justify a cluster-wide "Strong" rating. The one substantive, programme-
specific gap this standard's evidence map identified — Tech MBA's missing
TER/Assessment Policy/Examination Board Handbook set — has been closed in
this drafting pass through adaptation, not left open. One residual open
item (subcontracted-activities disclosure, item 13 above) remains outstanding
and should be resolved with Didem before this standard is finalised, but is
minor relative to the standard as a whole.

## Process feedback (for improving Stages 1, 1.5, and this agent, ahead of Standards 2–10)

1. **The evidence map's "resolved" format worked well and should be the
   template going forward.** Having the original question and Didem's
   resolution side by side, per gap, made it possible to act immediately
   without re-litigating or re-asking anything — Stage 2 needed zero
   clarifying questions on the four pre-resolved items. Recommend Stage 1.5
   keep this format for every standard, not just Standard 1.

2. **"Needs adapting" vs "false gap" distinction, while conceptually
   correct, created some duplicate bookkeeping.** The evidence map listed
   the Tech MBA QA Policy adaptation under both "Needs adapting" and "False
   gaps" (same item, two sections). For Standards 2–10, consider merging
   these into a single "Case-2 adaptation tasks" list per standard, since
   in practice Stage 2 treats them identically — the "false gap" framing
   is more about heading off a wrong instinct to ask Didem than it is a
   separate action category.

3. **Document scale is uneven across evidence types, and Stage 1.5 could
   flag this.** The TER/Assessment Policy/Examination Board Handbook
   documents are 1,000+ lines each, dense with WHW-legal detail alongside
   programme-specific curriculum content (assessment tables, module lists).
   Adapting them for Tech MBA required distinguishing "institution-wide
   regulatory framework" (safe to reuse near-verbatim) from "Applied-AI-
   curriculum-specific content" (not transferable) within the same
   document. Stage 1.5 could pre-flag, for any large regulatory document, a
   rough split between "shared/regulatory" and "programme-content-specific"
   sections — this would save Stage 2 a full read-through to make that same
   judgment call for every large document, across 9 more standards where
   these same three documents will likely resurface (Standards 3, 4).

4. **The "no per-programme copy" rule vs. "adapt into a named copy" rule
   sit close together and are easy to conflate.** The cluster-mode
   instructions say a cluster-wide fact gets one appendix item, never one
   per programme — but this standard's evidence map explicitly called for
   *named per-programme copies* of the QA Policy, Org Structure, and Field
   Rep guidelines (because each programme's own TER/Handbook cross-
   references "the QA Policy" as if local). Worth making explicit in the
   `folder-developer` agent instructions: the *SAR narrative* cites a
   shared document once, but the *evidence pool* may still legitimately
   need named per-programme copies when programme-specific regulatory
   documents (TER, Handbook) cross-reference it as if local — these are not
   contradictory, but a first read of the cluster-mode rule suggests they
   might be, and it took deliberate reasoning here to reconcile them.

5. **Ex-ante framing needed active vigilance in a few spots** — e.g. Sample
   Question 6 ("results of assessing stakeholder satisfaction") could
   easily have been answered with a fabricated-sounding "satisfaction has
   been high" instead of correctly describing only the *planned* mechanism.
   The config's ex-ante note is good; recommend Stage 1's checklist keep
   flagging this per standard (as it already did well here) since several
   of Standards 5, 7, and 9's Sample Questions will hit the same trap
   (5-year mobility data, KPI trend data, survey results).

6. **One thing Stage 1.5 could not have caught, and Stage 2 only found by
   reading the actual Tech MBA source documents directly:** the evidence
   map correctly identified Tech MBA's *document* gap (no TER/Assessment
   Policy/Handbook) but the underlying *procedural* facts needed to adapt
   them (Programme Manager's name, the Internal/External Examiner
   mechanism, the four-certification credit structure) were sitting in the
   Tech MBA Supervision Guide and Programme Handbook, not summarized
   anywhere in the evidence map. For Standards 2–10, it would help if
   Stage 1.5 did a lighter per-programme "known facts inventory" (name,
   structure, key mechanisms) for the least-documented programme in the
   cluster (here, Tech MBA), even where a full document doesn't exist yet —
   this is exactly the kind of fact-finding Stage 2 has to redo standard by
   standard otherwise.

7. **Suggest Stage 4 (mock panel) specifically probe the Tech MBA
   adaptations in this draft**, since they are the newest, least field-
   tested evidence in this standard — a mock panel question along the
   lines of Sample Question 9 ("business/scientific community/staff/
   student interaction — mechanisms, examples?") aimed specifically at
   Tech MBA would test whether the adapted Field Representatives copy
   holds up without a dated operating record behind it yet, the way the
   Bachelor's copy has.
