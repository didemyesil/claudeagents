# Responsibilities and Guidelines for the Field Representatives — Bachelor's in Applied Computing

*(Adapted from `/evidence/professional-masters-applied-ai/appendices/Appendix 20 - Responsibilities and Guidelines for the Field Representatives.md`, per the evidence map's judgment — endorsed here — that this document's content is institution-wide in substance rather than genuinely Master's-specific. Team Academy recognises the value and importance of involving external stakeholders from industry, education and other relevant sectors, especially in the planning and development of its programmes, including the Bachelor's in Applied Computing. The Group of Field Representatives creates an essential link between the programmes and industry, in order to achieve a higher level of quality and relevance of the programmes.

## The Purpose

The field representatives serve as an advisory group to the curriculum team in key areas such as programme-market fit, programme-industry fit and continuous academic enhancement of the programmes, including the Bachelor's.

### Role and Responsibilities of the Field Representatives

- Provide recommendations and strategic planning guidance for the university's activities and programmes, including the Bachelor's;
- Make recommendations to ensure that programmes address the critical needs of the employment and industry market;
- Provide perspectives from the employer market, industry, and companies for strategic planning;
- Guide the university in the continuous evaluation of programme objectives and recommend enhancements based on data from learners and university representatives — for the Bachelor's, this role is documented in operation via the Consolidated Field Representative Feedback record (18 verbatim comments on the Programme Brochure and Modular Learning Outcomes, each with a decision/action/status);
- Assist the university in identifying new board members for wider diversity;
- Commit to regular attendance at meetings.

## The Meetings

The curriculum team meets with the group members at the design and annual review stages of the Bachelor's programme. The review meeting aims to validate the Bachelor's-level of the curriculum and final projects, and — as recorded in the Bachelor's Action Plan — this loop has already operated for the Bachelor's programme (target application October 2026), with the QA policy adaptation from the Master's programme marked "done" and the field-representative feedback cycle already producing dated, tracked decisions.

Meetings are moderated by a representative from the institution, minutes are kept, and the discussion translates into decisions and recommendations reviewed at the next meeting.

## The Composition of the Group

The Group represents a diverse mix of business and academic professionals; selection of further members takes into consideration status, gender, race, ethnicity, age and geographic location.

The suggested number of the group is at least 3. Members serve three-year terms with the possibility of renewal. The group is non-voting and does not engage in decision-making related to programme personnel, budget, or internal policy development within the partner institutions.
